### 1. Prompts and Base Models in Multi-Agent Systems

In LLM-based multi-agent systems (MAS), overall performance is primarily determined by **base model capability and prompt design** — not the agent framework itself.

The base model sets the performance ceiling. It defines reasoning ability, domain knowledge, language understanding, and robustness under complex tasks. A weak underlying model will produce poor outputs regardless of how well the multi-agent structure is designed. Stronger models, by contrast, tend to produce more consistent results across multi-step reasoning chains.

Prompts, meanwhile, determine how effectively a model uses its capabilities. In systems like FinRobot, agents such as Expert, Shadow, and UserProxy typically share the same base model but are differentiated through prompt design. Prompts govern role behavior, reasoning style, output structure, and inter-agent interaction patterns. Techniques such as chain-of-thought prompting, structured output requirements, or introducing a critic agent can significantly improve output quality and reduce downstream errors.

In a multi-agent setting, these effects are amplified through interaction. Weak reasoning or poorly designed prompts in one agent can cascade across the system, while thoughtful prompt engineering can enable agents to mutually refine and correct one another. Ultimately, **MAS performance emerges from the interplay between model capability and prompt design within structured interaction** — making these two factors far more consequential than simply scaling the number of agents.

### 2. The Role of MAS in Equity Research

**Equity research** is the process of analyzing publicly traded companies to evaluate their financial performance, business fundamentals, and investment prospects. It typically encompasses financial statement analysis, earnings reports, industry dynamics, macroeconomic context, and company-specific developments.

A multi-agent system like FinRobot can automate and enhance this process by decomposing equity research into specialized, collaborative roles. Rather than relying on a single-pass generation, the system iteratively refines analysis through structured agent interaction — improving reasoning depth, reducing errors, and producing more comprehensive reports. This architecture is particularly well-suited to complex financial tasks that demand multi-step reasoning and critical evaluation.
