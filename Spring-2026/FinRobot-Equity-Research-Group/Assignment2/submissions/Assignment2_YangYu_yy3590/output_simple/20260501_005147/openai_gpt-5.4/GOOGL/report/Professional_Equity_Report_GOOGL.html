Investment Thesis

Alphabet combines strong top-line momentum with expanding scale, growing revenue from $258 billion in 2021 to $403 billion in 2025, with continued growth projected through 2028. Profitability has strengthened meaningfully, with EBITDA rising to $180 billion in 2025 and margins expanding to 44.7%, supported by higher contribution margins and disciplined operating leverage. Company Overview

Alphabet Inc. is a diversified digital platform company whose core business is monetizing user activity across search, video, mobile, maps, and web services, primarily through advertising. Its largest revenue engine is Google’s ad ecosystem, which includes Search, YouTube, and network advertising, while secondary growth drivers include Google Cloud, subscription products, hardware, app store fees, and newer AI-enabled services. Alphabet also invests in longer-duration initiatives through Other Bets, although the core earnings base remains centered on Google Services and Cloud.

The company’s product portfolio is unusually broad and deeply embedded in global internet usage. Google Search remains a foundational gateway for information discovery, YouTube is one of the world’s largest digital video platforms, Android is the leading mobile operating system globally, and Google Maps, Chrome, and Gmail reinforce user engagement across consumer and enterprise touchpoints. In enterprise technology, Google Cloud provides infrastructure, data analytics, cybersecurity, and AI tools, positioning Alphabet as a major competitor to Microsoft and Amazon in cloud computing. This breadth gives Alphabet strong cross-platform data, distribution, and monetization advantages.

Alphabet’s market position remains exceptionally strong, supported by scale, brand, and a massive installed user base. Financially, it compares well with large-cap peers. EBITDA reached $135.4 billion in 2024 and $180.0 billion in 2025, ahead of both Meta and Microsoft in the peer set for those years. At the same time, Alphabet’s 2025 EV/EBITDA multiple of 21.2x sits below Microsoft’s 23.6x, suggesting the market continues to value Alphabet at a discount to the highest-premium software peer despite its strong earnings base, while still above Meta’s 16.4x.

Recent performance has been particularly strong. Revenue increased from $307.4 billion in 2023 to $350.0 billion in 2024 and $403.0 billion in 2025, implying a 2021-2025 CAGR of 11.8%. Profitability also improved sharply: EBITDA margin rose from 31.9% in 2023 to 38.7% in 2024 and 44.7% in 2025, while contribution margin expanded to 59.7%. EPS climbed from $5.84 in 2023 to $8.13 in 2024 and $10.91 in 2025. Looking ahead, revenue growth is projected to moderate to about 5% annually through 2028, but continued margin expansion suggests Alphabet is entering a more efficient, higher-cash-generation phase.

Margin trends strengthen the competitive case. At the same time, SG&A margin declines from 15.0% in 2022 to 12.5% in 2025, indicating improving cost discipline and operating leverage.

On valuation, Alphabet trades between Meta and Microsoft. Its 2025 EV/EBITDA of 21.2x is above Meta’s 16.4x but below Microsoft’s 23.6x. That suggests investors view Alphabet as stronger and more durable than Meta, but still assign Microsoft a premium, likely reflecting its broader enterprise mix and perceived stability.

Overall, Alphabet appears competitively well positioned: larger and more profitable than Meta on this dataset, and increasingly closing or surpassing Microsoft on EBITDA scale. The main watchpoint is growth normalization, with revenue growth projected to slow to 5% from 2026 onward.

Risk Factors

  • ‣ Growth slowdown risk: Revenue growth falls from 15.1% in 2025A to just 5.0% annually in 2026E–2028E. If growth slips below that level, the stock could face downside from weaker expectations.
  • ‣ Margin assumption risk: EBITDA margin is projected to rise from 44.7% in 2025A to 51.7% in 2028E, well above prior historical levels. That requires strong operating leverage; any cost creep could materially hurt earnings.
  • ‣ Valuation compression risk: Alphabet trades at 28.7x 2025A P/E and 21.2x 2025A EV/EBITDA, both above 2024A levels (23.3x P/E, 17.2x EV/EBITDA) despite forecast growth slowing. A rerating lower is a clear risk.
  • ‣ Profit volatility risk: In 2022A, revenue still grew 9.8%, but EBITDA fell from $103.5B to $85.2B and EPS dropped from 5.69 to 4.59. This shows earnings can contract sharply even without a revenue decline.
  • ‣ Peer relative-value risk: Alphabet’s 2025 EV/EBITDA of 21.2x is meaningfully above META’s 16.4x and not far below MSFT’s 23.6x. If investors prefer cheaper or more defensible peer exposure, Alphabet could underperform.

Key Takeaways

Revenue Growth

Alphabet’s revenue has grown strongly, with an 11.8% CAGR from 2021A to 2028E. After slowing to 8.7% in 2023, growth reaccelerated to 13.9% in 2024 and 15.1% in 2025 before normalizing to 5.0% annually in 2026E-2028E.

Gross Profit Margin

Gross profit margin (as implied by contribution margin) improved from 56.9% in 2021A to 59.7% in 2025A. It is projected to keep expanding to 62.7% by 2028E, indicating better cost efficiency and operating leverage.

SG&A Expense Margin

SG&A expense margin peaked at 15.0% in 2022A but improved to 12.5% by 2025A. The margin is expected to decline further to 11.0% by 2028E, showing stronger expense discipline as revenue scales.

EBITDA Margin

EBITDA margin fell sharply to 30.1% in 2022A but recovered to 44.7% in 2025A. It is forecast to reach 51.7% by 2028E, supported by strong EBITDA growth that already puts Alphabet ahead of Meta and Microsoft in absolute EBITDA by 2025A.
