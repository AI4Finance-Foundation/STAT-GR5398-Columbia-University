Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL) competes directly with Apple, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft across digital advertising, cloud computing, and AI-driven services. A review of the financial data reveals both Alphabet's strengths and areas where peers maintain advantages.

On EBITDA, Alphabet has shown the most dramatic improvement among its peers over the past several years. Starting at roughly $103.5 billion in 2021, its EBITDA climbed to approximately $180 billion in 2025, surpassing Apple, Meta, and Microsoft in that year. Amazon leads the group in projected 2026 EBITDA at roughly $230 billion, with Alphabet second at approximately $211 billion, suggesting Amazon's cloud and commerce operations are scaling aggressively.

Alphabet's EBITDA margin trajectory is particularly notable, rising from 40.2% in 2021 to 44.7% in 2025, with estimates projecting further expansion to over 50% by 2027. This margin profile reflects meaningful operating leverage and cost discipline, including a declining SG&A margin that fell from 15% in 2022 to an estimated 11% by 2027.

On valuation, Alphabet's EV/EBITDA multiple of 21.2x in 2025 sits above Amazon's 15.3x and Meta's 16.4x, but below Apple's 27.0x and Microsoft's 23.6x. This positions Alphabet as reasonably valued relative to Apple and Microsoft while commanding a premium over Amazon and Meta.

Revenue growth of 15.1% in 2025 outpaced most peers, though forward estimates moderate to 4 to 6%, which may reflect maturing core search revenues and intensifying competition in cloud and AI from Microsoft and Amazon.
