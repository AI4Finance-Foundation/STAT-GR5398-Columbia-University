Earnings Analysis

Earnings analysis for Apple Inc. is constructive but execution-sensitive. The latest actual period shows a return to positive revenue momentum and resilient EPS, while the forecast assumes meaningful margin expansion through the outer years. For Apple, the earnings debate is less about near-term revenue growth alone and more about whether product refresh cycles, services monetization, pricing/mix discipline, and installed-base retention can sustain operating leverage. Given the valuation premium, investors should focus on gross margin guidance, Services growth, iPhone replacement demand, AI-related product adoption, China exposure, and regulatory pressure.

Key Earnings Read-Through

  • ▪ Apple Inc. generated $416.2B of revenue in 2025A; latest observed revenue growth was 6.4%, providing the starting point for the earnings forecast.
  • ▪ Year-over-year revenue growth was 6.4%, indicating a constructive recovery in top-line momentum.
  • ▪ EPS increased to $7.49 in 2025A and is forecast to reach $8.93 by 2027E; the implied earnings upside depends on sustaining revenue growth while converting margin expansion into per-share profit growth.
  • ▪ EBITDA is forecast to increase from $144.4B in 2025A to $215.8B in 2027E; this makes operating leverage and margin execution the central earnings debate.
  • ▪ EBITDA margin is projected to expand from 34.7% in 2025A to 44.8% in 2027E; the magnitude of this step-up should be treated as an important execution assumption rather than a mechanical extrapolation.
  • ▪ Contribution margin is expected to rise from 46.9% to 49.9%, supporting the earnings growth outlook through pricing discipline, services mix, and cost control.
  • ▪ SG&A margin is expected to move from 6.6% to 5.1%, indicating continued cost discipline.
  • ▪ Relative to peers, AAPL remains a major EBITDA generator, with peer comparison data available through 2026; this supports the quality of the earnings base even if growth is more moderate than some peers.
  • ▪ AAPL trades at approximately 27.0x EV/EBITDA versus a peer average of 20.7x in 2025, implying that earnings delivery must support the valuation premium.
