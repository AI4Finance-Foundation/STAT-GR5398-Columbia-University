Apple Inc. (AAPL) currently trades at a forward EV/EBITDA multiple of 26.6x (2024E) and 27.0x (2025E), which is at the high end of mega-cap tech peers. For comparison, Microsoft trades at 26.1x (2024E) and 23.6x (2025E), while Alphabet and Meta both stand closer to the high teens and low twenties. This premium reflects Apple’s strong brand, robust ecosystem, and consistent operating performance, but also raises the bar for continued execution and growth.

Apple’s EBITDA remains industry leading ($129-145B range through 2025A), and its EBITDA margin is expanding (from 33.7% in 2021 to 34.7% in 2025A, with further step-ups forecasted beyond). Contribution margins are also rising, supported by a growing mix of higher-margin services revenue and disciplined operating cost management.

The company’s forecasted revenue growth (5.0% in 2025E, 6.0% in 2026E, and 4.0% in 2027E) is notably above its historical average (3.4%), but appears reasonably supported by identified drivers: product refresh cycles, services and ecosystem monetization, pricing leverage, and installed-base engagement. SEC filings reinforce this story, highlighting margin discipline, services expansion, and retention as key catalysts, while acknowledging risks from macro conditions, competitive intensity, and supply chain or regulatory headwinds.

In context, Apple’s high multiple is justified by its superior margins, resilience, and ecosystem strength, though it leaves less room for error versus peers. Continued revenue and margin growth will be essential to maintain this fair value premium, especially amid ongoing macro and competitive risks. Overall, growth assumptions are credible given Apple’s track record, but the valuation reflects strong expectations for execution.
