Apple Inc. (AAPL) remains one of the leading technology companies globally, but its recent financial performance and forecasted growth highlight both strengths and challenges relative to its major peers, Alphabet (GOOGL) and Microsoft (MSFT).

Revenue growth for Apple has slowed, with a three-year CAGR of 3.3% from 2021 to 2024, and growth in 2023 was negative (-2.8%). By contrast, both Alphabet and Microsoft are expected to continue outpacing Apple in EBITDA growth, with Alphabet projected to surpass both Apple and Microsoft by 2025 ($179.96B vs. Apple’s $144.43B and Microsoft’s $160.17B). Apple’s EBITDA margin is forecasted to improve significantly, reaching close to 45% by 2027, which would position it strongly on profitability, but peers are also demonstrating expanding margins.

In terms of efficiency, Apple’s SG&A margin remains lower than its peers (declining to 5.1% by 2027), indicating tight cost controls. Contribution margins are also rising, suggesting Apple is extracting more value from each dollar of revenue.

Valuation multiples, however, indicate Apple is currently trading at a premium EV/EBITDA ratio (26.6x in 2024, rising to 27.0x in 2025), higher than Alphabet (17.2x in 2024, 21.2x in 2025) and slightly above Microsoft (26.1x in 2024, 23.6x in 2025). This premium reflects investor confidence in Apple’s brand, ecosystem, and cash generation but may also limit upside if growth continues to lag.

In summary, Apple leads in profitability and efficiency, but with moderating revenue growth and higher valuation multiples, it faces increased competition from peers who are catching up or surpassing in EBITDA growth. Apple’s ability to innovate and diversify revenue streams will be critical to maintaining its premium position in the years ahead.
