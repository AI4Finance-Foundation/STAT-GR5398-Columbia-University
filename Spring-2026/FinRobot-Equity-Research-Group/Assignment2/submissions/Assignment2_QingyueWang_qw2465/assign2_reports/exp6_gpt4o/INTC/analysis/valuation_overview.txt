Valuation Analysis of Intel Corporation (INTC)

Current Valuation Metrics: Intel's financial performance has seen a decline over recent years, with revenue decreasing from $79 billion in 2021 to $53 billion in 2024. The company's EBITDA has also significantly dropped, reflecting in its EBITDA margin, which fell from 42.9% in 2021 to a low of 2.3% in 2024, though it is projected to recover to 30.6% by 2027. The EPS has turned negative, indicating financial challenges. The PE ratio has become negative, showing the market's lack of confidence in Intel's current earnings potential.

Peer Comparison: When compared to peers like AMD and Nvidia, Intel's performance is less robust. AMD has maintained a more stable EBITDA and lower EV/EBITDA ratios, suggesting better market valuation efficiency. Nvidia, despite high EV/EBITDA ratios, indicates strong growth prospects, especially with its substantial increase in EBITDA from 2025 onwards. Intel's EV/EBITDA ratio is currently higher than AMD but lower than Nvidia, reflecting mixed market sentiment relative to its competitors.

Fair Value Assessment: Based on current metrics and peer performance, Intel appears undervalued when considering its historical performance, but this is mitigated by its recent earnings decline and negative outlook. Its recovery trajectory in the coming years, as projected, could justify a higher valuation if executed successfully. However, the current negative EPS and PE ratio, coupled with a high EV/EBITDA, suggest that the market perceives significant risk. For Intel to reach fair value, it needs to demonstrate sustainable revenue growth and improved profitability, aligning closer to peers like AMD in efficiency or leveraging its scale to capture Nvidia-like growth potential.
