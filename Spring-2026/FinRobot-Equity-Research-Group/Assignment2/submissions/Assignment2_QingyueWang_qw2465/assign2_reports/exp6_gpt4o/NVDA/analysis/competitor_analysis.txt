NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) stands out among its peers, AMD and Intel (INTC), primarily due to its robust revenue growth and superior contribution and EBITDA margins. NVIDIA's revenue is projected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 68.3% from 2022 to 2027, significantly outpacing typical industry growth rates. This impressive growth trajectory is largely driven by its strong foothold in the graphics processing unit (GPU) market and its expansion into artificial intelligence (AI) and data centers.

In terms of profitability, NVIDIA's EBITDA margin is projected to increase from 66.9% in 2026 to 73.5% in 2027. This is substantially higher than AMD and Intel, reflecting NVIDIA's efficient cost management and high-margin product offerings. The company's contribution margin also remains robust, indicating effective operational efficiency.

Compared to its peers, NVIDIA's PE ratio shows a declining trend, suggesting that as the company's earnings per share (EPS) grow, the stock may be becoming more attractive in terms of valuation. Although NVIDIA's PE ratio was higher than its peers in earlier years, the decreasing trend towards 2027 suggests better valuation alignment with its earnings growth.

In contrast, AMD and Intel face more modest growth and profitability challenges. AMD shows healthy EBITDA growth but lacks the explosive revenue growth of NVIDIA. Intel, on the other hand, has struggled with declining EBITDA over the years but is projected to recover slightly by 2027.

NVIDIA's EV/EBITDA ratio, although higher than its peers in 2023, is expected to normalize by 2027, reflecting the market's recognition of its sustained growth and profitability prospects. Overall, NVIDIA's strong financial performance and strategic positioning in high-growth sectors such as AI and data centers position it favorably against AMD and Intel in the competitive landscape.
