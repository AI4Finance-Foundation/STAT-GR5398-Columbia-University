Advanced Micro Devices Competitor Analysis

AMD operates in the highly competitive semiconductor industry, where its two primary peers are Intel (INTC) and NVIDIA (NVDA). Each company has carved out distinct market positions, though their trajectories have diverged sharply in recent years.

In terms of revenue growth, AMD has demonstrated consistent momentum, posting a 20.5% CAGR from 2021 to 2025 and reaching $34.6 billion in 2025. This reflects AMD's successful expansion in data center CPUs and GPUs. NVIDIA, however, has emerged as the dominant force, driven by explosive AI accelerator demand that has propelled its EBITDA to $86.1 billion in 2025, dwarfing AMD's $7.3 billion. Intel, by contrast, has struggled significantly, with EBITDA collapsing to $1.2 billion in 2024 before recovering to $14.4 billion in 2025, signaling ongoing operational challenges and market share losses.

On valuation, AMD trades at an EV/EBITDA of approximately 47.8x in 2025, a premium over NVIDIA's 33.8x and well above Intel's 14.5x. This suggests the market prices in strong future growth expectations for AMD, though it also implies execution risk if growth disappoints. NVIDIA's lower multiple despite superior growth may reflect its already massive earnings base.

AMD's EBITDA margins, projected to expand from 21% in 2025 to 42% by 2027, indicate improving operational efficiency. However, AMD still lags NVIDIA in absolute profitability and scale. Intel remains the weakest peer, facing structural headwinds in manufacturing and competitive positioning.

Overall, AMD occupies a credible middle ground, outperforming Intel while competing aggressively but distantly with NVIDIA in the AI-driven semiconductor race.
