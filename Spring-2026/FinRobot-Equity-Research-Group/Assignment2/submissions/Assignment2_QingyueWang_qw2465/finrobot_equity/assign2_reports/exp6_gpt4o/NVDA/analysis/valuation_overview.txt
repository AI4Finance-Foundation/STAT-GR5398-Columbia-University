NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) boasts strong growth metrics, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 68.3% in revenue from 2022 to 2027. Notably, the company's revenue is projected to grow substantially, reaching approximately $250 billion by 2027. The EBITDA margin is also expected to improve from 42.2% in 2022 to 73.5% by 2027, indicating efficient cost management and operational scalability.

In comparison with peers like AMD and Intel, NVIDIA's EBITDA figures are significantly higher, particularly from 2024 onwards, showcasing its leadership in the semiconductor industry. By 2027, NVIDIA's EBITDA is projected to be $382 billion, dwarfing AMD's and Intel's projections. This dominance is reflected in NVIDIA's EBITDA margin, which is set to outperform its peers consistently.

The Price-to-Earnings (PE) ratio for NVIDIA has been decreasing from 62.7 in 2022 to an estimated 32.4 by 2027, suggesting potential undervaluation relative to its growth trajectory. The EV/EBITDA ratio for NVIDIA also declines over time, from 81.0 in 2023 to 31.4 in 2026, indicating a more attractive valuation as earnings grow.

In assessing fair value, NVIDIA's robust growth prospects and improving margins suggest that it is likely undervalued compared to its future earnings potential. The company's market position and technological advancements in AI and data centers further bolster its long-term prospects. However, potential investors should consider the volatility in tech valuations and the competitive landscape when assessing its fair value. Overall, NVIDIA appears to be positioned favorably for sustained growth, making it a compelling option for growth-focused investors.
