NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) Investment Update

NVIDIA has delivered one of the most remarkable financial transformations in semiconductor history, with revenue surging from $26.9 billion in fiscal 2022 to $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026, representing a five-year CAGR of 68.3%. This explosive growth has been driven almost entirely by insatiable demand for AI accelerators, with data center customers racing to build out large-scale GPU infrastructure. The company's ability to translate top-line momentum into profitability has been equally impressive, with EBITDA margins expanding from 22.2% in fiscal 2023 to 66.9% in fiscal 2026, reflecting extraordinary operating leverage as fixed costs are spread across a rapidly growing revenue base.

The primary growth driver remains AI infrastructure buildout across hyperscalers, enterprise customers, and sovereign AI programs globally. NVIDIA's CUDA software ecosystem continues to serve as a powerful competitive moat, creating high switching costs that reinforce hardware dominance. EPS has grown from $0.18 in fiscal 2023 to $4.93 in fiscal 2026, with estimates projecting further expansion to $5.88 by fiscal 2027.

Relative to peers, NVIDIA's EBITDA trajectory is in a different category entirely. AMD and Intel combined cannot approach NVIDIA's absolute earnings scale, and NVIDIA's EV/EBITDA multiple has compressed meaningfully from 81x in fiscal 2023 to approximately 31x in fiscal 2026, suggesting the market is beginning to price in a more mature growth profile.

The key risks to monitor include revenue growth deceleration, which estimates project slowing to 4-6% annually through fiscal 2027, potential export restrictions, and emerging competition from custom silicon developed by hyperscaler customers. At current valuations, execution must remain flawless.
