Meta Platforms delivered a strong Q1 2026 earnings report, beating consensus estimates on both revenue and earnings per share. The company reported revenue of $56.31 billion, reflecting 33% year-over-year growth, while adjusted EPS came in at $7.31, surpassing the Zacks consensus estimate of $6.71 and well above the prior year figure of $6.43. Despite these results, Meta shares declined approximately 5 to 7 percent in post-earnings trading, reflecting investor concern over rising capital expenditure guidance.

The primary source of market anxiety was Meta's decision to raise its full-year capex outlook to a range of $125 to $145 billion, driven by aggressive artificial intelligence infrastructure investment. This reignited broader fears about near-term margin compression and the sustainability of returns on AI spending. Additionally, Meta flagged legal and regulatory risks in both the European Union and the United States, particularly around youth social media usage, as potential headwinds to future financial performance.

From a financial trajectory standpoint, the company's contribution margin has steadily improved from 80.8 percent in 2021 to 82 percent in 2025, with forward estimates projecting further expansion to 85 percent by 2027. EBITDA is also expected to grow significantly in forward estimates, though the gap between reported and estimated EBITDA figures warrants scrutiny.

Retail sentiment is broadly elevated with a buzz score of 82.8 out of 100, though there is notable divergence across platforms. Reddit leans bearish at 29 percent bullish, while X.com shows stronger conviction at 60 percent bullish. For long-term investors, the post-earnings dip may represent an attractive entry point given strong underlying fundamentals, though near-term volatility tied to AI spending and regulatory exposure remains a legitimate risk.
