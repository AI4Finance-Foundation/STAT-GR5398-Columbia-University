Tesla Inc. continues to demonstrate strong top-line growth relative to the broader automotive sector, with a 15.2% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) in revenue from 2021 to 2027E. This rate outpaces most legacy automakers, which typically post low single-digit growth, and positions Tesla ahead of emerging electric vehicle (EV) peers in terms of scale. However, revenue growth is expected to slow significantly post-2023, with 2024 and 2025E showing minimal or negative growth (0.9% and -2.9%, respectively) before rebounding modestly, indicating potential market saturation or increased competition.

Tesla's contribution margin, once a clear strength at over 25% in 2021-2022, has compressed to the high teens (18.2% in 2023, 17.9% in 2024A), reflecting pricing pressures and higher costs—a trend also visible in legacy automakers during industry downcycles. This margin is still generally superior to traditional automakers but is narrowing, suggesting competitive pressure from both established OEMs and newer EV entrants. The projected recovery to a 21% margin by 2027E, if achieved, would help Tesla maintain its profitability edge.

EBITDA margins follow a similar trajectory, declining from 21.7% in 2022A to a trough of 12.4% in 2025A, before a gradual recovery. This dip signals operational headwinds, possibly from higher input costs, R&D investment, or increased competition. Still, Tesla’s EBITDA margins remain above most legacy auto peers, who often operate in the high single digits, but now approach those of other high-growth EV companies.

Tesla maintains cost discipline in SG&A, with margins around 5-6%, which is leaner than many peers, supporting its operating leverage. However, the sharp drop in EPS from 2023A (4.73) to 2025A/E (1.18/1.25) highlights near-term profitability challenges.

In summary, while Tesla’s growth, margins, and cost structure remain strong compared to legacy and new EV competitors, recent trends indicate increased competitive and cost pressures. Maintaining its historical profitability leadership will require overcoming these challenges as the EV market matures and competition intensifies.
