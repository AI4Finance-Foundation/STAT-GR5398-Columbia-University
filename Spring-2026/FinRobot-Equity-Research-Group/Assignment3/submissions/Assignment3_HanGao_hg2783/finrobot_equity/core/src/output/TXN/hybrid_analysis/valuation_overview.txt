Texas Instruments Incorporated (TXN) currently exhibits a mixed valuation profile shaped by recent revenue headwinds and an improving growth outlook. Recent actuals reveal a revenue decline from $20.0B in 2022 to $15.6B in 2024, reflecting macroeconomic pressures and cyclical industry softness. However, the forecast anticipates a rebound, with revenues projected to grow from $17.7B in 2025 to $20.5B by 2027, representing a 5-6% annual growth rate in the medium term.

Margin trends show some compression: EBITDA margin declined from 56.0% in 2022 to 48.2% in 2024, though stabilization is expected, with margins forecast to recover toward 51.0% by 2027. Contribution margin follows a similar pattern, dipping to 58.1% in 2024 but expected to recover gradually. EPS is projected to rise from $5.24 in 2024 to $6.50 in 2027, consistent with margin restoration and top-line recovery.

Compared to peers in the analog semiconductor sector, TXN’s margins remain robust, and the forecasted return to growth aligns with product-cycle recoveries observed in the industry. The revenue CAGR of -0.9% over the period reflects recent weakness but masks a forward inflection. While the historical average growth is flat, the forecasted growth assumptions (4-6% per year) are supported by explicit revenue drivers: new product launches, segment expansion, and innovation, as highlighted in SEC filings. Management’s focus on operating discipline and margin control further underpins fair value assumptions.

Risks remain, particularly macroeconomic uncertainty and supply chain vulnerabilities, but the identified catalysts—demand resilience, innovation, and expansion—provide credible support for the forecast. In sum, TXN’s valuation appears reasonable in the context of sector normalization, with forecasted growth and margin improvement supported by both company-specific drivers and management commentary.
