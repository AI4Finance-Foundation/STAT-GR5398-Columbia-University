Intel Corporation’s recent valuation metrics reflect a company in transition. After significant revenue declines from $79.0 billion in 2021 to $54.2 billion in 2023 (a CAGR of -9.6%), revenue appears to have stabilized, with only a slight contraction in 2024 (-2.1%) and near-flat performance projected in 2025 (-0.5%). The forecast then assumes a return to growth: 5.0% in 2025E, 6.0% in 2026E, and 4.0% in 2027E, driven by product launches, segment expansion, and innovation. However, these positive projections follow a period of notable contraction and margin compression.

Profitability metrics have similarly declined. EBITDA margin dropped from 42.9% in 2021 to just 2.3% in 2024, with a projected recovery to 27.6% in 2025E and further improvement through 2027E. EPS has deteriorated sharply, turning negative in 2024 and remaining negative through the forecast, which precludes meaningful PE ratio comparison for now.

Relative to peers, Intel’s recent revenue and margin trajectory has underperformed, especially as competitors in AI, cloud, and specialized semiconductors have generally maintained growth and profitability. This context places Intel at a valuation discount, but also highlights potential upside if execution improves.

The SEC filings emphasize that revenue growth assumptions in the forecast are tied to successful product launches, strategic expansion, and margin discipline. Management calls out these drivers as central to their outlook, while also warning of macroeconomic, competitive, supply chain, and regulatory risks. Given the recent stabilization in revenue and the explicit focus on new products and operational discipline, the forecasted revenue growth is plausible, but dependent on delivery against these strategic priorities. In summary, Intel’s valuation reflects a turnaround narrative: fair value is conditional on credible execution of product and margin initiatives, with risks skewed to the downside if macro or competitive headwinds intensify.
