Company Overview

Broadcom Inc. (AVGO) is a leading global technology company that designs, develops, and supplies a broad range of semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions. The company operates a diversified business model with two primary segments: semiconductor solutions and infrastructure software. Its semiconductor products include custom and standard integrated circuits for data center networking, broadband, wireless communications, enterprise storage, and industrial markets. Broadcom’s infrastructure software portfolio covers mainframe, cybersecurity, enterprise, and storage area networking solutions, providing mission-critical software for large enterprises and service providers.

Broadcom has established itself as a key player in the semiconductor industry, consistently ranking among the top providers of networking, storage, and wireless connectivity chips. Its business model is characterized by a mix of organic innovation and strategic acquisitions, which have expanded its product suite and customer base across various end-markets. The company’s focus on high-value, mission-critical components and software ensures strong customer loyalty and pricing power.

In terms of market position, Broadcom competes with major players such as Qualcomm (QCOM), Texas Instruments (TXN), and Marvell Technology (MRVL). Broadcom’s EBITDA performance surpasses most of its peers, with 2023 EBITDA of $20.6 billion—more than double that of Texas Instruments and well ahead of Qualcomm. This superior profitability is also reflected in its EBITDA margins, which remained robust, averaging over 55% from 2021 to 2023, despite a temporary dip in 2024 due to integration costs and higher operating expenses.

Financially, Broadcom has demonstrated impressive growth. From 2021 to 2023, revenue grew at a compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) of nearly 20%, accelerating to 44% in 2024, driven by both organic growth and the impact of recent acquisitions. Contribution margin and EBITDA margin have stayed strong, with the company’s 2023 contribution margin at 68.9% and EBITDA margin at 57.4%. Earnings per share (EPS) have increased steadily, except for a temporary dip in 2024 due to non-recurring charges, but are projected to rebound strongly in 2025 and beyond.

Looking forward, Broadcom is well-positioned for continued growth, anchored by its leadership in high-growth markets such as cloud infrastructure, AI, and enterprise software. Forecasts suggest revenue will surpass $67 billion by 2025, with margins expected to expand further, underscoring Broadcom’s resilience, operational discipline, and strong competitive positioning in a rapidly evolving technology landscape.

Investment Update

Broadcom Inc. (AVGO) delivered robust financial performance over the past three years, with revenue rising from $27.5 billion in 2021 to $51.6 billion in 2024, reflecting a strong compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 23.5%. This growth accelerated sharply in 2024 with a 44% year-over-year increase, driven primarily by the VMware acquisition and continued strength in networking and semiconductor solutions. Contribution margin held steady above 60%, and is projected to reach nearly 71% by 2027, indicating sustained operating leverage and scale advantages.

EBITDA grew from $14.7 billion in 2021 to $23.9 billion in 2024 and is estimated to climb to $48.6 billion by 2027, outpacing peers such as Qualcomm (QCOM), Texas Instruments (TXN), and Marvell (MRVL). Broadcom’s EBITDA margins are forecast to expand from 46.3% in 2024 to 65.7% by 2027, highlighting improving profitability despite short-term dilution from the VMware integration.

EPS dipped in 2024 due to acquisition-related costs but is projected to recover strongly, reaching $5.86 by 2027. The company’s EV/EBITDA multiple has risen to 35x in 2024 and could increase further as the market prices in future earnings growth and synergy realization. While this premium reflects high expectations, Broadcom’s scale, diversified product portfolio, and strong cash generation underpin its valuation.

Looking ahead, AVGO’s key growth drivers include AI infrastructure demand, continued networking expansion, and cross-selling opportunities post-VMware. Risks remain around integration execution and elevated valuation, but the outlook is positive as Broadcom consolidates its leadership in the global semiconductor and infrastructure software markets.

Valuation

Broadcom Inc. (AVGO) currently trades at premium valuation multiples, reflecting both its robust growth outlook and leading profitability. The company’s 2024E EV/EBITDA is 35.1x, rising sharply to 50.6x on 2025E, compared to peers like Qualcomm (QCOM) at 15.5x (2024E) and 12.7x (2025E), and Texas Instruments (TXN) at 24.2x (2024E) and 20.6x (2025E). Marvell (MRVL), while less profitable, trades at even higher EV/EBITDA multiples, but with much smaller EBITDA.

AVGO’s forward PE ratio is also elevated—2024E PE at 132.2x falls back to 73.9x in 2025E, but remains well above industry norms. This reflects a temporary dip in 2024 EPS (from $3.39 in 2023 to $1.27 in 2024 due to one-time charges or dilution), before rebounding strongly to $4.91 in 2025E and continuing steady growth.

Operationally, Broadcom’s EBITDA and contribution margins are industry-leading (2023 EBITDA margin: 57.4%, vs. QCOM at 36.3%, TXN at 31.4%), and revenue is forecast to grow at a 23.5% CAGR through 2027—well above most large semiconductor peers. This is largely driven by the VMware acquisition and continued strength in networking and infrastructure, supporting the market’s willingness to pay a premium.

In peer comparison, Broadcom’s size, growth, and margins justify some premium, but current multiples—especially on 2024–2025 figures—are stretched. On a normalized 2025E EV/EBITDA (~50x), AVGO trades at a significant premium to QCOM and TXN (the latter averaging ~16x–21x). Even with superior growth, a fair value range might be closer to 25–30x EV/EBITDA, implying the stock is trading ahead of fundamentals and may be priced for perfection. Investors should be cautious of execution risks and integration challenges, as any disappointment could trigger a valuation reset.

  • ▪ Aggressive Growth Assumptions: Revenue and EBITDA projections assume high double-digit CAGRs (e.g., 23.5% revenue CAGR), and any macroeconomic slowdown, tech cycle downturn, or integration challenges could result in significant forecast misses.
