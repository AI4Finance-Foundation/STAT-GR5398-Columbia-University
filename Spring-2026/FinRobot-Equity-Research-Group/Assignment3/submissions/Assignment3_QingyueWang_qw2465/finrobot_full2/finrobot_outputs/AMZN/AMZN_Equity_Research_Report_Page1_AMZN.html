Company Overview

Amazon.com, Inc. is a global leader in e-commerce, cloud computing, and digital services, with a diversified business model that spans retail, technology infrastructure, and digital media. Founded in 1994, Amazon has evolved from an online bookstore into one of the world’s largest and most influential companies, serving hundreds of millions of customers worldwide.

Amazon’s business model is built on three primary pillars: its online retail marketplace, Amazon Web Services (AWS), and a growing portfolio of subscription and advertising services. The company’s e-commerce platform offers a vast selection of goods, leveraging third-party sellers alongside its own inventory, supported by a world-class logistics and fulfillment network. AWS, Amazon’s cloud computing arm, provides infrastructure-as-a-service (IaaS), platform-as-a-service (PaaS), and a variety of other cloud-based solutions to businesses, governments, and developers globally. In addition, Amazon has established a strong presence in digital content (Prime Video, Kindle, Audible), smart devices (Echo, Alexa), and increasingly, advertising, which is becoming a key profit driver.

Amazon holds a dominant position in both global e-commerce and cloud computing markets. It is the world’s largest online retailer and commands a significant share of the cloud infrastructure market, often competing with Microsoft and Google. Compared to peers, Amazon’s EBITDA has shown robust growth, surpassing Walmart and closely tracking with Microsoft and Google in recent years.

Financially, Amazon demonstrated consistent revenue growth, achieving a CAGR of 11.1% from 2021 to 2027E, with annual revenues projected to reach over $800 billion by 2027. The company’s contribution margin has steadily improved, rising from 42.0% in 2021 to a projected 53.3% in 2027, reflecting operational efficiencies and the higher-margin profile of AWS and advertising. After a challenging 2022, marked by a rare negative EPS due to macroeconomic headwinds and investment cycles, Amazon rebounded in 2023 with strong profitability and continues to expand its EBITDA margin, expected to rise from 15.6% in 2023 to 46.7% by 2027E. The company’s valuation, as measured by EV/EBITDA, remains competitive within its peer set.

In summary, Amazon’s diversified business model, scale, and ongoing innovation position it as a market leader with strong growth prospects and improving profitability, underpinned by its expansion in high-margin, technology-driven segments.

Investment Update

Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN) delivered robust financial performance in 2023, with revenue growing 11.8% year-over-year to $575 billion. This momentum is expected to continue, with consensus forecasts projecting an 11.1% revenue CAGR through 2027. Amazon’s contribution margin expanded steadily from 42.0% in 2021 to 47.0% in 2023, reflecting improved operational efficiency and a growing mix of higher-margin businesses such as AWS and advertising.

EBITDA rebounded sharply in 2023 to $89.4 billion, more than doubling from 2022’s $38.4 billion, as the company recovered from inflationary pressures and cost headwinds. EBITDA margin also improved to 15.6% in 2023 and is projected to rise to 23.1% in 2025 and nearly 47% by 2027, signaling significant profitability gains. Compared to peers, Amazon’s 2023 EBITDA was second only to Microsoft and Google, and its margin improvement trajectory stands out in the sector.

Key growth drivers include continued expansion of AWS, which benefits from secular demand for cloud computing, and increasing advertising revenues. Operational cost control and declining SG&A margins are also contributing to improved earnings leverage. EPS recovered to $2.95 in 2023 and is expected to grow to $8.69 by 2027.

Valuation appears reasonable relative to large-cap tech peers, with a forward PE ratio declining from 51x in 2023 to 27x by 2027 and EV/EBITDA projected to fall to 15.3x in 2025. Overall, Amazon’s growth outlook remains compelling, underpinned by strong market positioning, margin expansion, and ongoing innovation. Continued execution in AWS and advertising, along with operational discipline, should support further upside for long-term investors.

Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN) continues to demonstrate robust growth and strategic transformation, drawing widespread investor optimism and analyst confidence. The company recently reported its fifth consecutive quarter of exceeding Wall Street’s EPS expectations, with shares climbing over 35% since March and consistently trading above $270. Wall Street sentiment is notably bullish: 60 out of 65 analysts rate Amazon a Buy, with no Sell recommendations, and a long-term price target of $374.25.

Key financial metrics reveal Amazon’s accelerating momentum. Revenue has grown at an 11.1% CAGR from 2021 to 2027E, with projections reaching nearly $830 billion by 2027. EPS is expected to rise steadily, and the trailing P/E ratio, currently around 33x, remains attractive given Amazon’s growth trajectory and earnings consistency.

Retail investor sentiment remains broadly positive, though social buzz is trending downward on Reddit and X.com despite rising activity on prediction markets. Overall, Amazon’s expanding margin profile, dominant cloud positioning, and relentless innovation underpin its strong buy thesis, making it a compelling long-term investment amid ongoing sector disruptions and technological advances.

Valuation

Valuation Overview: Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN)

Amazon’s current valuation profile reflects strong expected growth, but also a premium relative to peers. As of 2024, Amazon trades at 18.98x forward EV/EBITDA, above Walmart (13.64x) and Google (17.24x), but below Microsoft (26.1x). On a P/E basis, Amazon’s 2024E multiple is 38.8x, and is projected to decline to 31.7x (2025E) and 27.2x (2027E), reflecting rapid earnings growth (2023-2027 EPS CAGR >30%).

From a profitability perspective, Amazon’s EBITDA margin is expanding sharply, projected to reach 23.1% in 2025 and above 40% by 2026-2027. This is converging with, and even exceeding, the margins of peers like Google and Microsoft. Amazon’s EBITDA is expected to surpass $230 billion by 2027, outpacing Microsoft and Google in absolute dollar terms.

Relative to its peer set, Amazon is currently more expensive than Walmart and Google on an EV/EBITDA basis but trades at a discount to Microsoft. The premium to Walmart is justified by Amazon’s much higher growth rate (revenue CAGR 11% vs. low single digits for Walmart) and margin expansion. Compared to Google, Amazon’s higher multiple is supported by its superior scalability and growth in both e-commerce and cloud.

Fair Value Assessment: While Amazon’s shares trade at a premium to most peers, this is justified by its accelerating margin profile and robust top-line growth. With EBITDA margins set to expand and earnings multiples declining as profitability grows, Amazon’s current valuation appears fair, if not modestly attractive on a long-term basis. Upside remains supported by continued operating leverage and leadership in high-growth sectors. Investors should expect modest multiple compression as Amazon matures, but ongoing EPS growth supports a positive long-term fair value outlook.

Risks

Key Investment Risks for Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN):

  • ▪ Margin Volatility and Forecast Uncertainty: Significant EBITDA margin swings (e.g., 15.8% in 2021, down to 7.5% in 2022, then up to 43.7% forecasted for 2025E) suggest uncertainty in cost control, operational efficiency, or changes in business mix, making future profitability unpredictable.
  • ▪ Aggressive Forecast Assumptions: Projected EBITDA and margins for 2025–2027 (e.g., EBITDA margin rising to 46.7% in 2027E) are substantially higher than historical levels and peers, raising concerns about the realism of forecasts and potential for investor disappointment if not achieved.
  • ▪ Competitive Pressure: Peer metrics show that Amazon’s EV/EBITDA multiples are in line with or slightly higher than competitors (e.g., 18.98x in 2024 vs. GOOGL at 17.24x and MSFT at 26.11x), while EBITDA size and growth are closely matched, indicating persistent competitive threats from major tech and retail firms.
  • ▪ Earnings Volatility: EPS turned negative in 2022 (-$0.27), reflecting exposure to earnings shocks from operational, macroeconomic, or regulatory factors, and highlighting potential for future bottom-line volatility.
  • ▪ Valuation Risk: Despite volatile earnings and operational results, Amazon’s PE and EV/EBITDA multiples remain elevated (e.g., 31.7x PE in 2025, 18.98x EV/EBITDA in 2024), exposing investors to downside risk if growth expectations are not met or if market multiples compress.
