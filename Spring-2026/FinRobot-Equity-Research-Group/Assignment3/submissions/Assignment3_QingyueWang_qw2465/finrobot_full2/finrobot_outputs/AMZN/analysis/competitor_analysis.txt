Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN) stands out among its peers—Google (Alphabet), Microsoft, and Walmart—due to its robust revenue growth and improving profitability metrics. Over 2021-2027, Amazon is expected to deliver a revenue CAGR of 11.1%, outpacing the more mature growth rates of retail peer Walmart and aligning closely with digital leaders like Google and Microsoft.

Amazon’s contribution margin has consistently improved, rising from 42.0% in 2021 to a projected 53.3% by 2027, signaling the company’s ability to scale efficiently. EBITDA margin, after a dip in 2022 (7.5%), rebounds sharply, with forecasts reaching 46.7% by 2027—on par with tech giants and far ahead of traditional retailers like Walmart.

In absolute EBITDA, Amazon is converging with Google and Microsoft. By 2027, Amazon’s EBITDA ($230.1B) is projected to surpass Microsoft ($188.0B) and approach Google ($210.7B), while dramatically outpacing Walmart ($51.5B). This suggests Amazon is bridging the gap between platform-based tech and retail scale.

From a valuation perspective, Amazon’s EV/EBITDA has trended downward, from 23.8x (2021) to a projected 15.3x (2025), becoming more attractive relative to historical levels. This is competitive with Google and Microsoft, and notably higher than Walmart, reflecting Amazon’s stronger future growth and profitability prospects.

Amazon’s PE ratio remains elevated (27.2x in 2027), reflecting ongoing market expectations for superior growth and reinvestment. Despite a brief negative EPS in 2022, Amazon’s earnings are expected to rebound and grow, supporting its premium multiple.

In summary, Amazon is positioned as a unique hybrid—leveraging platform efficiencies and scale to deliver high growth and elevated profitability, with metrics increasingly comparable to leading tech peers and well ahead of traditional retail competitors.
