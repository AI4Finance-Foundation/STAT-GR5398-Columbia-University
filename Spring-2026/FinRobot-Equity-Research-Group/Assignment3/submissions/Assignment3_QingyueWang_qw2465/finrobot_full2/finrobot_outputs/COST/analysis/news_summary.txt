Costco Wholesale Corporation (COST) continues to outperform its big-box retail peers, driven by robust financial growth and strong consumer demand. Recent news highlights that Costco stores are notably busier, with foot traffic up over 18% in the past seven years—far surpassing the flat trends at competitors Walmart and Target. This surge demonstrates Costco’s enduring appeal and efficient club model, which fosters customer loyalty and steady membership growth (membership fees up 13.6%).

Financially, Costco remains in a strong position. Revenue has grown at an 8.9% CAGR since 2021, and is projected to reach nearly $319 billion by 2027. Contribution margin and EBITDA margin are both on an improving trajectory: contribution margin is expected to climb from 12.9% in 2021 to 15.8% in 2027, while EBITDA margin could reach 8.2%. These improvements are underpinned by Costco’s disciplined cost management and scale advantages.

Relative to peers, Costco’s EBITDA is rising faster than BJ’s and Target, though Walmart remains the market leader by size. However, Costco’s EV/EBITDA multiple is notably higher (32.5x in 2024 vs. Walmart’s 13.6x), reflecting a market premium for its growth, resilience, and cash flow profile.

Costco’s balance sheet is robust, with $18.2 billion in liquid assets and low debt, enabling aggressive expansion (28 new warehouses planned) and ongoing dividend growth. While retail sector sentiment is mixed—online buzz and bullishness are trending down—Costco’s fundamentals and operational momentum position it as a top pick in consumer staples.

Investment implications: While Costco trades at a premium valuation, its combination of financial strength, consistent store traffic growth, and prudent expansion justifies ongoing investor confidence. Long-term investors may find Costco attractive for its growth, defensiveness, and reliable shareholder returns, though valuation risks should be monitored.
