Company Overview

Intel Corporation (NASDAQ: INTC) is a leading global semiconductor company specializing in the design and manufacturing of advanced integrated digital technology platforms. Intel’s core business model is vertically integrated: it designs, manufactures, and sells microprocessors and related hardware for computing devices, supplying both original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and cloud service providers. The company generates revenue mainly from its Client Computing Group (CCG), Data Center and AI Group (DCAI), and emerging businesses such as foundry services and automotive solutions.

Intel’s primary products include x86-compatible central processing units (CPUs) for personal computers and servers, chipsets, graphics processing units (GPUs), field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs), and various system-on-chip (SoC) solutions. The company is also investing heavily in foundry services, aiming to become a key third-party chip manufacturer for global technology firms, and expanding into AI accelerators, autonomous vehicle technology, and edge computing.

Historically, Intel has maintained a dominant position in the CPU market, especially for PCs and data centers, facing competition from Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and, increasingly, Nvidia in the AI and data center segments. However, the last three years have been challenging. Revenue declined sharply from $79 billion in 2021 to $54.2 billion in 2023, reflecting a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of -9.6%. This contraction is attributed to macroeconomic headwinds, declining PC demand post-pandemic, increased competitive pressure, and execution challenges in technology transitions.

Profitability has also come under pressure. Contribution margin dropped from 55.4% in 2021 to 40.0% in 2023, while the EBITDA margin fell from 42.9% to 20.7% over the same period. Intel’s 2024 guidance and forward estimates suggest stabilization, with revenues expected to bottom out before a modest recovery (~5-6% annual growth) from 2025 onwards, and EBITDA margins gradually rebounding above 27%. Nevertheless, Intel’s earnings per share (EPS) turned negative in 2024 and are expected to remain in the red through at least 2027, reflecting substantial investment and ongoing margin pressure.

Compared to peers, Intel’s recent operational metrics and valuation multiples (e.g., EV/EBITDA) have deteriorated, particularly as Nvidia accelerates in AI-driven markets. In summary, Intel remains a critical player in global semiconductors, but is navigating a period of transition, seeking to regain competitiveness and profitability through aggressive investment in manufacturing, foundry, and new growth verticals.
