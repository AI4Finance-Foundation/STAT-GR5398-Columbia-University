Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) has demonstrated robust growth and resilience in a fiercely competitive semiconductor landscape dominated by Intel (INTC) and NVIDIA (NVDA). Over the 2021-2027 period, AMD’s revenue is projected to grow at a strong 20.5% CAGR, outpacing Intel’s recent stagnation and aligning more closely with NVIDIA’s high-growth trajectory, especially as demand for AI and high-performance computing accelerates.

AMD’s contribution margin is forecasted to improve steadily, reaching 52.5% by 2027, reflecting efficiencies in operations and a focus on higher-margin products. Comparatively, AMD’s EBITDA margin remains below NVIDIA’s but is set to expand significantly from 18.3% in 2023 to 42.0% in 2027, narrowing the profitability gap as AMD scales its data center and AI offerings. Still, NVIDIA’s explosive growth, especially from 2024 onward, puts it in a league of its own, with EBITDA surging to $382B by 2027, dwarfing both AMD and Intel.

In contrast, Intel’s EBITDA collapsed sharply post-2023, highlighting its struggle with execution and competitive pressure, though a partial rebound is expected by 2027. AMD’s EBITDA, while smaller than Intel’s at scale, is forecasted to grow more consistently, indicating operational stability and improving profitability.

Valuation-wise, AMD trades at high EV/EBITDA multiples (38-58x), reflecting investor optimism about future growth but also highlighting rich expectations relative to Intel (generally lower multiples) and NVIDIA (recently trading at a premium, but moderating as growth normalizes).

In summary, AMD has successfully positioned itself as a dynamic challenger: executing on growth, improving margins, and gaining share in both CPUs and GPUs. However, it remains caught between an entrenched but struggling Intel and an ascendant, high-multiple NVIDIA, requiring continued innovation and flawless execution to maintain momentum.
