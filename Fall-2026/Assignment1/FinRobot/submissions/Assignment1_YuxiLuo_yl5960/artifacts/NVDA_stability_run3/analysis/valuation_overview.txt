NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) currently trades at stretched valuation multiples, reflecting its dominant position in AI-driven semiconductor demand and extraordinary growth rates. As of 2024, NVDA’s PE ratio stands at 51.6x, declining to 39.9x in 2025E and 35.9x in 2026E. This compares to the S&P 500 multiple (~22x 2024E) and remains well above peers such as AMD (2024E EV/EBITDA: 38.3x) and Intel (2024E: 106.9x, though distorted by depressed earnings). NVDA’s EV/EBITDA, a key capital structure-neutral measure, is forecast at 43.5x in 2024E, moderating to 33.8x in 2025E and 31.4x in 2026E—still a premium to AMD (2025E: 47.8x) but with much stronger underlying growth.

NVDA’s revenue CAGR (2024-2027E) is an exceptional 68.3%, with EBITDA margins expanding from 58.4% (2024) to over 70% by 2027E. This far outpaces AMD and Intel, both in growth and profitability. Peer comparison highlights NVDA’s superior scale: 2025E EBITDA is projected at $86.1 billion, versus $7.3 billion for AMD and $14.4 billion for Intel.

Despite lofty absolute multiples, NVDA’s valuation is underpinned by unprecedented growth and margin expansion due to generative AI and datacenter demand. However, current multiples embed high expectations for sustained leadership. A fair value assessment depends on whether NVDA can maintain its technological lead and growth trajectory. If growth slows or competitors catch up, downside risk exists given the premium. At a justified PEG ratio (PE-to-growth) near 1, fair value could be 30-35x forward earnings, suggesting NVDA is near the upper end of fair value, but not in bubble territory given current sector dynamics. Investors should monitor growth durability and competitive threats, as any deceleration could pressure multiples.
