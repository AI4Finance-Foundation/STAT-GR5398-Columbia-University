NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) is a global leader in graphics processing technology and artificial intelligence (AI) computing. Founded in 1993 and headquartered in Santa Clara, California, NVIDIA has transformed from a pioneer in graphics processing units (GPUs) for gaming to the dominant force in data center acceleration and AI infrastructure.

Business Model:
NVIDIA’s business model revolves around designing advanced semiconductor chips and software platforms that power high-performance computing across multiple industries. The company primarily generates revenue by licensing and selling its proprietary GPUs, systems, and software to data centers, gaming, automotive, and professional visualization markets. NVIDIA’s ecosystem approach—offering both hardware and a comprehensive suite of software development tools—creates a high barrier to entry and fosters long-term customer relationships.

Products and Services:
NVIDIA’s flagship products include the GeForce line of gaming GPUs, the NVIDIA RTX platform for real-time ray tracing, and the powerful data center GPU families such as the A100 and H100. Its CUDA programming platform enables developers to harness GPU acceleration for a range of applications, from deep learning to scientific computing. The company also offers networking products (via Mellanox acquisition), AI software stacks, and emerging automotive technologies, including autonomous driving solutions under the NVIDIA DRIVE platform.

Market Position:
NVIDIA holds a dominant share in both discrete GPU markets for gaming and in data center accelerators—critical for AI model training and inferencing. The company’s leadership in AI hardware and software has positioned it as the primary technology provider for hyperscale cloud providers, enterprise AI deployments, and academic research. Competition comes from peers like AMD and Intel, but NVIDIA’s technological edge and software ecosystem are key differentiators.

Recent Performance:
NVIDIA’s financials reflect explosive growth driven by surging AI demand. Revenues leaped from $26.9 billion in FY2023 to $60.9 billion in FY2024, and are projected to more than double to $130.5 billion in FY2025. EBITDA margins have expanded sharply, reaching 58.4% in FY2024 and projected to exceed 70% by FY2027, indicating strong operating leverage. EPS has grown from $0.18 in 2023 to $1.21 in 2024, with a forecast of $5.88 by 2027. NVIDIA’s EBITDA far outpaces peers AMD and Intel, and its premium valuation is reflected in higher EV/EBITDA multiples.

In summary, NVIDIA is at the forefront of the AI and accelerated computing revolution, enjoying robust growth, superior profitability, and an entrenched market position that is likely to persist as AI adoption accelerates globally.
