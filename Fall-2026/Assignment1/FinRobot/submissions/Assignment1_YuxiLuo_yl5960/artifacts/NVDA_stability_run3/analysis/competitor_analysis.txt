NVIDIA Corporation stands out as the dominant player among its semiconductor peers, AMD and Intel, based on recent and projected financial metrics. NVIDIA’s revenue growth trajectory is exceptional, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 68.3% from 2022 to 2027, far surpassing industry norms. The company’s revenue exploded from $26.9B in 2022 to $60.9B in 2024, and is forecasted to reach over $240B by 2027—a scale neither AMD nor Intel is projected to approach.

NVIDIA’s profitability is equally impressive. Its EBITDA margin is expected to rise from 58.4% in 2024 to over 73% by 2027, reflecting strong operating leverage and a high-value product mix, largely driven by dominance in AI and data center markets. In contrast, AMD and Intel exhibit much lower EBITDA generation, with AMD’s EBITDA projected at just $8.6B in 2027 and Intel’s at $47.4B, compared to NVIDIA’s staggering $382B.

Valuation multiples tell a nuanced story. NVIDIA’s EV/EBITDA multiples are high in recent years (81.0x in 2023, declining to 31.4x by 2026), indicating a premium for its growth and earnings power. By comparison, AMD’s and Intel’s multiples are more modest and volatile, reflecting their slower growth and greater exposure to legacy markets.

Overall, NVIDIA is outpacing AMD and Intel by a wide margin in growth, margins, and earnings scalability. This performance, driven by AI and high-performance computing demand, justifies its premium valuation, but also sets a high bar for continued execution and innovation as competition intensifies.
