NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) has experienced a dramatic financial transformation over the past three years, with revenue surging from about $26.9 billion in FY2022 to a projected $130.5 billion in FY2025, reflecting a CAGR exceeding 68%. This rapid top-line expansion is mirrored by EBITDA growth, expected to escalate from $11.4 billion in FY2022 to $86.1 billion in FY2025 and $144.6 billion in FY2026. Margins have expanded significantly, with EBITDA margins forecast to reach 70%+ by FY2027, highlighting operating leverage and pricing power, especially in AI and data center markets.

From a valuation standpoint, NVIDIA currently trades at a premium but with a rapidly declining forward PE ratio—falling from 106x in FY2023 to 39.9x in FY2025 and 34.1x in FY2026. The EV/EBITDA multiple also compresses from 81.0x in FY2023 to 33.8x in FY2025 and 31.4x in FY2026. In comparison, AMD and Intel trade at lower multiples, with AMD’s EV/EBITDA at 47.8x and Intel’s at 14.5x for FY2025, but both lag far behind NVIDIA in growth, scale, and profitability. For example, NVIDIA’s FY2025 EBITDA is more than 10x that of AMD and 6x that of Intel, underscoring its market dominance.

Given this context, NVIDIA’s premium valuation appears justified by its unmatched growth profile, superior margins, and industry leadership in accelerated computing and AI. While the stock is not inexpensive, ongoing margin expansion and robust earnings growth suggest further upside. Based on historical and peer multiples, a fair value range would likely be in the low- to mid-30x forward PE and EV/EBITDA, aligning closely with current estimates, supporting the view that NVIDIA remains appropriately valued for a high-growth, high-margin technology leader.
