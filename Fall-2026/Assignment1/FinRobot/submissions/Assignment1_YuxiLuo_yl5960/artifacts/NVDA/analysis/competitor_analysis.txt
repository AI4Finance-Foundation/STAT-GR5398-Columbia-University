NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) stands out as the clear leader among its semiconductor peers, AMD and Intel, driven by explosive revenue and EBITDA growth, as well as superior profitability metrics. NVIDIA's top-line growth is exceptional—a CAGR of 68.3% is forecast from 2024 through 2027, with revenue expected to nearly quadruple from $60.9 billion in 2024 to $249.9 billion by 2027. In contrast, AMD and Intel's growth rates and absolute numbers are significantly lower, indicating NVIDIA's dominant position in high-growth markets like AI and accelerated computing.

Profitability is a key differentiator. NVIDIA's EBITDA is projected to leap from $35.6 billion in 2024 to $382 billion by 2027, dwarfing AMD ($8.6 billion in 2027) and Intel ($47.4 billion in 2027). EBITDA margins for NVIDIA are also outstanding, expanding to over 70% by 2027 versus lower double-digit EBITDA margins typical for AMD and Intel. Contribution margins and SG&A margins further highlight NVIDIA's operating efficiency, with the company maintaining a high contribution margin (above 70%) and disciplined SG&A spending, forecast to drop below 1% of revenue by 2027.

Valuation metrics reflect the premium investors assign to NVIDIA. Its EV/EBITDA ratio, while high (31.4x in 2026), is justified by its growth trajectory and margin profile, and is broadly comparable to AMD’s, while Intel trades at a significant discount due to growth and margin challenges. NVIDIA’s PE ratio is forecast to decline but remain elevated (32.4x by 2027), signaling continued market confidence in its execution and future prospects.

In summary, NVIDIA's outsized growth, industry-leading margins, and premium valuations set it apart from AMD and Intel. Its dominant position in AI accelerators and data center solutions underpins its superior financial performance, justifying its status as the semiconductor industry's clear front-runner.
