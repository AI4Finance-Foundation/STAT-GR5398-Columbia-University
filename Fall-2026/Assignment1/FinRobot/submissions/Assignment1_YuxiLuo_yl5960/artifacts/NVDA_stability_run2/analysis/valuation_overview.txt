NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) is currently trading at premium valuation metrics, reflecting its dominant position in the AI and data center semiconductor space. As of 2024, NVDA’s forward P/E ratio stands at 51.6x, projected to decline to 37.8x in 2026 and 32.4x in 2027, as consensus expects robust earnings growth. The company’s EV/EBITDA multiple is 43.5x for 2024E, compressing to 33.8x in 2025 and 31.4x in 2026, based on explosive projected EBITDA growth.

Compared to peers, NVIDIA’s 2024E EV/EBITDA (43.5x) is higher than AMD’s (38.3x) but much lower than Intel’s (106.9x), likely reflecting stronger growth and profitability. By 2025, NVIDIA’s EV/EBITDA (33.8x) is above INTC (14.5x) and AMD (47.8x), although its scale and margins are far superior. The company’s EBITDA is expected to grow at a CAGR exceeding 70% through 2027, dwarfing peer growth, as NVDA captures outsized demand in accelerated computing and AI.

NVIDIA’s contribution and EBITDA margins are forecast to expand meaningfully, reaching 74% and 73.5% respectively by 2027, indicating exceptional operating leverage versus peers. Revenue is projected to more than quadruple from 2023 to 2026 (68.3% CAGR), supporting the elevated valuation.

In fair value terms, while current multiples are rich by historical standards, the premium is justified by NVIDIA’s unmatched growth, margin profile, and market leadership in high-demand AI infrastructure. Using 2025E EBITDA and a peer-average EV/EBITDA of ~30x, fair value appears aligned with current trading levels. Downside risks include potential AI demand normalization or increased competition, but on present trajectory, NVIDIA merits its valuation premium.
