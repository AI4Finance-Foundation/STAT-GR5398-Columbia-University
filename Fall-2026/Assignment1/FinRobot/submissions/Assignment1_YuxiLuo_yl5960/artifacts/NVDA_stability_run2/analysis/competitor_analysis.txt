NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) stands out as the clear leader in the semiconductor and accelerated computing space, especially when compared to its primary peers, AMD and Intel (INTC). NVIDIA’s financial performance has been exceptional, with revenue surging from $26.9 billion in 2022 and 2023 to an estimated $130.5 billion in 2025 and a projected $215.9 billion in 2026. This represents a remarkable 68.3% CAGR, driven by explosive demand for NVIDIA’s AI and data center products. In contrast, AMD and Intel show far more modest growth trajectories.

NVIDIA’s EBITDA performance is particularly noteworthy. In 2023, NVIDIA’s EBITDA was $5.99 billion, below Intel’s $11.24 billion, but by 2024, NVIDIA is projected to leap ahead to $35.58 billion, vastly outpacing both AMD ($5.26 billion) and Intel ($1.2 billion). This rapid expansion continues, with NVIDIA’s EBITDA expected to reach $144.6 billion by 2026 and $382 billion by 2027, dwarfing its peers’ figures.

Profitability metrics underscore NVIDIA’s superior operating leverage. Its EBITDA margin is set to climb from 22.2% (2023) to over 70% by 2027, while SG&A costs drop as a percentage of revenue. Contribution margins and EPS also expand dramatically, highlighting efficient cost management and strong pricing power.

On valuation, NVIDIA trades at a premium, with EV/EBITDA ratios peaking at 81x in 2023 but moderating to 31x by 2026, reflecting both rapid earnings growth and high investor expectations. AMD and Intel have lower multiples, indicating either slower growth or lower confidence in their ability to capture the current AI-driven market opportunity.

In summary, NVIDIA enjoys clear financial and operational advantages over AMD and Intel, powered by its dominance in AI and accelerated computing. However, its premium valuation reflects lofty expectations that require continued execution and market leadership.
