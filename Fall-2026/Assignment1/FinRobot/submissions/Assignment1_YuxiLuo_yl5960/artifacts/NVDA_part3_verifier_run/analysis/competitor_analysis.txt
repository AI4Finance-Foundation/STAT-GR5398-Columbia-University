NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) has delivered extraordinary financial performance compared to its primary peers, AMD (Advanced Micro Devices) and Intel (INTC), particularly over the period from FY2022A to FY2026A. Reported revenue grew from $26.9B in FY2022A to $215.9B in FY2026A, representing a remarkable CAGR of 68.3%. This far outpaces peers, who have exhibited much slower top-line growth. NVDA’s reported EBITDA also surged, from $11.4B in FY2022A to $144.6B in FY2026A, highlighting the company’s ability to scale earnings alongside revenue.

Gross margins (contribution margin) have been a significant differentiator. NVIDIA reported margins between 56.9% and 75.0% over the last five years, indicating strong pricing power and operational efficiency. EBITDA margin also rose from 22.2% in FY2023A to 66.9% in FY2026A, with this pipeline’s forecast suggesting further improvement to 73.5% by 2029E.

Peer comparison on profitability underscores NVIDIA’s dominance. AMD’s reported EBITDA in 2025 was $7.3B, while Intel’s was $14.4B, both substantially lower than NVIDIA’s $86.1B in the same year. The pipeline’s 2027E projections continue this trend, with NVIDIA forecasted to reach $159.8B EBITDA, compared to AMD’s $8.6B and Intel’s $47.4B.

Valuation, however, remains premium. NVIDIA’s reported EV/EBITDA peaked at 81.0x in 2023A, declining to 31.4x by 2026A, still above Intel and AMD for most years, reflecting market expectations of continued superior growth and profitability. Despite a high PE ratio (37.8x in 2026A), ongoing margin expansion and revenue growth support this premium.

In summary, NVIDIA substantially outperforms AMD and Intel across revenue growth, margins, and profit scale, justifying its market premium. Its leadership in AI and data center markets, reflected in these financials, positions it as the clear industry leader among its peers.
