NVIDIA’s reported FY2026 results reflect extraordinary growth, with revenue reaching $215.9 billion, up from $26.9 billion in FY2022, representing a four-year CAGR of 68.3%. This is driven by explosive demand for AI and accelerated computing, as seen in the 66.9% EBITDA margin for FY2026 and a contribution margin above 70% since FY2024. EPS for FY2026 was reported at $4.93, with a price-to-earnings (PE) ratio of 37.8x.

Looking forward, this pipeline’s forecast projects more moderate—but still robust—top-line growth: 5% in 2027E, 6% in 2028E, and 4% in 2029E. Margins are expected to further expand, with EBITDA margin forecasted to reach 73.5% by 2029E. EPS is projected to rise steadily, reaching $5.88 in 2029E, with the PE ratio declining to 32.4x by that year—suggesting continued multiple compression as earnings grow.

Peer comparison places NVIDIA at a premium. For FY2024, NVIDIA’s EV/EBITDA was 43.5x, higher than AMD (38.3x) but well below Intel (106.9x, but with depressed earnings). By FY2026, NVIDIA’s multiple compresses to 31.4x, still within AMD’s historic range (17.9x–57.1x) and Intel’s (6.5x–106.9x), but justified by NVIDIA’s industry-leading margins and market dominance in AI hardware. NVIDIA’s 2027E EBITDA forecast of $159.8 billion dwarfs AMD and Intel projections, emphasizing its scale.

In summary, NVIDIA’s valuation remains rich relative to peers, justified by its superior growth, margin profile, and market position. The current PE of 37.8x and EV/EBITDA of 31.4x (FY2026) imply the market is pricing in continued outperformance, but with growth moderating, further multiple compression is likely. On a fair value basis, NVIDIA trades at a premium, but the premium is justified as long as it continues to deliver above-peer growth and profitability.
