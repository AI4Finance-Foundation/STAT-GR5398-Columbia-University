Valuation Overview: Intel Corporation (INTC)

Intel’s recent financial performance reflects significant operational challenges. Revenue has declined at a -9.6% CAGR from $79.0B in 2021 to a projected $53.1B in 2024, with only modest growth expected from 2025 onward. EBITDA margin has compressed sharply, from 42.9% in 2021 to a projected low of 2.3% in 2024, before recovering to around 27-31% by 2027. Earnings have collapsed, with EPS dropping from $4.89 (2021) to negative values in 2024 and beyond, resulting in a distorted PE ratio.

Peer comparison highlights Intel’s relative underperformance. While AMD and NVDA also experienced volatility, Nvidia’s EBITDA is surging (projected $38.2B in 2027), driven by leadership in AI and data center markets. Meanwhile, Intel’s EBITDA ($11.2B in 2023, $14.4B in 2025, $47.4B in 2027) lags Nvidia, though it recovers in later years. On EV/EBITDA, Intel’s valuation has spiked to 106.9x (2024), reflecting depressed earnings and market skepticism, but normalizes to 14.5x by 2025, in line with AMD (47.8x) and below Nvidia (33.8x).

Fair Value Assessment: Intel is trading at a high near-term EV/EBITDA multiple due to cyclical weakness and restructuring costs, but this is expected to moderate as margins recover. Compared to peers, Intel’s long-term EV/EBITDA (14-15x) appears reasonable, assuming successful execution of turnaround strategies. However, execution risk remains high, and Intel’s earnings outlook is still negative through at least 2025. Given these factors, Intel’s shares currently seem fairly to slightly overvalued on near-term metrics, but could offer upside if margin recovery materializes as projected. Investors should monitor execution risk and broader semiconductor demand trends closely.
