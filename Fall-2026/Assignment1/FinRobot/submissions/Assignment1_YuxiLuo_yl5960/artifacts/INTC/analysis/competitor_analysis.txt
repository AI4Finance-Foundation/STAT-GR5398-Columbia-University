Intel Corporation (INTC) faces a challenging competitive landscape as it contends with Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and NVIDIA (NVDA). Over the 2021-2023 period, Intel’s revenue declined sharply (CAGR: -9.6%), with revenue dropping from $79.0B in 2021 to $54.2B in 2023. This contraction is mirrored in EBITDA, which fell from $33.9B in 2021 to $11.2B in 2023, and margin compression—EBITDA margin slid from 42.9% to 20.7% over the same period. Intel’s profitability suffered, with EPS plummeting from $4.89 to just $0.40 in 2023, turning negative in 2024 and beyond.

By comparison, AMD, while smaller, has demonstrated greater resilience and growth in EBITDA, increasing from $4.17B in 2021 to an estimated $7.28B in 2025 and $8.6B in 2027. NVIDIA, meanwhile, has outpaced both rivals in profit growth, with EBITDA surging from $11.4B in 2022 to a projected $86.1B in 2025 and $382B in 2027, driven by its dominance in AI and accelerated computing.

On valuation, Intel’s EV/EBITDA ratio was historically lower than AMD’s and NVIDIA’s, suggesting it traded at a discount, possibly reflecting investor skepticism about its growth prospects. However, in 2024, Intel’s ratio spiked (106.9x), reflecting depressed EBITDA and negative earnings, whereas NVIDIA’s ratio remained elevated but more stable (43.5x). AMD’s ratios, while volatile, remain above Intel’s in most years, indicating higher growth expectations.

In summary, Intel is currently losing ground to both AMD and NVIDIA in terms of revenue growth, profitability, and investor sentiment. While cost containment and margin stabilization are expected in the coming years, Intel must execute effectively on its turnaround and innovation efforts to regain competitiveness against faster-growing and more profitable peers.
