Investment Update: Intel Corporation (INTC)

Recent Financial Performance  
Intel’s financial results over the past few years highlight a period of significant challenge and transition. Revenue has declined sharply from $79.0 billion in 2021 to $54.2 billion in 2023, representing a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of -9.6%. The contraction continued into 2024, with revenue expected to reach $53.1 billion, before a modest recovery is projected from 2025 onward. Profitability has also deteriorated: EBITDA fell from $33.9 billion in 2021 to just $1.2 billion in 2024, with margins compressing from 42.9% to 2.3%. Earnings per share (EPS) turned negative in 2024, and Intel’s PE and EV/EBITDA multiples have become less meaningful as profits have evaporated.

Growth Drivers  
Despite near-term headwinds, Intel is taking steps to reposition for future growth. The company is aggressively investing in next-generation process technologies and foundry capabilities, aiming to regain technological leadership in semiconductors. Management is also focused on cost optimization, as seen in declining SG&A margins, while recalibrating operational expenses. Longer-term, Intel’s exposure to AI infrastructure, data centers, and edge computing offers growth potential as demand for advanced chips accelerates.

Outlook  
Consensus forecasts suggest stabilization in 2025, with revenue expected to bottom out and return to single-digit growth through 2027. EBITDA and margins are projected to recover as operational leverage improves and product launches ramp up. However, Intel continues to lag peers like AMD and Nvidia in both growth and profitability, underscored by recent EV/EBITDA multiples and peer EBITDA trends.

In summary, Intel faces a challenging near-term outlook as it executes a complex turnaround. Investors should expect ongoing volatility but monitor progress on technology execution and market share gains, which are critical for a sustained recovery.
