NVIDIA Corporation stands out among its semiconductor peers, AMD and Intel, due to its exceptionally strong financial performance and rapid growth, particularly in recent years. NVIDIA’s revenue has surged from $26.9 billion in 2022 to a projected $130.5 billion in 2025, reflecting a remarkable CAGR of 68.3%. This growth far outpaces AMD and Intel, whose revenues and EBITDA figures have remained relatively stable or grown modestly in comparison.

Looking at EBITDA, NVIDIA’s trajectory is particularly notable. While AMD and Intel report EBITDA in the single-digit to low double-digit billions, NVIDIA’s EBITDA is expected to reach $86.1 billion in 2025 and $144.6 billion in 2026, a magnitude higher than its peers. This translates into superior EBITDA margins, climbing from 22.2% in 2023 to an estimated 73.5% by 2027, while AMD and Intel historically maintain much lower margins.

NVIDIA’s profitability is also reflected in its contribution and EBITDA margins, both of which are expanding, and its cost structure is becoming more efficient, with SG&A margin dropping to below 1% by 2027. In contrast, AMD and Intel do not exhibit such dramatic improvements in operating leverage.

However, NVIDIA’s rapid growth and strong margins are reflected in its valuation. Its EV/EBITDA ratios have historically been much higher than peers, peaking at 81.0 in 2023. While this is expected to moderate to 31.4 by 2026, it still commands a premium compared to AMD and Intel, suggesting high market expectations for continued dominance and growth.

In summary, NVIDIA leads its peers in growth, profitability, and operational efficiency, justifying its premium valuation, but continued outperformance will be necessary to sustain its elevated market multiples.
