NVIDIA’s current valuation reflects its exceptional growth, profitability, and market leadership in AI and data center semiconductors. For FY2024, NVIDIA posted 126% revenue growth, and consensus estimates project a revenue CAGR of 68.3% through 2027, outpacing peers AMD and Intel by a wide margin. Margins are also expanding dramatically: EBITDA margins are forecast to rise from 58% in 2024 to over 73% in 2027, while SG&A as a percentage of revenue is trending towards just 0.6%—indicating strong operating leverage.

On valuation, NVIDIA trades at a 2024E PE ratio of 51.6x (falling to 32.4x by 2027E) and a 2024E EV/EBITDA of 43.5x (declining to 31.4x by 2026E). These multiples are elevated relative to most large-cap tech firms but are commensurate with NVIDIA’s near-monopoly in high-margin AI accelerators and rapid earnings growth. For comparison, AMD and Intel’s 2024E EV/EBITDA ratios are 38.3x and 106.9x, respectively, but both have much lower expected earnings growth and margins. By 2025, NVIDIA’s EV/EBITDA falls to 33.8x, while AMD and Intel are at 47.8x and 14.5x, respectively, further highlighting NVIDIA’s premium.

Given the robust earnings trajectory, margin expansion, and clear technological leadership, NVIDIA’s premium valuation appears justified versus peers. However, the stock’s fair value is sensitive to sustained high growth rates and AI demand; any disappointment could compress multiples. Based on consensus 2025-2026 EBITDA and peer multiples, fair value appears aligned with current levels, although upside is dependent on NVIDIA’s ability to maintain its growth lead and competitive moat.
