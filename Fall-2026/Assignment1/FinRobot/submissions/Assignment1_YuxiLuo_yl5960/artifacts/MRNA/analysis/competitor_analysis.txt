Moderna, Inc. (MRNA) operates in the biotechnology and pharmaceutical sector, competing with industry giants such as Pfizer (PFE) and Johnson & Johnson (JNJ). A review of key financial metrics reveals that Moderna experienced an exceptional revenue peak in 2021–2022, driven by its COVID-19 vaccine, but has since faced a sharp revenue decline, with a -42.5% CAGR projected through 2027. In contrast, JNJ and PFE, with diversified portfolios, have demonstrated more stable financial performance and resilience to pandemic-related demand swings.

By 2023, Moderna’s revenue dropped by over 60% year-over-year, and EBITDA turned negative (-$3.3B in 2023, worsening through 2025), while both JNJ and PFE maintained positive EBITDA, even as COVID-19 vaccine demand subsided. Moderna’s EBITDA margins reflect this struggle, plunging from 76.3% in 2021 to deeply negative levels, compared to JNJ’s and PFE’s consistently healthy margins.

Moderna’s EV/EBITDA ratios also highlight market concerns: negative values from 2023 onward (e.g., -11.06 in 2023 vs. JNJ’s 18.36 and PFE’s 24.11) suggest investor skepticism about future profitability. Moderna’s SG&A margin has soared to over 50%, indicating rising cost pressures, while its peers, with broader pipelines and established products, have managed costs more effectively.

In summary, Moderna’s financial performance is highly volatile and currently under significant pressure due to its reliance on a single product and the post-pandemic market reset. JNJ and PFE, benefiting from scale, diversification, and established franchises, show much greater stability and profitability. For Moderna to remain competitive, it must successfully diversify its portfolio, manage costs, and deliver on its pipeline beyond COVID-19.
