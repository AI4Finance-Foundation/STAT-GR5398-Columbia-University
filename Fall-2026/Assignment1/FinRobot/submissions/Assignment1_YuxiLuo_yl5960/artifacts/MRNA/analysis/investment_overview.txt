Investment Update: Moderna, Inc. (MRNA)

Moderna’s financial performance has undergone a significant transition following the COVID-19 pandemic. Revenue peaked at $18.9 billion in 2022, driven by unprecedented vaccine demand, but has since declined sharply—falling 64% in 2023 and projected to drop another 53% in 2024, with further declines expected into 2025. The post-pandemic environment has led to a revenue CAGR of -42.5% from 2021 to 2027, reflecting normalization in COVID-related sales and a lack of comparable new commercial products.

Profitability has been heavily impacted by the revenue decline. EBITDA dropped from $13.5 billion in 2021 to negative $3.3 billion in 2023, with continued losses expected through 2025. Contribution margin fell to 31.5% in 2023, though it is forecast to recover above 55% by 2025 as cost structures adjust. EBITDA margin is also expected to turn positive by 2025–2026, but at much lower absolute levels than in the pandemic era. On a peer comparison, Moderna’s negative EBITDA stands in contrast to Johnson & Johnson and Pfizer, which continue to generate strong positive EBITDA.

Moderna’s near-term outlook remains challenging. While the company is investing in new mRNA-based treatments and vaccines, these have yet to materially offset the loss in COVID vaccine revenue. Modest revenue stabilization is projected from 2025 onward, with low single-digit growth rates, but profitability is likely to remain under pressure.

Valuation metrics such as PE and EV/EBITDA are not meaningful due to negative earnings and EBITDA. Compared to peers, Moderna trades at a discount but faces higher risk given its heavy product concentration and ongoing losses.

In summary, Moderna is in a transitional phase. Recovery depends on successful pipeline execution and diversification, which remain medium-term risks. Investors should expect continued volatility and limited near-term upside until new products deliver substantive revenue.
