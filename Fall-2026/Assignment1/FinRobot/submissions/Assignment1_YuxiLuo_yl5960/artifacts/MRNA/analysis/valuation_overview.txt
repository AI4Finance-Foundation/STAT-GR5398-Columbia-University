Moderna, Inc. (MRNA) is currently experiencing a significant revenue decline following the COVID-19 vaccine boom, with top-line sales dropping from $18.9 billion in 2022 to a projected $2.0 billion in 2025—a compound annual decline of 42.5%. This contraction has driven a sharp deterioration in profitability: EBITDA swung from $9.95 billion in 2022 to negative $3.28 billion in 2023, with continued losses expected through 2026. EBITDA margins, which peaked at 52.7% in 2022, are now deeply negative and are only forecast to turn modestly positive (4.4%-7.4%) by 2027, reflecting the company’s struggle to offset falling COVID sales with new products.

In terms of valuation, Moderna’s trailing and forward PE ratios are currently negative, reflecting ongoing net losses. Its EV/EBITDA multiple has also turned negative, at -11.1x in 2023 and -4.2x expected for 2025. By comparison, peers such as Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) and Pfizer (PFE) are trading at 2025 EV/EBITDA multiples of 13.0x and 12.4x, respectively, supported by positive and stable earnings. This stark disparity highlights the market’s skepticism about Moderna’s near-term earnings recovery and pipeline execution.

Assessing fair value, Moderna trades at a steep discount to large-cap pharma peers due to its weak earnings outlook and dependence on future pipeline success. While the negative multiples preclude traditional valuation methods, the gap versus peers suggests limited upside until the company demonstrates a path back to profitability. Unless Moderna can deliver new commercial products or cost efficiencies, the current valuation appears justified, and the shares are likely to remain under pressure relative to peers in the near term.
