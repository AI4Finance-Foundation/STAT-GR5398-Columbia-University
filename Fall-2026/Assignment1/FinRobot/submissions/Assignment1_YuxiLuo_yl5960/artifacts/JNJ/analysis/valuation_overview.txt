Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) currently trades at a 2024E PE ratio of 24.5x, with forward multiples expected to normalize to 18.8x in 2025E and 17.0x in 2026E. The company's EV/EBITDA multiple for 2024E is 14.4x, declining to 13.0x by 2025E. Revenue is projected to grow at a 6.2% CAGR through 2027, with EBITDA margins expanding from 27.9% in 2024E to 47.3% in 2027E, reflecting operational leverage and potential cost control improvements.

Peer comparison shows JNJ’s 2024E EV/EBITDA (14.4x) is below AbbVie’s (25.3x) and Pfizer’s (11.9x), positioning JNJ at a reasonable valuation midpoint within its large-cap pharma cohort. In terms of forward growth, JNJ’s EBITDA is forecast to outpace both AbbVie and Pfizer by 2025E, with a substantial jump in profitability (EBITDA nearly doubling from $24.8B in 2024E to $41.1B in 2025E). This improvement is likely tied to product mix, cost initiatives, and the full-year impact of recent corporate actions (e.g., Kenvue spin-off).

Given its steady revenue growth, margin expansion, and lower peer-relative EV/EBITDA for 2025E, JNJ appears fairly valued to modestly undervalued. The current PE and EV/EBITDA ratios embed expectations for improving fundamentals but do not fully reflect JNJ’s robust cash flow trajectory and market defensive qualities. On a DCF or relative basis, upside could be realized as estimates de-risk and the market rewards JNJ’s stability and growth. The stock is attractive for long-term investors seeking consistent returns and resilience versus sector peers.
