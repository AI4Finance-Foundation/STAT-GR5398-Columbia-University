Pfizer Inc. (PFE) currently trades at a forward P/E of 17.4x (2025E) and a forward EV/EBITDA of 12.4x, broadly in line with large-cap pharmaceutical peers such as Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) and AbbVie (ABBV), whose 2025E EV/EBITDA multiples are 13.0x and 26.6x, respectively. Historically, Pfizer’s valuation has oscillated, reflecting sharp COVID-related revenue swings: the P/E moved from 9.2x (2022) to an elevated 76.7x (2023) as earnings cratered post-pandemic, then normalized to the mid-to-high teens as earnings recover.

Peer comparison shows Pfizer’s EV/EBITDA multiples are below ABBV but close to JNJ, suggesting the market is cautious but not overly punitive. However, Pfizer’s EBITDA margin (projected to recover to 50-53% by 2026-2027) and contribution margin (over 70%) are strong, reassuring given the revenue reset and cost discipline. EPS is expected to rebound from $0.38 (2023) to $1.44 (2025E) and $1.62 (2027E), but remains well below 2021-22 levels.

Pfizer’s revenue CAGR for 2021-2027 is negative (-6.3%), reflecting the post-COVID comedown, but management’s focus on cost control and margin expansion is evident in sharply improving EBITDA and margin forecasts. Still, the company’s valuation is unlikely to re-rate meaningfully higher without clear top-line growth catalysts.

Fair value assessment: Pfizer’s current valuation implies a reasonable risk/reward profile. The shares are not expensive versus peers on EV/EBITDA or P/E, but upside may be capped until revenue growth resumes. If Pfizer executes on pipeline and business development, a fair value in line with the group median (EV/EBITDA ~13-14x) is justified, suggesting the stock is fairly valued at current levels. Downside risk is limited by cost actions and a strong balance sheet, but upside is dependent on new growth drivers.
