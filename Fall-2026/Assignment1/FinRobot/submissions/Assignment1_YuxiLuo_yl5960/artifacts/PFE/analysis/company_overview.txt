Pfizer Inc. (NYSE: PFE) is a global biopharmaceutical company renowned for its innovation and leadership in the research, development, and commercialization of medicines and vaccines. Its business model centers on discovering, developing, manufacturing, and marketing prescription pharmaceuticals across a diverse range of therapeutic areas, including oncology, immunology, cardiology, internal medicine, rare diseases, and vaccines. Pfizer generates revenue primarily through direct sales of its proprietary medicines to healthcare providers, governments, and distributors worldwide.

The company's product portfolio includes blockbuster drugs such as Ibrance (oncology), Eliquis (cardiovascular), Prevnar (vaccines), and the COVID-19 vaccine Comirnaty, which was developed in partnership with BioNTech. Pfizer also markets Paxlovid, an oral COVID-19 treatment. The company continues to invest heavily in R&D to replenish its pipeline, focusing on both innovative new molecules and lifecycle management of existing franchises.

Pfizer holds a strong market position as one of the largest pharmaceutical companies globally. Its scale, extensive global reach, and robust R&D capabilities enable it to compete effectively with peers such as AbbVie (ABBV) and Johnson & Johnson (JNJ). However, Pfizer's fortunes in recent years have been closely tied to its COVID-19 vaccine and treatment sales, which drove exceptional revenue growth in 2021 and 2022 but have since normalized.

Financially, Pfizer experienced a revenue surge to $100.3 billion in 2022 (+23.4% YoY), primarily due to pandemic-related products. However, as COVID-19 demand receded, 2023 revenue declined sharply to $59.6 billion (-40.6% YoY), with a modest recovery projected for 2024 ($63.6 billion, +6.8%). Despite the revenue drop, cost reductions and operational efficiencies have improved contribution and EBITDA margins, which are projected to reach 73.3% and 53.5% respectively by 2027. Post-pandemic normalization has impacted EPS (falling from $5.59 in 2022 to $0.38 in 2023), with gradual recovery expected over the next few years.

Compared to peers, Pfizer's recent EBITDA performance lagged in 2023 but is forecast to recover, and its EV/EBITDA valuation remains competitive. Pfizer’s pivot to non-COVID growth drivers and its strong pipeline position it well for long-term stability and leadership in the global pharmaceutical industry, though near-term growth faces headwinds as the COVID-19 demand tailwinds fade.
