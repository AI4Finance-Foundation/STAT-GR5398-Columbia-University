AbbVie Inc. (ABBV) currently trades at a premium valuation relative to both its historical averages and peers. As of 2023, ABBV’s EV/EBITDA multiple stands at 18.7x, up significantly from 12.8x in 2021, and well above Pfizer’s 24.1x and Johnson & Johnson’s 18.4x for the same year. However, ABBV’s forward EV/EBITDA multiples (25.3x in 2024 and 26.6x in 2025) are notably higher than JNJ (14.4x and 13.0x) and PFE (11.9x and 12.4x), reflecting either a market premium for AbbVie’s pipeline or skepticism about future earnings stability amid Humira’s patent expiry.

On a price/earnings basis, ABBV’s 2023 PE ratio soared to 56.8x, with forward estimates rising further (74.0x in 2024 and 96.4x in 2025). This sharp increase is due to declining GAAP EPS, driven by increased costs and lower net income, despite stable or rising revenue projections. In contrast, large-cap pharma peers such as JNJ and PFE typically trade at mid-to-high teens PE ratios, underlining AbbVie’s current overvaluation on this metric.

Fundamentally, AbbVie’s 2023 revenue dip (-6.4% YoY) was offset by positive forward growth expectations (5-6% annually through 2027) and improving contribution/EBITDA margins post-2024, indicating management’s confidence in new product launches and cost controls. However, the current premium multiples are not yet backed by a visible rebound in EPS or EBITDA, adding risk for new investors.

Based on peer comparisons and current earnings power, ABBV appears overvalued. A fair value assessment would likely place ABBV closer to its peer EV/EBITDA range (13-18x), implying downside risk from current market levels unless earnings recover more quickly than forecast. Investors should monitor margin recovery and new product traction closely to justify the premium valuation.
