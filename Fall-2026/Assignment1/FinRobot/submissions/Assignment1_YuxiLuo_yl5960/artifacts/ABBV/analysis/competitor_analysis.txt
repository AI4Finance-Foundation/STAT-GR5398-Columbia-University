AbbVie Inc. (ABBV) is a major player in the pharmaceutical sector, competing closely with Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) and Pfizer (PFE). Analyzing AbbVie’s recent financial performance and comparing it to its peers highlights several key strengths and challenges.

AbbVie’s revenue saw modest growth from 2021 to 2022, but declined in 2023 due to patent cliffs and increased competition, before rebounding in 2024 and showing projected growth through 2027 (CAGR ~2.1%). Contribution margins have remained strong, hovering around 70%, signaling operational efficiency. However, EBITDA margin dropped sharply in 2023 (31.6%) and 2024 (26.5%), before an expected recovery above 48% from 2025 onwards. This margin volatility contrasts with JNJ, which maintains more stable EBITDA and EBITDA margins over the period.

Compared to Pfizer, AbbVie’s EBITDA was higher in 2021 and 2023, but lags JNJ in the near-term projections (2025-2026), as JNJ’s EBITDA is set to nearly triple AbbVie’s in 2025. AbbVie’s EV/EBITDA ratio has risen significantly, reaching 25.3x in 2024 and 26.6x in 2025, well above both JNJ and PFE, suggesting AbbVie is trading at a premium relative to its peers, possibly reflecting market optimism about its pipeline or concerns about near-term earnings volatility.

EPS has declined substantially since 2022, with AbbVie’s PE ratio ballooning above 90x, indicating either a depressed earnings base or high market expectations for future earnings recovery. In contrast, JNJ and PFE maintain lower EV/EBITDA multiples, reflecting steadier earnings and perhaps less perceived risk.

In summary, AbbVie faces near-term earnings headwinds but benefits from strong operational margins and anticipated recovery. However, relative to JNJ and PFE, AbbVie currently trades at a higher valuation, underscoring both the risks and the market’s long-term confidence in its ability to innovate and regain earnings momentum.
