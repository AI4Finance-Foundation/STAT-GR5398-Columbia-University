Apple Inc. (AAPL) currently trades at a premium relative to both its historical average and peers. The stock’s 2024 PE ratio is 37.3x, well above its 2021-2023 range (24.5x–27.8x), and forward PE ratios remain elevated (32.4x for 2025E, 30.8x for 2026E). On an EV/EBITDA basis, Apple trades at 26.6x for 2024 and 27.0x for 2025, above its 2021–2023 range (19.1x–21.6x), and higher than Alphabet (GOOGL) and Microsoft (MSFT) for most periods. For 2024E, Alphabet and Microsoft trade at lower EV/EBITDA multiples (17.3x and 26.1x, respectively).

Apple’s revenue growth is expected to be modest, with a 3.3% CAGR through 2027. Consensus forecasts show mid-single-digit top-line growth (4–6% annually) and improving margins, with EBITDA margin expanding from 33.7% in 2023 to nearly 45% by 2027. EPS is projected to rise from $6.16 (2023A) to $8.93 (2027E), a 10% CAGR, supported by ongoing buybacks and margin gains.

Compared to peers, Apple’s EBITDA is strong but not outpacing Microsoft or Alphabet’s expected growth. By 2027, both GOOGL and MSFT are projected to exceed Apple in absolute EBITDA.

Valuation at current levels appears stretched relative to both Apple’s historical norms and peer averages, particularly given moderate growth expectations. While Apple’s premium is partly justified by brand strength, balance sheet quality, and execution, the current multiples leave limited margin of safety. Based on peer multiples and growth prospects, fair value would likely imply a modest discount from current levels, suggesting the shares are fully valued or slightly overvalued at present.
