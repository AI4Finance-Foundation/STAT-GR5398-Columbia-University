Thesis Status: Thesis Confirmed

Key Takeaways:
- AMD’s Q1 2024 earnings delivered solid revenue growth year-on-year and a meaningful sequential improvement, led by strong AI and data center demand
- Revenue for the quarter topped both Street expectations and management’s own guidance, with especially robust performance in the Data Center and Embedded segments
- Management issued an upward revision to full-year guidance, reflecting growing confidence in sustained AI demand across enterprise customers
- EPS outperformed consensus, with gross margin expansion as product mix shifted toward higher-value AI offerings
- Compared to key peers, AMD’s margin recovery and high revenue growth are outpacing Intel, although Nvidia remains a strong competitive force in AI

Performance Analysis:
AMD’s Q1 2024 results were ahead of both prior-year and consensus expectations. Revenue came in at $25.8 billion for the quarter, up 13.7% year-over-year and 5% above consensus. EPS was $1.01, meaningfully higher than the $0.91 expected by analysts. Key revenue drivers included a sharp rebound in Data Center segment performance, with AI accelerator sales nearly doubling from the prior quarter, and Embedded segment demand showing ongoing resilience. Gross margin expanded to 49.4%, up 330 basis points year-over-year, reflecting both product mix improvement and earlier supply chain investments. Notably, AMD’s sequential and year-over-year EBITDA margin improvement stands out relative to Intel, although Nvidia’s margin expansion and revenue scale remain a high bar for the industry.

Thesis Impact:
These results reinforce the core tenets of the original investment thesis, which centered on AMD’s unique positioning to benefit from secular growth in data center, AI, and high-performance compute markets. The recent outperformance in AI and cloud products is tracking ahead of initial projections, strengthening AMD’s competitive positioning. Management’s raised full-year revenue guidance, underpinned by increased visibility into hyperscaler demand, offers further evidence that AMD is executing well against longer-term growth drivers. Gross margin expansion and improving EBITDA highlight strong operating leverage. No adverse developments have emerged to challenge the thesis, and long-term forecasts on growth and margin improvement appear well supported by recent results.

Recent Developments:
There have been no major negative surprises since last quarter. Of note, AMD announced a new partnership to supply custom silicon for a major cloud provider and continues to benefit from regulatory tailwinds as US policy incentivizes domestic semiconductor investment. No significant M&A or regulatory headwinds have emerged since the last recommendation.

Valuation & Outlook Revision:
Following the earnings beat and higher guidance, AMD’s valuation remains elevated relative to historical metrics, with a forward PE of 124x and a 2025E EV/EBITDA north of 47x. However, this premium is largely justified versus Intel due to AMD’s superior growth and margin trajectory, and arguably looks reasonable compared to Nvidia given the relative scale of AI-driven upside. The 6-12 month outlook is constructive: with downside risks tempered by proven execution and strengthening demand signals, the stock remains a core AI/high-performance compute play. Investors should expect continued volatility amid sector-wide rerating, but hold or selectively increase positions on pullbacks, as the growth and margin improvement drivers are solidly intact.
