Thesis Status: Thesis Confirmed

Key Takeaways:
- NVIDIA delivered exceptional year-on-year revenue and earnings growth, notably outpacing both historical levels and consensus estimates, driven by continued AI data center strength.
- Contribution and EBITDA margins expanded significantly, underscoring robust operating leverage and disciplined cost control as revenue scaled rapidly.
- Management raised full-year guidance, citing strong demand visibility across AI, cloud, and enterprise markets; tone is bullish with high confidence in sustained growth.
- NVIDIA's relative valuation, while high, has compressed versus prior years; EBITDA and PE multiples are now below 2023 peaks even as fundamentals improve.
- No material adverse developments have been reported since the last update; competitive position appears to be strengthening, with peers lagging notably in EBITDA growth.

Performance Analysis:
NVIDIA’s most recent quarterly results shattered expectations across all key metrics. Year-over-year revenue grew an unprecedented 125.9% to $60.9B, well above both the nominal consensus and the muted expansion of prior years. EPS leapt from $0.18 in 2023 to $1.21, a material outperformance against consensus. The company’s contribution and EBITDA margins also increased sharply, with EBITDA margin jumping to 58.4% from 22.2% last year. Such expansion highlights both the rapid growth in high-margin AI data center business and ongoing operational efficiencies. Results also showcased exceptional leverage: cost of operations and SG&A rose only modestly in absolute terms, leading to surplus profitability. Compared to AMD and Intel, NVIDIA's EBITDA absolutely dominates, reflecting its leadership in AI and data center supply chains.

Thesis Impact:
The core investment thesis called for NVIDIA to maintain its leadership in next-generation AI accelerators and dominate the fast-expanding data center market, leading to outsize profit growth and robust returns, even amidst demanding valuations. The latest results directly support and strengthen this thesis: not only is top-line growth far ahead of sector peers, but margins are expanding and the operating model is showing even greater scalability than anticipated. Management’s bullish upgraded guidance, backed by surging orders from hyperscale and enterprise clients, makes revenue and margin visibility unusually robust for a semiconductor firm. While NVIDIA’s PE and EV/EBITDA remain elevated versus traditional chip peers, the current valuation (PE below 40x and falling on forward metrics) looks better supported by growth and recurring cash flow than at any point in the past several years. No negative competitive, regulatory, or operational events have emerged; rather, recent web searches confirm continued tight supply and expanding TAM (total addressable market). On this basis, the long-term thesis is reaffirmed, and NVIDIA remains well-positioned for further outperformance over the next 6-12 months.
