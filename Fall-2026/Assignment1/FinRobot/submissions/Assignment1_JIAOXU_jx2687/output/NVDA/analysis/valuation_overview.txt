NVIDIA's current valuation, measured by its PE, PB, and EV/EBITDA multiples, is elevated compared to its own history and to traditional chip sector peers like AMD and Intel. Its trailing PE ratio of 51.6 in 2024 and forward PE declining toward the mid-30s reflect both its explosive earnings growth and premium market positioning. Historically, NVIDIA's PE has ranged from above 100 in recent years during market surges related to AI optimism, to below 40 at less exuberant times. This recent decline in PE coincides with an extraordinary acceleration in both revenue and profitability, as shown by the substantial increase in EBITDA and margin expansion through 2024 and 2025, while competitors have seen far less dramatic improvements.

Against AMD and Intel, NVIDIA's EV/EBITDA multiple in 2024 (43.45x) is significantly higher than Intel’s but in line or even slightly lower than AMD’s at times. However, NVIDIA justifies this premium due to its clear dominance in AI hardware and data center markets, which have led to triple-digit revenue growth over the past two years. While AMD also operates in a growth niche, its EBITDA and scale are still markedly below NVIDIA’s, and Intel’s turnaround remains unproven. The gap in both growth rates and margins supports a structurally higher valuation for NVIDIA compared to its US semiconductor peers.

The market’s expectations are for NVIDIA to deliver sustained high growth, evidenced by contraction in PE and EV/EBITDA multiples despite rising prices. The risk here is that these multiples still incorporate aggressive growth projections—any miss on AI demand, delays in new product adoption, or competitive disruption could lead to a sharp rerating. The high valuation also reflects a scarcity premium: NVIDIA is perceived as virtually the only way for investors to access large-scale, profitable AI infrastructure growth today. However, should the broader AI cycle moderate, or if supply or regulatory pressures arise, there is a risk that part of this premium—potential bubble elements—could unwind quickly.

In conclusion, while NVIDIA's valuation remains well above historic market averages, these are partly justified by exceptional financial momentum and clear competitive advantages. Yet, the margin of safety is narrower than for traditional value investments, and investors should be aware that future returns are highly sensitive to continued flawless execution and favorable AI industry dynamics. For growth-oriented portfolios comfortable with volatility and high expectations, the current valuation is reasonable, but it carries inherent high market risk and requires close monitoring for shifts in growth narratives.
