Revenue Growth: Microsoft's revenue growth has notably accelerated, moving from 6.9% in 2023 to 15.7% in 2024 and 14.9% in 2025, driven by broad-based adoption of cloud services, AI integration, and continued diversification across business segments. This acceleration highlights the firm's strong execution in high-demand enterprise technology and cloud infrastructure, solidifying its dominant market position while making it a key platform for digital transformation. Such multipronged revenue drivers underpin the company's resilience and reinforce the sustainability of growth, as Microsoft remains well-entrenched across both commercial and consumer verticals.

Gross Profit Margin: Contribution margin has steadily expanded from 68.4% in 2022 to 69.8% in 2024, reflecting robust pricing power, high value-addition from software and cloud businesses, and operational leverage from scale. Margin gains demonstrate the stickiness of Microsoft's products and the embedded switching costs in its ecosystem. This margin expansion underscores a durable competitive advantage, enabled by recurring revenue models and continued investment in high-margin cloud/AI offerings, further signaling high business quality and long-term profitability.

SG&A Expense Margin: The SG&A margin fell consistently from 14.3% in 2023 to 10.4% in 2026, demonstrating significant operating leverage and disciplined cost management. This declining trend points to scalability in operations, allowing Microsoft to grow revenue while keeping overheads in check—a hallmark of mature, efficiently-run platform businesses. As SG&A as a percentage of sales decreases, it highlights management’s ability to drive greater profitability from incremental revenues, suggesting that margin improvements are not driven by unsustainable cost cuts but by true scale advantages.

EBITDA Margin Stability: Microsoft’s EBITDA margins have expanded substantially, rising from 49.6% in 2023 to 62.5% in 2026. This margin profile considerably outpaces Apple’s (which remains in the mid-30% range) and is only rivaled by Nvidia in future years. Such superior profitability points to the high quality, subscription-heavy nature of Microsoft’s business and its entrenched ecosystem. Furthermore, while Microsoft’s EV/EBITDA multiple has been higher than Apple’s in recent years, its declining trend (from 26.1x in 2024 to 13.9x in 2026) combined with industry-leading margins suggests increasing investment attractiveness as the risk/reward improves. For institutional investors, this profitability leadership paired with multiple compression supports a premium valuation, justified by clear competitive advantages and sustained cash flow generation.
