Revenue Growth: NVIDIA's revenue growth is driven by a structural transformation in AI and accelerated computing demand. The company’s jump from $26.9B in 2023A to $60.9B in 2024A (+125.9%), then nearly doubling again to $130.5B in 2025A, reflects rapid adoption of GPU and data center solutions by hyperscale and enterprise customers. This scale and velocity indicate that NVIDIA is capturing outsized share of AI infrastructure investment, establishing itself as a de facto platform standard for AI workloads. The depth and sustainability of this growth point to entrenched competitive advantages and increased barriers to entry for rivals, positioning NVDA as a critical supplier in next-generation computing.

Gross Profit Margin: The gross profit margin (reported as contribution margin) expanded sharply from 56.9% in 2023A to 72.7% in 2024A, and continues above 70% through 2026. This margin expansion stems from a combination of product mix shift toward higher-margin data center products, pricing power amid supply constraints, and operational leverage as fixed costs are absorbed over a larger sales base. Such pronounced margins signal durable competitive moats, notably IP leadership, limited direct competition at the high end, and a fundamentally superior cost structure. This margin structure reinforces NVIDIA’s identity as a high-quality business, which is rare within semiconductor hardware.

SG&A Expense Margin: SG&A margin dropped from 9.0% (2023A) to 4.4% (2024A), then further to 2.1% by 2026A, highlighting powerful operating leverage. Despite unprecedented revenue expansion, SG&A costs have been tightly controlled, reflecting management’s discipline and the highly scalable nature of NVIDIA’s business model. This margin compression supports long-term scalability and indicates that overhead investment is not rising in tandem with revenue, which is exceptional for a company at this growth stage. For investors, this signals not only efficient execution but also potential for stronger free cash flow generation as the business matures.

EBITDA Margin Stability: NVIDIA’s EBITDA margin soared from 22.2% in 2023A to 58.4% in 2024A, reaching nearly 67% by 2026A—dramatically above AMD and Intel. Peers' EBITDA margins typically range from low to mid-teens, with AMD’s forecast (e.g., $8.6B on much smaller revenue in 2027) and Intel’s margins remaining significantly lower. This demonstrates NVIDIA’s leadership in profitability and scalability. Despite a premium EV/EBITDA multiple (e.g., 43.5x vs. AMD’s 38.3x and Intel’s 106.9x in 2024), investors are paying for unmatched margin, growth, and cash generation quality. For institutional investors, this margin leadership justifies a structural premium valuation and solidifies NVIDIA’s status as the highest-quality comp in AI-driven semis.
