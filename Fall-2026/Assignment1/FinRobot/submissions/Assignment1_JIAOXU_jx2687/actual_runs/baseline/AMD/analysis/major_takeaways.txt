Revenue Growth:
AMD delivered a standout 43.6% revenue growth in 2022, primarily fueled by strength in data center, embedded, and gaming segments, reflecting successful product launches and deepening customer engagements. However, the 2023 revenue contracted by 3.9%, indicating some cyclical headwinds or normalization after a peak COVID demand, or competitive pressures. The 13.7% rebound in 2024 highlights AMD's ability to reaccelerate growth, focusing on high-growth markets like AI, cloud, and custom silicon. The strategic implication is AMD’s growing relevance in critical compute markets and its improving product portfolio quality, strengthening its long-term market position against peers like Intel and NVIDIA.

Gross Profit Margin:
Gross margins show an improvement from 44.9% in 2022 to 46.1% in 2023 and rising further to 49.4% in 2024. This expansion signals robust pricing power and operational leverage, as AMD migrates toward higher-value product mix and capitalizes on supply chain efficiencies. The company’s ability to increase contribution margins amid fluctuating revenue points to deepening competitive moats, stemming from product differentiation and sustained technological leadership, particularly in advanced nodes and chiplet architectures. This bodes well for future earnings quality and reinforces AMD's status as a high-quality growth asset in the semiconductor space.

SG&A Expense Margin:
SG&A margin has trended modestly higher, from 8.8% in 2021 to 10.4% in 2023, peaking at 12.0% in 2025 before moderating slightly. While this uptick implies increased investment in R&D, talent, and go-to-market efforts essential for scaling new verticals, the margin stabilizes around 10-11%, evidencing operating leverage as revenues grow. This effectiveness in managing expense growth relative to topline, especially as AMD scales, points to disciplined management execution and operational scalability, which are key for long-term profitability.

EBITDA Margin Stability:
AMD's EBITDA margin dipped from 25.3% in 2021 to 18.3% in 2023, reflecting near-term cost pressures and investments, but is reverting upwards towards 20.4% in 2024. While this margin remains below NVIDIA, which commands superior profitability (e.g., NVDA’s 2024 EBITDA guidance is markedly higher), AMD significantly outshines Intel, whose margins have deteriorated. Notably, AMD trades at a premium EV/EBITDA multiple (38.3x in 2024) versus Intel (107x, distorted by low EBITDA) but below NVIDIA’s 43.5x, reflecting its position as a high-growth challenger. This contextually elevated multiple signals investor confidence in AMD’s margin expansion potential and strategic positioning in AI and data center, underpinning its investment case as a structurally advantaged player amid semiconductor industry transformation.
