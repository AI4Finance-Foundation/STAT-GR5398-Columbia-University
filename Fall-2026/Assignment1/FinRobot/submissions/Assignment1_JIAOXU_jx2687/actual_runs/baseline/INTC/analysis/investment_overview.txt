Thesis Status: Thesis Under Review

Key Takeaways:
- Intel's Q1 2024 results showed revenue of $53.1 billion, down 2.1% year-over-year and below consensus expectations; EPS was sharply negative at -$4.38, entirely missing analyst forecasts due to non-recurring charges and continued margin compression.
- Contribution and EBITDA margins reached multi-year lows in 2024, signaling structural profitability challenges, though 2025-2027 forecasts show modest improvement driven by cost control and stabilization.
- Management emphasized ongoing turnaround efforts and investments in foundry, but near-term guidance remains cautious, and Intel guided flat topline with incremental improvements in profitability metrics.
- There is no material positive catalyst evident from the quarter, and Intel continues to lag key peers such as AMD and Nvidia on EBITDA growth and valuation metrics.

Performance Analysis:
For the latest quarter (Q1 2024), Intel reported revenue of $53.1 billion, marking a further 2.1% year-over-year decline. This figure was slightly below already modest analyst and market expectations. The company's EPS came in at -$4.38, a dramatic deterioration versus the prior year and far worse than consensus, driven by continued gross margin pressure, restructuring costs, and increased investment in new business initiatives (notably the foundry business). Moreover, the contribution margin shrank further to 32.7%, and EBITDA margin collapsed to just 2.3%—well below both company history and market expectations. The miss relative to consensus was largely due to weaker-than-anticipated data center demand and ongoing competitive and pricing headwinds in client computing. Compared to peers, Intel’s profitability is now meaningfully below that of AMD and dramatically worse than Nvidia, whose margins and valuations continue to expand.

Thesis Impact:
The original investment thesis for Intel was predicated on (a) stabilization and eventual recovery of the core PC and data center business, (b) successful expansion into foundry services as a long-term secular growth driver, and (c) improvement in profitability and competitive positioning vis-à-vis AMD and Nvidia. The latest results, together with managerial commentary, suggest the recovery trajectory is more protracted than initially anticipated. While management reaffirmed its commitment to the foundry transition and cost rationalization, tangible progress (in terms of margins, growth, or competitive wins) remains elusive. Despite forecast stabilization from 2025 onwards, current valuation is not compelling given near-term losses, operational uncertainty, and negative earnings, reflected in highly stretched PE and EV/EBITDA multiples for 2024. Forward multiples improve only if management executes on margin improvement, but relative to peers, Intel remains a laggard. Therefore, the thesis is under review pending evidence of execution and credible signs of fundamental business turnaround.
