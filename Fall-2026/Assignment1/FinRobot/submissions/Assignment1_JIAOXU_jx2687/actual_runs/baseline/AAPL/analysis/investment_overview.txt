Thesis Status: Thesis Under Review

Key Takeaways:
- Apple’s 2024 revenue of $391.0 billion grew 2% year-over-year, in line with consensus but below peer momentum.
- EPS for 2024 came in at $6.11, flat versus the prior year and below anticipated 2024 growth, reflecting margin compression despite stable topline.
- Guidance indicates modest revenue acceleration in 2025 and beyond, but at a reduced pace compared to peers such as Microsoft and Nvidia, with Apple projecting 5-6% annual revenue growth versus higher double-digits at rivals.
- Apple’s PE ratio has expanded post-earnings to 37.3x against a flat EPS backdrop, stretching valuation relative to historical levels and elevated versus MSFT and NVDA on forward multiples.
- Competitive and product cycle headwinds persist, with no recent blockbuster product launches or major positive regulatory news to alter the growth outlook.

Performance Analysis:
Apple’s fiscal 2024 results were broadly in line with expectations as revenue advanced just 2% year-over-year to $391 billion, matching consensus but failing to fully reclaim the prerecession growth cadence. EPS stagnated, coming in at $6.11 versus $6.16 the prior year, evidencing pressure from ongoing operating expense growth and a somewhat muted mix improvement. Margins slightly expanded at the gross and contribution levels, but SG&A grew faster than revenue, resulting in only modest EBITDA expansion.

Thesis Impact:
The core elements of the investment thesis have come under pressure. The original case was predicated on Apple’s ability to return to outsized revenue growth and margin expansion through continued iPhone innovation, services monetization, and emerging hardware/software categories. The latest results and guidance suggest only modest topline acceleration, with no significant new products or platform shifts to ignite faster growth. While financial resilience is intact, Apple is treading water relative to peers that are seeing inflections from AI and cloud—particularly Microsoft and Nvidia. With valuation now elevated (PE above 37x), the risk-reward calculus has become less favorable, especially given Apple’s slowing growth. No material M&A, regulatory, or product disclosures since the last update have emerged to alter this outlook. While Apple remains a quality franchise, the thesis now hinges on accelerating product innovation or a major new revenue stream to justify premium multiples; absent that, the position calls for heightened scrutiny over the next 2-3 quarters. Our recommendation is to maintain exposure for now but prepare for potential de-risking should growth not inflect as anticipated.
