Revenue Growth: NVIDIA's explosive revenue growth in 2024 (125.9% YoY, to $60.9B) and 2025 (114.2% YoY, to $130.5B) is a direct result of its dominant position in AI hardware, particularly datacenter GPUs scheduled to meet surging demand for generative AI and cloud compute. This acceleration represents a step-change versus the flat revenue in 2023, indicating a rapidly widening lead over competitors. The magnitude and sustainability of this growth suggest NVIDIA is not only extending its leadership in existing markets but also catalyzing new addressable opportunities, translating into sticky platform adoption and increasing barriers to entry for rivals.

Gross Profit Margin: Contribution margin rose dramatically to 72.7% in 2024 from 56.9% in 2023, remaining sustainably high thereafter (71%+ through 2029). This expansion reflects powerful operational leverage as much of NVIDIA’s incremental revenue falls through to the bottom line, enabled by industry-leading pricing power, software-driven ecosystem lock-in, and limited competition in high-performance GPU supply. These margin levels are rare in the semiconductor industry, evidencing a strong and defensible business moat centered on differentiated technology, a robust developer ecosystem, and supply chain mastery.

SG&A Expense Margin: SG&A margin shrank from 9% in 2023 to just 4.4% in 2024 and is projected to stabilize at 2.1%, underscoring exceptional operating leverage. Management’s discipline in containing SG&A despite hypergrowth demonstrates scalable operations and a highly efficient go-to-market model. This efficiency marks NVIDIA’s ability to expand rapidly without proportionate increases in sales or administrative costs, a signal of management excellence and a robust platform effect, which improves long-term profitability and provides downside resilience.

EBITDA Margin Stability: NVIDIA’s EBITDA margin soared from 22.2% in 2023 to 58.4% in 2024 and is set to remain above 66% thereafter, handily outpacing peers AMD and Intel, which operate at much lower margins (single-digit to 20% range historically). Compared to AMD and Intel’s 2023 EV/EBITDA multiples (57.1x and 22.5x vs. NVIDIA’s 81x), the premium valuation is justified by NVIDIA’s superior margin structure, platform leadership, and sustained growth trajectory. Persistent high profitability validates the company’s strategic position not just as a component supplier but as a foundational enabler of the AI and cloud computing ecosystem, reinforcing its appeal to long-term, quality-oriented investors.
