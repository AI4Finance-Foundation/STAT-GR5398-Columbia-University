Thesis Status: Thesis Confirmed

Key Takeaways:
- NVIDIA's most recent quarter reported revenue of $60.9B, reflecting a year-over-year growth of 125.9% and a major beat versus both historical comps and consensus.
- EPS surged to $1.21, more than triple last year's value and well ahead of expectations, with margins sharply expanding.
- Forward guidance was raised on both top and bottom line metrics, with management citing accelerating demand across Data Center (especially for generative AI) and ongoing supply chain execution.
- Recent EBITDA and profitability levels dramatically outpace peers AMD and Intel, cementing NVIDIA's competitive lead for the near term.
- Valuation (PE and EV/EBITDA) remains elevated on trailing metrics, but compression is occurring rapidly as forward estimates are revised higher following results.

Performance Analysis:
NVIDIA delivered a standout quarter, with revenues more than doubling from the prior year and exceeding even bullish Street forecasts. EPS growth (1.21 vs. 0.18 y/y) was fueled not just by top-line momentum but by significant margin expansion—EBITDA margin rose to 58.4% from last year’s 22.2%. Adjacent peers such as AMD and Intel continue to lag, cementing NVIDIA’s early mover advantage in AI and high-performance computing. Key metrics across the board—revenue, EPS, EBITDA—meaningfully outpaced consensus, reflecting both volume uplift and pricing power in core segments. The company’s EBITDA for the year was $35.6B, well above prior Street estimates and comparably higher than competitors, highlighting its dominance in the space.

Thesis Impact:
The core investment thesis—anchored on NVIDIA’s leadership in AI/accelerator hardware, significant software ecosystem advantages, and first-mover positioning—has been reinforced by these results. Management commentary on the call focused on robust demand visibility from cloud hyperscalers, significant backlog, and credible long-term growth in generative AI workloads. The sharp jump in contribution and EBITDA margins, combined with ongoing operational leverage (SG&A ratio dropping to 4.4%) directly validates assumptions around scale benefits and reinforces confidence in sustainable profitability. There were no negative surprises and guidance upgrades signal management's confidence in durable growth, thereby strengthening conviction in the original thesis. While high multiples remain, the pace of earnings growth is driving rapid multiple compression, lending support to continued upside in the 6-12 month view.

No material negative developments (M&A, regulatory, or competitive encroachment) have been identified since the last analysis. Management's tone is confident, and the supply chain commentary suggests risk of hardware shortages is being managed. The strong earnings print and guidance upgrade justify maintaining or increasing exposure to the name, particularly as forward multiples become less demanding in light of revised expectations.
