NVIDIA (NVDA) has experienced a dramatic transformation in its financial profile, driven by surging demand for its AI and data center products. Revenue is forecast to grow at a 68% CAGR through 2027, reaching nearly $250B, with EBITDA margins expanding from 22% in 2023 to over 70% by 2027—levels far above historic semiconductor averages.

Currently, NVIDIA trades at a 2024 PE of 51.6x, falling to 37.8x and 32.4x on 2026 and 2027 estimates, respectively. On an EV/EBITDA basis, NVDA’s 2024 multiple stands at 43.5x, declining to 31.4x by 2026. In comparison, AMD and Intel trade at lower multiples: AMD’s 2024 EV/EBITDA is 38.3x (falling to 47.8x in 2025), while Intel is at 106.9x (falling to 14.5x in 2025), though both lag NVIDIA significantly in margin and growth metrics. NVIDIA’s EBITDA is expected to outpace peers, rising from ~$36B in 2024 to $144B in 2026, compared to AMD’s $5–8B and Intel’s $1–47B.

Despite premium valuation, NVIDIA’s multiples are supported by its sector-leading growth, dominant position in AI accelerators, and expanding profitability. The pace of multiple compression reflects expectations of ongoing rapid earnings growth. However, valuation remains sensitive to any slowdown in AI or data center demand, competitive threats, or margin pressure.

Fair value assessment: While NVIDIA trades at a premium, its superior growth and profitability justify a higher multiple relative to peers. Using a 30–35x forward EV/EBITDA and EPS multiple on 2026–2027 estimates suggests the stock is currently near fair value, with upside dependent on continued outperformance and AI adoption. Any material growth deceleration could lead to a sharper re-rating. Investors should monitor execution and competitive dynamics closely.
