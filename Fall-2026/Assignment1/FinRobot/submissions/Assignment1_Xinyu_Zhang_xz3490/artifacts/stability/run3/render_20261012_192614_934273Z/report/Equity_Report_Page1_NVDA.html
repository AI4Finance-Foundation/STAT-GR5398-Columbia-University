Company Overview

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) is a global leader in accelerated computing, renowned for its pioneering role in graphics processing units (GPUs) and artificial intelligence (AI) hardware and software. The company’s business model centers on designing and selling high-performance semiconductors and related platforms, which are integral to gaming, data centers, professional visualization, and automotive markets. NVIDIA generates revenue primarily from selling GPUs and system-on-chip (SoC) products, complemented by software solutions and licensing.

NVIDIA’s product and service portfolio is anchored by its GPU platforms, notably the GeForce series for gaming, the NVIDIA RTX and Quadro lines for professional graphics, and the A100 and H100 GPUs for data centers and AI workloads. Its CUDA programming model and associated software stack enable developers to leverage NVIDIA hardware for advanced computing applications, further embedding NVIDIA into the AI, machine learning, and high-performance computing ecosystems. The company also offers solutions for autonomous vehicles and edge computing, reflecting its ongoing diversification efforts.

In terms of market position, NVIDIA is widely regarded as the dominant player in AI accelerators and data center GPUs, with a substantial lead over competitors such as AMD and Intel. This leadership is reflected in financial performance: NVIDIA’s revenues soared from $26.9 billion in FY2023 to $60.9 billion in FY2024, marking a dramatic 125.9% year-over-year increase driven by explosive demand for AI and cloud computing infrastructure. The company’s EBITDA margin expanded significantly from 22.2% in FY2023 to 58.4% in FY2024, and is projected to exceed 70% in coming years—a level far surpassing peers. For comparison, AMD and Intel’s 2024 EBITDA figures are $5.3 billion and $1.2 billion, respectively, versus NVIDIA’s $35.6 billion.

Looking ahead, NVIDIA’s financial outlook remains robust, with consensus estimates projecting revenues to more than quadruple from $26.9 billion in FY2023 to nearly $216 billion by FY2026, representing a CAGR of 68.3%. Margins are expected to remain strong, reflecting operational leverage and premium pricing power in AI and data center markets. The company’s valuation, as indicated by a forward PE ratio in the mid-30s and a declining EV/EBITDA multiple, suggests high investor confidence in its growth trajectory and profitability.

In summary, NVIDIA’s unique combination of technological leadership, expanding end markets, and stellar financial performance cements its position as a cornerstone of the global AI and computing ecosystem, with strong momentum for continued growth.

Investment Update

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) continues to deliver exceptional financial performance, driven by surging demand for its advanced GPUs and AI-focused solutions. In fiscal 2024, revenue more than doubled year-on-year to $60.9 billion, with expectations to more than double again in 2025 to $130.5 billion. This explosive topline growth is primarily fueled by the accelerating adoption of generative AI and large language models, which require massive compute power—an area where NVIDIA’s data center products are the clear market leader.

Profitability has expanded in tandem with revenue. Contribution margin reached 72.7% in 2024 and is projected to remain above 70% through 2027, reflecting strong pricing power and operational leverage. EBITDA margin jumped from 22.2% in 2023 to 58.4% in 2024 and is expected to climb above 70% by 2027, underscoring the scalability of NVIDIA’s business model. SG&A as a percentage of revenue has declined significantly, indicating disciplined cost management despite rapid growth.

Compared to peers, NVIDIA is outpacing both AMD and Intel in EBITDA growth. NVIDIA’s estimated 2027 EBITDA of $382 billion dwarfs AMD’s $8.6 billion and Intel’s $47.4 billion. While the company’s EV/EBITDA multiple remains elevated (31.4x for 2026E), this premium reflects NVIDIA’s dominant market position and superior growth trajectory.

Looking ahead, NVIDIA is well-positioned to sustain strong growth, supported by robust demand for AI infrastructure, continued innovation, and expanding applications across industries. While valuation remains high, the exceptional earnings momentum and widening competitive moat support a positive outlook for long-term investors.

Valuation

NVIDIA (NVDA) has experienced a dramatic transformation in its financial profile, driven by surging demand for its AI and data center products. Revenue is forecast to grow at a 68% CAGR through 2027, reaching nearly $250B, with EBITDA margins expanding from 22% in 2023 to over 70% by 2027—levels far above historic semiconductor averages.

Currently, NVIDIA trades at a 2024 PE of 51.6x, falling to 37.8x and 32.4x on 2026 and 2027 estimates, respectively. On an EV/EBITDA basis, NVDA’s 2024 multiple stands at 43.5x, declining to 31.4x by 2026. In comparison, AMD and Intel trade at lower multiples: AMD’s 2024 EV/EBITDA is 38.3x (falling to 47.8x in 2025), while Intel is at 106.9x (falling to 14.5x in 2025), though both lag NVIDIA significantly in margin and growth metrics. NVIDIA’s EBITDA is expected to outpace peers, rising from ~$36B in 2024 to $144B in 2026, compared to AMD’s $5–8B and Intel’s $1–47B.

Despite premium valuation, NVIDIA’s multiples are supported by its sector-leading growth, dominant position in AI accelerators, and expanding profitability. The pace of multiple compression reflects expectations of ongoing rapid earnings growth. However, valuation remains sensitive to any slowdown in AI or data center demand, competitive threats, or margin pressure.

Fair value assessment: While NVIDIA trades at a premium, its superior growth and profitability justify a higher multiple relative to peers. Using a 30–35x forward EV/EBITDA and EPS multiple on 2026–2027 estimates suggests the stock is currently near fair value, with upside dependent on continued outperformance and AI adoption. Any material growth deceleration could lead to a sharper re-rating. Investors should monitor execution and competitive dynamics closely.

Risks

  • ▪ Valuation Risk: NVIDIA’s PE and EV/EBITDA multiples are significantly higher than peers (e.g., 2024 EV/EBITDA: NVDA 43.5x vs. AMD 38.3x, INTC 106.9x; 2024 PE: NVDA ~51.6x), indicating premium pricing and potential for sharp correction if growth expectations are not met.
  • ▪ Growth Sustainability Risk: Revenue growth rates are expected to decelerate sharply after 2026E (from >100% CAGR in recent years to low single digits), raising concerns about whether current high valuation is justified by future performance.
  • ▪ Margin Compression Risk: While EBITDA margins are projected to expand (from 22.2% in 2023 to 73.5% in 2027E), any increase in competition, input costs, or R&D/SG&A spending could pressure margins below these optimistic forecasts.
  • ▪ Peer Competition Risk: Competitors such as AMD and Intel are also investing heavily; any major technological breakthrough or pricing action by peers could erode NVIDIA’s market share and profitability.
  • ▪ Execution/Operational Risk: The scale of projected growth (e.g., revenue from ~$26B in 2023 to ~$250B in 2027E) requires flawless execution in supply chain, product development, and go-to-market—any missteps could materially impact financial performance.
