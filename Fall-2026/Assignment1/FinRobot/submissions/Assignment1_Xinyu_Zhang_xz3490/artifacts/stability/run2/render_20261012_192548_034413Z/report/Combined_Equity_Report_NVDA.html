Company Overview

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) is a global leader in visual computing technology and artificial intelligence (AI) hardware and software. Founded in 1993, NVIDIA revolutionized the graphics processing unit (GPU) market with its invention of the GPU in 1999, which became the engine of modern computer graphics and, more recently, the backbone of AI computing. The company’s core business model centers around designing, developing, and selling high-performance GPUs, systems on a chip (SoCs), and related software. NVIDIA’s products are integral in diverse markets, including gaming, professional visualization, data centers, and automotive.

NVIDIA’s flagship product families include the GeForce GPUs for gaming, Quadro for professional graphics, and the data center-focused NVIDIA A100 and H100 GPUs, which dominate the AI and machine learning accelerator market. NVIDIA also provides high-performance computing solutions, networking products (Mellanox), and a robust software ecosystem including CUDA, its proprietary parallel computing platform, which has driven widespread adoption in AI research and enterprise applications.

NVIDIA’s market position is exceptionally strong, particularly in the data center segment, where it has emerged as the preferred hardware vendor for AI workloads. Its competitive advantage lies in its advanced GPU architectures and comprehensive software stack, which competitors have struggled to match. Compared to peers AMD and Intel, NVIDIA’s EBITDA and margins have surged dramatically in recent years, highlighting its operational leverage and leadership in high-growth areas.

Financially, NVIDIA has demonstrated explosive growth. Revenue soared from $26.9 billion in FY2023 to $60.9 billion in FY2024, and is projected to more than double to $130.5 billion in FY2025 and reach nearly $216 billion in FY2026—a remarkable CAGR of 68.3%. Contribution and EBITDA margins have also expanded, with EBITDA margin rising from 22.2% in 2023 to an estimated 73.5% by 2027, indicating both top-line momentum and disciplined cost management. Earnings per share (EPS) have rapidly increased, and while NVIDIA’s price-to-earnings (PE) and EV/EBITDA multiples remain high, they are trending downward as earnings scale, reflecting the market’s confidence in sustained growth.

In summary, NVIDIA’s robust business model, innovation leadership, and dominant market position—especially in AI and data center technologies—have translated into outstanding recent financial performance and position the company as a critical enabler of the AI-driven digital economy.

Investment Update

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) Investment Update

NVIDIA has delivered exceptional financial performance over the last three years, with a sharp acceleration in growth driven by surging demand for its AI and data center products. In fiscal 2024, revenue more than doubled year-over-year to $60.9 billion, up 125.9%, reflecting NVIDIA’s unique position as the leading provider of advanced GPUs for AI workloads. Looking ahead, revenue is projected to surpass $130 billion in 2025 and $215 billion by 2026, representing a remarkable revenue CAGR of 68.3% from 2022 to 2027E.

Profitability has scaled with revenue. EBITDA margins expanded from 22.2% in 2023 to 58.4% in 2024, and are expected to reach over 73% by 2027. EPS has increased sharply, from $0.18 in 2023 to $1.21 in 2024, and is forecast to grow to $5.88 by 2027. Cost discipline is evident, with SG&A margin declining to below 1% by 2027E.

NVIDIA’s growth is underpinned by its dominance in AI chips, with broad adoption across hyperscalers, cloud providers, and enterprises investing in generative AI and accelerated computing. Its product roadmap continues to outpace competitors, and the high efficiency of its operations supports exceptional profitability.

In peer comparison, NVIDIA’s EBITDA ($35.6 billion in 2024) far outstrips AMD and Intel, and its EV/EBITDA multiple is normalizing (43.5x in 2024, trending down to 31.4x by 2026). While the stock’s valuation remains premium, this is supported by industry-leading growth, margin expansion, and sustained earnings momentum.

Outlook: NVIDIA’s leadership in AI hardware and accelerating financials position it as a core long-term compounder in technology. While valuations are elevated, strong execution and sector tailwinds justify continued investor confidence.

Valuation

NVIDIA (NVDA) has experienced unprecedented growth, with revenue rising from $26.9 billion in 2022 to an estimated $215.9 billion by 2026, reflecting a remarkable CAGR of 68.3%. The company’s EBITDA margin is projected to expand from 22.2% in 2023 to over 70% by 2027, indicating remarkable operating leverage and efficiency, largely driven by its dominant position in the AI and data center GPU markets.

Currently, NVIDIA trades at a 2024E P/E of 51.6x, dropping to 39.9x in 2025E and 37.8x in 2026E. Its EV/EBITDA multiple, while elevated at 81.0x in 2023, is expected to decline to 43.5x in 2024 and further to 31.4x in 2026, as EBITDA more than quadruples over this period. In comparison, AMD’s 2025E EV/EBITDA is 47.8x and Intel’s is 14.5x, positioning NVIDIA above Intel and roughly in line with AMD on a forward basis, while its superior growth and margins arguably justify some premium.

NVIDIA’s exceptional growth, best-in-class margins, and clear technological leadership support its premium valuation. However, the current multiples already reflect high expectations and leave modest room for error, especially as growth normalizes (2025E-2027E revenue growth slows to single digits). Based on peer multiples and projected financials, fair value appears to be near current levels, as further multiple expansion is unlikely unless NVIDIA can sustain hypergrowth or further expand margins. Investors should monitor for any deceleration in AI demand or increased competition, which could pressure both growth rates and valuation multiples. Overall, NVIDIA remains a high-quality growth story, but its valuation is full and sensitive to execution.

Risks

Certainly. Based on the provided financial metrics and peer comparison data for NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA), here are 5 key investment risks:

  • ▪ Valuation Risk: NVIDIA’s current and projected PE and EV/EBITDA multiples are significantly higher than peers (e.g., 2023 EV/EBITDA: NVDA 81.0 vs. AMD 57.1, INTC 22.5), indicating premium valuation and potential vulnerability to market corrections if growth expectations are not met.
  • ▪ Revenue Growth Sustainability: The company shows extraordinary revenue growth in 2024 and 2025 (125.9% and 114.2% YoY), but this is expected to slow dramatically to single digits by 2026-2027, raising concerns about the ability to maintain high growth rates and justify current valuation levels.
  • ▪ Margin Compression Risk: While EBITDA margin is projected to expand (from 22.2% in 2023 to 73.5% in 2027), any increase in competition, operational costs, or R&D/Sales expenses could pressure margins, especially as SG&A margin is forecasted to decline uncommonly low.
  • ▪ Competitive Pressure: Peer data shows AMD and INTC are also investing and growing (e.g., rising EBITDA for AMD, recovering for INTC by 2027), suggesting risk of market share loss or price competition, particularly as the semiconductor/AI market matures.
  • ▪ Execution Risk on Forecasts: The financial projections for revenue, EBITDA, and margins are highly optimistic. Any operational missteps, supply chain constraints, regulatory changes, or delayed AI/semiconductor adoption could result in significant underperformance versus these aggressive forecasts.

This is far ahead of the more modest growth rates seen at AMD and Intel.

NVIDIA’s EBITDA trajectory is even more striking. EBITDA jumps from $11.4B in 2022 to $86.1B in 2025, dwarfing AMD’s $7.3B and Intel’s $14.4B in the same year. While its multiple is expected to moderate (to 31.4x by 2026), it remains above AMD and Intel, signaling a premium justified by superior growth and profitability.

In summary, NVIDIA outpaces AMD and Intel in revenue growth, profitability, and margin expansion, supported by its dominant position in AI and data center markets. However, its premium valuation increases expectations and pressure to deliver on growth forecasts, while peers may offer more value-oriented exposure but lack NVIDIA’s explosive momentum and margins.
