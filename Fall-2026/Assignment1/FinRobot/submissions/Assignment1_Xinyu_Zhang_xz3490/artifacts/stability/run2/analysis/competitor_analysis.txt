NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) is currently the clear performance leader among its primary peers, Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Intel (INTC), driven by explosive revenue and EBITDA growth. NVIDIA’s top-line expansion is remarkable, with revenue growing from $26.9B in 2022 to a projected $130.5B in 2025 and $215.9B in 2026, representing a CAGR of over 68%. This is far ahead of the more modest growth rates seen at AMD and Intel.

NVIDIA’s EBITDA trajectory is even more striking. EBITDA jumps from $11.4B in 2022 to $86.1B in 2025, dwarfing AMD’s $7.3B and Intel’s $14.4B in the same year. Moreover, NVIDIA’s EBITDA margin climbs from 22.2% in 2023 to an estimated 73.5% by 2027, indicating rising operational efficiency and strong pricing power. In contrast, AMD and Intel’s EBITDA margins and absolute levels are lower and grow at a much slower pace.

On valuation, NVIDIA’s EV/EBITDA ratio has historically been much higher than its peers, peaking at 81.0x in 2023, reflecting market expectations for continued rapid growth. While its multiple is expected to moderate (to 31.4x by 2026), it remains above AMD and Intel, signaling a premium justified by superior growth and profitability.

In summary, NVIDIA outpaces AMD and Intel in revenue growth, profitability, and margin expansion, supported by its dominant position in AI and data center markets. However, its premium valuation increases expectations and pressure to deliver on growth forecasts, while peers may offer more value-oriented exposure but lack NVIDIA’s explosive momentum and margins.
