Company Overview

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) is a leading global provider of graphics processing units (GPUs), system-on-chip units (SoCs), and related software solutions. The company’s business model is primarily centered on designing, developing, and selling high-performance computing hardware and software platforms that accelerate computing-intensive tasks. NVIDIA serves diverse end markets including gaming, professional visualization, data centers, and automotive systems, but its recent performance has been driven predominantly by explosive demand in artificial intelligence (AI) and data center applications.

NVIDIA’s product portfolio includes GeForce GPUs for gaming, Quadro and RTX GPUs for professional visualization, and the powerful data center platform built around its A100, H100, and upcoming Blackwell GPU architectures. The company also offers AI and deep learning software frameworks (e.g., CUDA and TensorRT), which have become industry standards for AI developers. Its automotive solutions power infotainment and autonomous driving systems in modern vehicles. NVIDIA’s business model is asset-light; it outsources manufacturing while focusing on R&D and design, enabling high margins and rapid innovation cycles.

Over the past three years, NVIDIA has cemented a dominant market position, particularly in AI accelerators—a segment critical to generative AI, large language models, and hyperscale cloud computing. Its data center revenues have surged due to widespread adoption of NVIDIA GPUs in AI training and inference workloads by major technology companies and cloud providers. This leadership is reflected in its financial performance: revenue soared from $26.9 billion in 2023 to $60.9 billion in 2024, with guidance of $130.5 billion in 2025 and $215.9 billion in 2026, representing a remarkable 68.3% CAGR from 2023-2026. Contribution and EBITDA margins have expanded sharply, with EBITDA margin expected to exceed 70% by 2027, indicating significant operating leverage.

Compared to peers such as AMD and Intel, NVIDIA’s EBITDA and margins are substantially higher, underscoring its unique positioning and pricing power in the high-end accelerator market. Peer EV/EBITDA multiples also highlight the premium investors are willing to pay for NVIDIA’s growth and profitability profile.

In summary, NVIDIA’s robust product pipeline, entrenched ecosystem, and leadership in AI computing have positioned it as the preeminent beneficiary of the current AI-driven technology cycle. The company’s recent and projected financial growth, sustained margin expansion, and market dominance underscore its role as a critical enabler of next-generation computing.

Investment Update

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) – Investment Update

NVIDIA has delivered exceptional financial performance over the past three years, cementing its position as the leading provider of AI and accelerated computing solutions. Revenue reached $60.9 billion in FY2024, a year-on-year increase of 125.9%, and is projected to more than double again in FY2025 to $130.5 billion. This rapid top-line expansion is being driven by surging demand for NVIDIA’s data center GPUs and AI platforms, as cloud providers, enterprises, and governments ramp up investments in generative AI and high-performance computing.

Profitability has scaled even faster. EBITDA jumped from $6.0 billion in FY2023 to $35.6 billion in FY2024, and is forecast to reach $86.1 billion in FY2025, equating to a margin of 66.0%. Contribution margin has expanded sharply, reflecting strong operating leverage and disciplined cost control, while SG&A as a percentage of revenue is set to decline further to just 2.7% in FY2025. EPS is also on a steep upward trajectory, expected to rise from $1.21 in FY2024 to $2.97 in FY2025 and $4.93 in FY2026.

Compared to peers, NVIDIA’s EBITDA outpaces both AMD and Intel by a wide margin, and the company’s EV/EBITDA multiple, while still elevated at 33.8x for FY2025, is quickly normalizing as earnings scale. With a three-year revenue CAGR of 68.3% and robust margin expansion, NVIDIA’s long-term outlook remains highly favorable. The company is well positioned to benefit from sustained AI infrastructure buildout, new product launches, and ecosystem expansion, supporting continued growth and shareholder value creation.

Valuation

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) is experiencing unprecedented growth, driven by surging demand for its AI and data center products. Revenue is projected to increase from $26.9 billion in FY2023 to $130.5 billion in FY2025 and $215.9 billion in FY2026, representing a remarkable 68% CAGR over the next three years. This explosive topline growth is translating into significant margin expansion, with EBITDA margin rising from 22.2% in FY2023 to an estimated 70.5% in FY2025 and 72% in FY2026. EPS is expected to grow from $0.18 in FY2023 to $5.23 in FY2025 and $5.60 in FY2026.

Currently, NVIDIA trades at a forward PE of 35.9x (2025E) and 34.1x (2026E), which is elevated versus the broader semiconductor sector but reasonable given its growth profile. On an EV/EBITDA basis, NVIDIA’s multiple compresses rapidly from 81.0x in 2023 to 43.5x in 2024 and 33.8x in 2025, moving closer to, but still above, peers Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Intel (INTC) whose 2025E EV/EBITDA are 47.8x and 14.5x, respectively. NVIDIA’s premium valuation is justified by its superior scale, margin profile, and growth outlook—its 2025E EBITDA is more than 10x that of AMD and over 6x Intel.

While the current multiples appear rich on an absolute basis, NVIDIA’s dominant position in the AI hardware market and its ability to maintain high margins and outsized growth support a fair value at the upper end of the semiconductor peer group. As growth normalizes post-2026, further multiple compression is likely, but the stock’s valuation remains supported by fundamental outperformance and industry leadership. On balance, NVIDIA appears fairly valued at current levels relative to its growth and profitability, though the margin for error is narrowing as expectations rise.
