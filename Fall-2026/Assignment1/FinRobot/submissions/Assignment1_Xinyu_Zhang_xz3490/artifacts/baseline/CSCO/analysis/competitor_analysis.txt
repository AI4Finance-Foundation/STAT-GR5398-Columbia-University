Cisco Systems, Inc. (CSCO) operates in the networking and IT infrastructure sector, competing primarily with technology giants such as Apple Inc. (AAPL) and NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA). 

Financially, Cisco's recent performance has been steady but lags its peers in both scale and growth trajectory. Cisco’s revenue for FY2023 was $56.9 billion, a 10.6% increase from the previous year. However, 2024 saw a revenue decline of 5.6%, with projections indicating moderate growth resuming afterward (5.3% in 2025E). In contrast, both Apple and NVIDIA are on steeper upward curves, with NVIDIA in particular showing explosive EBITDA expansion (projected to reach $382B by 2027 vs. Cisco’s $21B). 

Cisco maintains a healthy contribution margin (64.7% in 2024A, trending up), indicating strong core profitability and operational efficiency. Its EBITDA margin, however, has trended downward recently (29.3% in 2024A, from 32.6% in 2022A), before rebounding sharply in forecasts (expected to exceed 44% by 2026E). This margin remains below Apple’s, but is competitive with NVIDIA’s volatile margins, which are closely tied to its rapid growth and R&D investments.

Cisco’s valuation, as measured by EV/EBITDA, remains significantly lower than its peers, suggesting the market assigns it a lower growth premium. For 2024, Cisco’s EV/EBITDA is 13.8x, compared to Apple’s 26.6x and NVIDIA’s 43.5x. This reflects Cisco’s slower growth and more mature market positioning, but also suggests a value opportunity relative to its technology peers.

In summary, Cisco is a stable, margin-rich player with moderate growth prospects, but lacks the high-growth profile and market enthusiasm enjoyed by Apple and especially NVIDIA. Its lower valuation and steady margins make it attractive for investors seeking stability rather than the aggressive growth and volatility of its peers.
