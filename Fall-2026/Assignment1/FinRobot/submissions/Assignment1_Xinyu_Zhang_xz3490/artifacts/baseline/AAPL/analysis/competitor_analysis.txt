Apple Inc. (AAPL) remains a dominant force in the technology sector, consistently outperforming peers like Cisco Systems (CSCO) and Nvidia (NVDA) in both scale and profitability. Over 2021-2025, Apple’s revenue exhibits steady growth (CAGR 3.3%), with robust contribution and EBITDA margins that are markedly higher than its peers. By 2025, Apple’s EBITDA is projected to reach $144.4 billion, far surpassing Cisco ($15.3 billion) and Nvidia ($86.1 billion). This scale advantage is reflected in Apple’s margin profile; its EBITDA margin rises from 33.7% in 2021 to an estimated 34.7% in 2025 and is forecasted to exceed 40% thereafter—well above Cisco and more comparable to Nvidia’s future projections as its AI business accelerates.

Apple’s operating efficiency is highlighted by a declining SG&A margin (from 6.0% in 2021 to 5.1% in 2027E), indicating improved cost control relative to revenue. Its contribution margin also expands steadily, reflecting pricing power and a premium product mix. While Apple’s revenue growth is modest compared to Nvidia (which is experiencing explosive growth due to surging AI demand), Apple’s business is more mature and diversified, with far less volatility.

On valuation, Apple's EV/EBITDA multiples (21.6x in 2023, 26.9x in 2025) are higher than Cisco's (12.1x in 2023, 19.1x in 2025) but significantly lower than Nvidia’s (81.0x in 2023, 33.8x in 2025), suggesting Apple is viewed as a stable, premium asset but not in the same high-growth category as Nvidia.

In summary, Apple leads on scale, profitability, and operational discipline among established tech peers, while Nvidia’s growth potential is reflected in its higher valuation multiples. Cisco, by contrast, remains steady but lacks the growth and margin expansion of either Apple or Nvidia. Apple’s consistent performance, high margins, and strong cost discipline reinforce its competitive edge in the industry.
