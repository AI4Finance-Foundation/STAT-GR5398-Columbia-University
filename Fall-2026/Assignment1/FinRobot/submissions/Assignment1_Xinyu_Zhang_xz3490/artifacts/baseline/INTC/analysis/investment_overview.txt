Investment Update: Intel Corporation (INTC)

Recent Financial Performance  
Intel has faced significant headwinds over the past three years. Revenues fell from $79.0 billion in 2021 to $54.2 billion in 2023, representing a compound annual decline of roughly 9.6%. EBITDA dropped sharply from $33.9 billion in 2021 to $1.2 billion in 2024, with margins compressing from 42.9% to just 2.3% in the same period. The company posted negative EPS in 2024 (-$4.38), reflecting both top-line pressure and substantial restructuring costs. While SG&A spending as a percentage of revenue has declined, operational leverage remains under pressure.

Growth Drivers  
Intel is navigating a complex turnaround as it invests in next-generation manufacturing and product leadership. Key growth drivers include new product launches in AI and server chips, customer wins in foundry services, and efficiency efforts that are expected to gradually restore margins. Cost containment is evident in projected SG&A margin improvements (falling to 7.2% by 2027), while contribution margin is forecast to recover to 37.8%. Revenue growth is expected to return in 2025, with a 5-6% annual uptick through 2027 as Intel stabilizes its core businesses and leverages new technology cycles.

Outlook  
While Intel’s near-term financials remain challenged, consensus expects a gradual recovery. EBITDA is projected to rebound to $15.3 billion in 2025 and $18.7 billion by 2027, with margin expansion to 30.6%. However, profitability (EPS) is expected to remain negative through at least 2027, reflecting ongoing investments and competitive pressures. Relative to peers, Intel’s 2025E EV/EBITDA (14.5x) is in line with AMD (47.8x) and below Nvidia (33.8x), but its near-term valuation is volatile due to depressed earnings.

In summary, Intel is in a transitional phase: a return to growth is expected, but execution risk remains high. Investors should monitor progress on manufacturing, margin recovery, and successful product ramp-ups as key catalysts for future performance.
