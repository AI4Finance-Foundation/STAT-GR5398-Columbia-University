Intel Corporation (INTC) has faced substantial headwinds compared to its main semiconductor peers, AMD and NVIDIA, over the past several years. Intel’s revenue has declined at a -9.6% CAGR from 2021 to 2024, falling from $79 billion in 2021 to an estimated $53 billion in 2024. This contrasts sharply with NVIDIA, whose revenues and profitability have surged, driven by leadership in AI and accelerated computing, and with AMD, which has delivered steady growth in both server and client markets.

Intel’s profitability metrics highlight its challenging market position. Contribution margin fell from 55.4% in 2021 to just 32.7% in 2024, while EBITDA margin plummeted from 42.9% to 2.3% over the same period. In contrast, NVIDIA’s EBITDA is projected to reach $35.6 billion in 2024, dwarfing Intel’s $1.2 billion, and AMD remains more stable with $5.3 billion. Intel’s negative and deteriorating EPS, with a 2024 estimate of -$4.38, further underscores its struggles, while both AMD and NVIDIA maintain positive earnings.

Valuation also reflects Intel’s lagging performance. Its EV/EBITDA ratio spikes to 106.9x in 2024, indicating low investor confidence and limited profit generation relative to enterprise value. AMD and NVIDIA, while expensive by historical standards, trade at significantly lower multiples (38.3x and 43.5x, respectively), justified by their growth prospects and market momentum.

Looking forward, Intel’s restructuring and investments may gradually improve margins and EBITDA, but it remains at a competitive disadvantage in innovation, product leadership, and financial performance. For now, Intel trails both AMD and NVIDIA in growth, profitability, and market confidence.
