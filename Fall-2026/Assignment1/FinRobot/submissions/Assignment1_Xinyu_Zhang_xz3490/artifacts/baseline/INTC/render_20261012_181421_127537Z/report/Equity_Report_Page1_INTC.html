Company Overview

Intel Corporation (NASDAQ: INTC) is a global leader in semiconductor design and manufacturing, historically best known for its dominant position in the x86 microprocessor market, which powers the majority of personal computers and data centers worldwide. Intel’s business model is vertically integrated: the company designs, manufactures, and sells a wide range of semiconductor products, leveraging its own fabrication facilities (“fabs”) to maintain control over quality and supply.

Intel’s product portfolio encompasses central processing units (CPUs) for desktops, laptops, and servers, as well as graphics processing units (GPUs), networking components, memory products, and emerging solutions in artificial intelligence (AI), edge computing, and autonomous systems. The company’s core revenue historically came from its Client Computing Group (PC CPUs), but it is actively investing in its Data Center and AI Group, Foundry Services (manufacturing chips for third parties), and new markets such as automotive (Mobileye) and programmable solutions (FPGAs).

Despite its strong legacy, Intel has faced significant challenges in recent years. The company’s revenue has declined sharply, from $79.0 billion in 2021 to $54.2 billion in 2023, with a compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) of -9.6%. Profitability has also deteriorated: EBITDA fell from $33.9 billion in 2021 to $1.2 billion in 2024, with margins compressed from 42.9% to just 2.3% during this period. This underperformance is largely attributed to competitive pressures from rivals like AMD and NVIDIA, delays in advanced manufacturing process nodes, market share erosion, and cyclical weakness in the PC and server markets.

Looking forward, Intel is focused on regaining technology leadership through aggressive investment in advanced manufacturing (notably its next-generation process nodes), expanding its foundry business to serve external customers, and capturing growth in AI and automotive sectors. While near-term financials remain under pressure—2024 and 2025 earnings per share (EPS) are negative and revenue growth is modest—the company projects a return to growth and margin recovery by 2025-2027, driven by renewed product competitiveness and operational efficiency.

In comparison to its peers, Intel’s EBITDA and valuation multiples have lagged, reflecting investor concerns but also potential upside if its turnaround efforts succeed. The company’s market position remains substantial, but Intel’s future depends on successful execution of its strategic roadmap amid fierce industry competition and rapid technological change.

Investment Update

Investment Update: Intel Corporation (INTC)

Recent Financial Performance

Intel has faced significant headwinds over the past three years. Revenues fell from $79.0 billion in 2021 to $54.2 billion in 2023, representing a compound annual decline of roughly 9.6%. EBITDA dropped sharply from $33.9 billion in 2021 to $1.2 billion in 2024, with margins compressing from 42.9% to just 2.3% in the same period. The company posted negative EPS in 2024 (-$4.38), reflecting both top-line pressure and substantial restructuring costs. While SG&A spending as a percentage of revenue has declined, operational leverage remains under pressure.

Growth Drivers

Intel is navigating a complex turnaround as it invests in next-generation manufacturing and product leadership. Key growth drivers include new product launches in AI and server chips, customer wins in foundry services, and efficiency efforts that are expected to gradually restore margins. Cost containment is evident in projected SG&A margin improvements (falling to 7.2% by 2027), while contribution margin is forecast to recover to 37.8%. Revenue growth is expected to return in 2025, with a 5-6% annual uptick through 2027 as Intel stabilizes its core businesses and leverages new technology cycles.

Outlook

While Intel’s near-term financials remain challenged, consensus expects a gradual recovery. EBITDA is projected to rebound to $15.3 billion in 2025 and $18.7 billion by 2027, with margin expansion to 30.6%. However, profitability (EPS) is expected to remain negative through at least 2027, reflecting ongoing investments and competitive pressures. Relative to peers, Intel’s 2025E EV/EBITDA (14.5x) is in line with AMD (47.8x) and below Nvidia (33.8x), but its near-term valuation is volatile due to depressed earnings.

In summary, Intel is in a transitional phase: a return to growth is expected, but execution risk remains high. Investors should monitor progress on manufacturing, margin recovery, and successful product ramp-ups as key catalysts for future performance.

Valuation

Intel Corporation (INTC) has experienced significant revenue and profitability challenges in recent years. Revenue declined from $79 billion in 2021 to $54.2 billion in 2023, with a negative CAGR of -9.6%. Contribution and EBITDA margins compressed sharply over this period, with EBITDA margin dropping from 42.9% in 2021 to just 2.3% in 2024. Earnings per share (EPS) followed suit, turning negative in 2024 and projected to remain slightly negative through 2027, reflecting ongoing operational pressures and heavy investment.

Valuation metrics reflect these headwinds. Intel’s trailing PE ratio jumped to 125.6x in 2023 due to collapsing earnings, and forward PE ratios are not meaningful given negative earnings. On an EV/EBITDA basis, Intel traded at 7.1x in 2021 and 6.5x in 2022, but this escalated to 22.5x in 2023 and an extreme 106.9x in 2024 (driven by a trough in EBITDA). However, the metric normalizes to 14.5x by 2025, assuming management delivers on margin recovery.

Against peers, Intel’s valuation currently sits above AMD (EV/EBITDA 2024E: 38.3x) and NVDA (43.5x), but this is distorted by Intel’s cyclical trough in profitability. By 2025, Intel’s 14.5x EV/EBITDA is at a discount to AMD (47.8x) and NVDA (33.8x), suggesting the market expects only a partial recovery and remains cautious on Intel’s competitive position.

In summary, while Intel trades at a premium on near-term metrics due to depressed EBITDA, its 2025 EV/EBITDA suggests a discount to peers. Given ongoing challenges in earnings growth, margin compression, and negative EPS outlook, the fair value hinges on successful business turnaround. Barring a major shift in fundamentals, Intel appears fairly valued to slightly undervalued relative to peers on normalized forward metrics, but its near-term risk profile remains elevated.

Risks

  • ▪ Sustained Revenue Decline: Intel's revenue has contracted at a -9.6% CAGR from 2021 to 2024, with continued declines forecast through 2025, raising concerns about long-term demand, competitive positioning, and market share loss.
  • ▪ Profitability Pressure: EBITDA margin has plummeted from 42.9% (2021) to a low of 2.3% (2024), with only a gradual recovery projected, highlighting risks around cost control, operational efficiency, and pricing power.
  • ▪ Earnings Volatility and Negative EPS: Earnings per share have dropped sharply, moving negative in 2024 (EPS: -$4.38) with continued losses expected through 2027, undermining investor confidence and dividend sustainability.
  • ▪ Competitive Disadvantage: Intel's EBITDA and EV/EBITDA metrics lag key peers (notably NVIDIA and, in several years, AMD), indicating weaker operating leverage and market position amid intensifying industry competition.
  • ▪ Valuation Risk: Extreme volatility in the PE ratio (e.g., -614.6 in 2025) and a spike in EV/EBITDA (106.9x in 2024) suggest the stock could be overvalued relative to deteriorating fundamentals, exposing investors to significant downside if expectations are not met.
