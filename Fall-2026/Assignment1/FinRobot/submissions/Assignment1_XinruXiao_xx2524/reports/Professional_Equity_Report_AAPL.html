Company Overview

Apple Inc. is a leading global technology company headquartered in Cupertino, California, operating within the consumer electronics sector. Established in 1976, Apple has built a robust business model centered on the design, manufacture, and sale of a wide array of electronic devices and digital services. Its core hardware products include the iPhone smartphone, Mac computers, and iPad tablets, complemented by a diverse portfolio of wearables and accessories such as AirPods, Apple Watch, Apple TV, Beats products, and HomePod speakers.

Beyond hardware, Apple has developed a comprehensive ecosystem of services that enhance customer engagement and drive recurring revenue. The company offers support and cloud services, as well as a suite of digital platforms, most notably the App Store. The App Store serves as a central marketplace for applications and digital content, including music, e-books, games, videos, and podcasts. Apple also generates revenue from advertising through its own platforms and third-party licensing agreements.

Subscription-based services are a significant part of Apple’s strategy, with offerings such as Apple Music, Apple TV+, Apple Arcade, Apple Fitness+, and Apple News+ catering to a broad range of consumer interests. Financial services, including the Apple Card and Apple Pay, further diversify the company’s revenue streams. Apple also licenses its intellectual property and serves a wide customer base, ranging from individual consumers to businesses and institutions in education, corporate, and government sectors.

Apple’s products reach customers through a multi-channel distribution network, which includes its own retail stores and online platforms, direct sales teams, and partnerships with mobile carriers, wholesalers, retailers, and authorized resellers.

In terms of recent financial performance, Apple reported revenue of $416.2 billion in 2025A, up from $391.0 billion in 2024A and $383.3 billion in 2023A. The company’s historical revenue CAGR from 2021A to 2025A was 3.3%. Contribution margin improved from 41.8% in 2021A to 46.9% in 2025A, while EBITDA rose from $123.1 billion in 2021A to $144.4 billion in 2025A, with EBITDA margin increasing from 33.7% to 34.7% over the same period. Apple’s EPS reached 7.49 in 2025A. The company also maintained a strong balance sheet, with cash and cash equivalents of $35.9 billion and net debt of $76.4 billion in 2025A.

Apple’s market position is reinforced by its integrated ecosystem, strong brand loyalty, and consistent financial performance, making it a dominant force in the global technology and consumer electronics industry.

This represents a historical revenue CAGR of 3.3% from 2021A to 2025A. After a slight revenue decline in 2023A, Apple rebounded with growth of 2.0% in 2024A and 6.4% in 2025A. Contribution margin improved steadily, reaching 46.9% in 2025A, up from 41.8% in 2021A, reflecting ongoing operational efficiencies and a favorable shift toward higher-margin services and products.

EBITDA also grew consistently, from $123.1 billion in 2021A to $144.4 billion in 2025A, with EBITDA margin expanding from 33.7% to 34.7% over the same period. Earnings per share (EPS) increased from $5.67 in 2021A to $7.49 in 2025A, demonstrating Apple’s ability to translate top-line growth and margin expansion into shareholder value. The company’s net debt position improved, declining from $101.6 billion in 2021A to $76.4 billion in 2025A, supported by robust cash generation.

Looking ahead, Apple’s own forecasts anticipate continued momentum. Revenue is expected to reach $481.7 billion by 2028E, with contribution margin rising to 49.9%. EBITDA is projected to grow to $215.8 billion in 2028E, and EBITDA margin is forecast to expand to 44.8%. EPS is estimated to increase to $8.93 in 2028E. These forecasts reflect Apple’s ongoing focus on expanding its ecosystem, growing its services and subscription businesses, and maintaining innovation across hardware and software.

Overall, Apple’s recent results highlight resilient demand, operational discipline, and a strong outlook driven by both product and services growth. The company’s improving margins and earnings, alongside a strengthening balance sheet, position it well for continued value creation.

Its multi-channel distribution strategy and integration of hardware, software, and services have enabled Apple to maintain strong financial performance and brand loyalty.

Comparing Apple to key technology peers Cisco Systems (CSCO) and Microsoft (MSFT), Apple consistently delivers higher EBITDA in the historical period. In 2022A, Apple reported EBITDA of $133.1 billion, significantly outpacing Cisco’s $16.8 billion and Microsoft’s $100.2 billion. This trend continues through 2025A, with Apple reaching $144.4 billion in EBITDA, compared to Cisco’s $15.3 billion and Microsoft’s $160.2 billion. While Microsoft surpasses Apple in EBITDA by 2025A, Apple maintains a substantial lead over Cisco.

Apple’s historical revenue CAGR from 2021A to 2025A is 3.3%, reflecting steady growth in a mature market. The company’s EBITDA margin has remained robust, increasing from 33.7% in 2021A to 34.7% in 2025A, and is forecasted to rise further in subsequent years. Apple’s EV/EBITDA multiple has also trended higher than Cisco’s, reaching 26.9699 in 2025A versus Cisco’s 19.1074, and is comparable to Microsoft’s 23.5956 in the same year, indicating strong investor confidence and premium valuation.

In summary, Apple’s scale, profitability, and integrated ecosystem position it as a leader among technology peers. While Microsoft is closing the EBITDA gap, Apple’s diversified revenue streams and premium market positioning continue to differentiate it from both Cisco and Microsoft.

Risk Factors

  • ‣ Product Concentration Risk: Apple’s revenue is heavily dependent on a limited number of flagship products, such as the iPhone, Mac, and iPad, making the company vulnerable to shifts in consumer preferences or technological disruption affecting these categories.
  • ‣ Margin Pressure: Although Apple’s EBITDA margin increased from 33.7% in 2021A to 34.7% in 2025A, any rise in cost of operations or SG&A expenses could negatively impact profitability, especially if revenue growth slows or competition intensifies.
  • ‣ Revenue Growth Volatility: Apple experienced negative revenue growth in 2023A (-2.8%) and relatively modest growth in 2024A (2.0%), highlighting exposure to cyclical demand, macroeconomic factors, or market saturation.
  • ‣ High Net Debt Levels: Despite a reduction from $101.6 billion in 2021A to $76.4 billion in 2025A, Apple maintains significant net debt, which could constrain financial flexibility in adverse market conditions or during periods of increased investment needs.
  • ‣ Regulatory and Platform Risks: Apple’s reliance on the App Store and digital services exposes it to potential regulatory scrutiny and changes in digital platform policies, which could impact service revenues and profitability.
