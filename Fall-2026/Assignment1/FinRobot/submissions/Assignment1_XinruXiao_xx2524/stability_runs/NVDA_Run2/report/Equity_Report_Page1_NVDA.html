Company Overview

NVIDIA Corporation is a leading provider of advanced graphics, computational, and networking solutions, serving markets across the United States, Taiwan, China, and internationally. Founded in 1993 and headquartered in Santa Clara, California, the company operates primarily in the technology sector within the semiconductor industry. Under the leadership of CEO Jensen Huang, NVIDIA has established itself as a key innovator in both consumer and enterprise computing.

NVIDIA’s business model is structured around two main segments: Graphics and Compute & Networking. The Graphics division is anchored by GeForce GPUs, which are central to PC gaming and personal computing. This segment also includes the GeForce NOW cloud gaming service, Quadro and NVIDIA RTX GPUs for professional visualization, vGPU software for cloud-based visual computing, automotive infotainment platforms, and the Omniverse software suite for 3D design and virtual world creation.

The Compute & Networking segment is a cornerstone for artificial intelligence (AI), high-performance computing (HPC), and accelerated data center platforms. It incorporates Mellanox networking and interconnect solutions, automotive AI Cockpit technologies, and autonomous driving development through strategic agreements. This segment also produces cryptocurrency mining processors, Jetson platforms for robotics and embedded applications, and enterprise AI software such as NVIDIA AI Enterprise. NVIDIA’s solutions are widely used across gaming, professional visualization, data center, and automotive industries.

The company distributes its products through a broad ecosystem, including original equipment and device manufacturers, system integrators, add-in board makers, retail channels, software vendors, internet and cloud service providers, automotive manufacturers and suppliers, mapping firms, and technology startups. NVIDIA also maintains a notable strategic partnership with Kroger Co.

In terms of recent performance, NVIDIA has demonstrated exceptional growth. Revenue increased from $26.9 billion in 2022A to $215.9 billion in 2026A, representing a historical CAGR of 68.3% from 2022A to 2026A. The company’s EBITDA rose from $11.4 billion in 2022A to $144.6 billion in 2026A, with EBITDA margins expanding from 42.2% in 2022A to 66.9% in 2026A. Contribution margins and EPS have also shown significant improvement over this period. NVIDIA’s financial strength is further reflected in its declining net debt, which decreased from $9.8 billion in 2022A to $0.8 billion in 2026A. These results underscore NVIDIA’s strong market position and its ability to capitalize on the growing demand for AI, gaming, and data center solutions.

Investment Update

NVIDIA Corporation has demonstrated exceptional financial performance over the past several years, driven by its leadership in advanced graphics, AI, and data center solutions. In the most recent reported period, 2026A, NVIDIA achieved revenue of $215.94 billion, a substantial increase from $130.50 billion in 2025A and $60.92 billion in 2024A. This translates to a historical revenue CAGR of 68.3% from 2022A to 2026A, underscoring the company’s rapid expansion.

Profitability has also surged. EBITDA reached $144.55 billion in 2026A, up from $86.14 billion in 2025A and $35.58 billion in 2024A. EBITDA margin improved to 66.9% in 2026A, reflecting strong operating leverage and robust demand for NVIDIA’s high-margin data center and AI platforms. Contribution margin remained high at 71.1% in 2026A. EPS climbed to $4.93 in 2026A, compared to $2.97 in 2025A and $1.21 in 2024A.

Looking ahead, NVIDIA’s Financial Metrics forecast continued, though more moderate, growth. Revenue is expected to reach $226.73 billion in 2027E, $240.34 billion in 2028E, and $249.95 billion in 2029E, with annual growth rates of 5.0%, 6.0%, and 4.0%, respectively. EBITDA margins are forecast to further expand, reaching 73.5% by 2029E. EPS is projected to rise to $5.88 by 2029E.

NVIDIA’s growth is fueled by its dominant position in AI, high-performance computing, and accelerated data center platforms, as well as ongoing innovation in graphics and networking. The company’s broad ecosystem and strategic partnerships, such as with Kroger Co., support its diversified revenue streams across gaming, professional visualization, data center, and automotive markets.

In summary, NVIDIA’s recent actual results highlight extraordinary growth and profitability, with forecasts indicating sustained momentum, albeit at a more normalized pace. The company’s strong financial position and leadership in key technology sectors underpin a positive outlook.

Recent News Summary

NVIDIA Corporation has recently been in the spotlight due to several significant developments. Most notably, the company announced a historic $150 billion stock buyback plan, as reported on October 12, 2026. This massive repurchase program underscores NVIDIA’s confidence in its long-term growth prospects and is likely to be viewed positively by investors, as it signals management’s belief that the stock is undervalued and could provide support to the share price.

Despite this announcement, NVIDIA shares experienced a 1.3% decline, with the stock seeing a more pronounced dip than the broader market in recent trading sessions. Several news sources attribute this weakness to broader market volatility and sector-specific pressures, rather than company-specific issues. Additionally, the chip sector as a whole has been mixed, with major players like Intel, AMD, and Micron also experiencing fluctuations after the worst week in a month for chip stocks.

Investor sentiment remains divided. Some analysts argue that NVIDIA’s stock may be 27% undervalued despite reaching record highs, highlighting the company’s rapid growth and strong fundamentals. Others point to increased competition, noting that a private AI chip startup has outperformed NVIDIA’s stock gains recently, which could signal rising challenges in the AI and semiconductor space.

NVIDIA’s diverse business model—spanning gaming, professional visualization, data centers, automotive, and AI—continues to drive robust growth. The company’s strategic moves, such as the buyback and ongoing expansion in AI and data center markets, reinforce its leadership position. However, heightened competition and market volatility remain key risks for investors to monitor. Overall, the buyback plan and growth trajectory may provide long-term support, but near-term stock performance could remain volatile.

Valuation

NVIDIA Corporation’s valuation metrics reflect its rapid transformation and dominant position in the semiconductor industry, particularly in AI and data center solutions. As of 2026A, NVIDIA reported an EBITDA of $144.55 billion and an EBITDA margin of 66.9%, significantly higher than its closest peers. For comparison, Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Intel (INTC) reported 2025 EBITDA of $7.28 billion and $14.35 billion, respectively, while NVIDIA’s 2025 EBITDA was $86.14 billion. This highlights NVIDIA’s exceptional scale and profitability.

NVIDIA’s EV/EBITDA multiple has moderated from a peak of 81.05x in 2023A to 43.45x in 2024A, 33.78x in 2025A, and 31.43x in 2026A. These multiples remain above those of AMD (38.27x in 2024A, 47.85x in 2025A) and Intel (14.50x in 2025A), reflecting the market’s premium for NVIDIA’s superior growth and margins. Notably, NVIDIA’s historical revenue CAGR from 2022A to 2026A was 68.3%, underscoring its explosive expansion, while EBITDA margins have consistently exceeded 58% since 2024A.

Looking ahead, NVIDIA’s own forecasts anticipate continued revenue and EBITDA growth, with 2027E EBITDA expected at $159.85 billion and a margin of 70.5%. While revenue growth rates are forecast to moderate (5.0% in 2027E, 6.0% in 2028E, 4.0% in 2029E), profitability remains robust, with EBITDA margins projected to rise further.

In summary, NVIDIA trades at a premium to peers on EV/EBITDA, justified by its industry-leading growth, scale, and profitability. The company’s fair value appears supported by its strong financial performance and outlook, though the premium valuation reflects high market expectations for sustained leadership in AI and accelerated computing.

Risks

  • ▪ Revenue Growth Deceleration: After exceptionally high historical revenue growth (68.3% CAGR from 2022A to 2026A), forecasted revenue growth slows significantly to single digits (5.0% in 2027E, 6.0% in 2028E, and 4.0% in 2029E), indicating potential risk of growth normalization.
  • ▪ High Industry Competition: NVIDIA operates in the highly competitive semiconductor sector, facing strong rivals such as AMD and Intel, which may pressure margins and market share.
  • ▪ Geographic and Supply Chain Exposure: The company conducts significant business in the United States, Taiwan, and China, exposing it to geopolitical tensions, regulatory changes, and supply chain disruptions.
  • ▪ Customer and Channel Concentration: NVIDIA distributes products through a broad but specific ecosystem, including OEMs, system integrators, and cloud service providers; reliance on key partners or channels could pose concentration risks.
  • ▪ Volatility in End Markets: NVIDIA’s exposure to cyclical sectors such as gaming, data centers, and automotive, as well as volatile markets like cryptocurrency mining, may lead to unpredictable demand and earnings fluctuations.
