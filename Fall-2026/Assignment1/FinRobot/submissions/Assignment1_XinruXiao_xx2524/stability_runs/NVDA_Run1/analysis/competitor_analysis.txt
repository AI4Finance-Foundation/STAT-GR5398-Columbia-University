NVIDIA Corporation operates as a leading provider of advanced graphics, computational, and networking solutions, with a diversified portfolio spanning gaming, professional visualization, data center, and automotive sectors. Compared to its primary semiconductor peers, Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Intel Corporation (INTC), NVIDIA has demonstrated exceptional financial performance and growth in recent years.

From 2022A to 2026A, NVIDIA achieved a historical revenue CAGR of 68.3%, reflecting rapid expansion, particularly in AI and data center markets. In 2026A, NVIDIA reported revenue of $215.94 billion, far surpassing its 2022A revenue of $26.91 billion. This growth is also evident in EBITDA, which rose from $11.35 billion in 2022A to $144.55 billion in 2026A. In contrast, AMD’s EBITDA in 2025 is $7.28 billion, and Intel’s is $14.35 billion, both significantly lower than NVIDIA’s figures.

NVIDIA’s profitability metrics further distinguish it from peers. Its EBITDA margin increased from 42.2% in 2022A to 66.9% in 2026A, while contribution margins remained above 70% from 2024A onward. AMD and Intel do not report comparable margin levels in the supplied data.

Valuation multiples, as measured by EV/EBITDA, have been higher for NVIDIA than for AMD and Intel, peaking at 81.05 in 2023 and moderating to 31.43 in 2026A. This premium reflects market expectations for NVIDIA’s continued leadership and innovation, especially in AI and accelerated computing.

In summary, NVIDIA outpaces AMD and Intel in revenue growth, profitability, and market valuation, underpinned by its strong position in high-growth segments such as AI, data centers, and advanced graphics. Its financial trajectory and operational scale set it apart as the dominant force among semiconductor peers.
