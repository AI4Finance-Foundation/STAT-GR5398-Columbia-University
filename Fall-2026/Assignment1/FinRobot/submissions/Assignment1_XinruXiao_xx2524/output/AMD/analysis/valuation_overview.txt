Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) has demonstrated robust historical growth, with revenue increasing from $16.43 billion in 2021A to $34.64 billion in 2025A, representing a historical CAGR of 20.5% from 2021A to 2025A. EBITDA also grew from $4.17 billion in 2021A to $7.28 billion in 2025A. Contribution margins improved from 48.2% in 2021A to 49.5% in 2025A, while EBITDA margins ranged from 18.3% in 2023A to 25.3% in 2021A, recovering to 21.0% in 2025A. EPS fluctuated, reaching $2.67 in 2025A.

Looking ahead, AMD’s own forecasts anticipate continued revenue growth to $40.10 billion by 2028E, with EBITDA rising to $16.84 billion and EBITDA margins expanding to 42.0% in 2028E. Contribution margins are also expected to improve, reaching 52.5% in 2028E.

In terms of valuation, AMD’s EV/EBITDA multiples have been volatile, peaking at 57.1x in 2023A and settling at 47.8x in 2025A. This is higher than Intel’s 14.5x and Nvidia’s 33.8x in 2025A. In 2024A, AMD’s EV/EBITDA was 38.3x, compared to Intel’s 106.9x and Nvidia’s 43.5x. These figures indicate that AMD is trading at a premium to Intel but at a higher multiple than Nvidia in some years, reflecting market expectations for superior growth and margin expansion.

Relative to peers, AMD’s current valuation appears elevated, especially when compared to Intel, but is within the range of Nvidia’s multiples in recent years. The premium may be justified by AMD’s strong historical growth, improving profitability, and positive outlook. However, the high EV/EBITDA multiple suggests that much of the anticipated growth is already reflected in the current valuation, warranting careful monitoring of execution against forecasts to support fair value.
