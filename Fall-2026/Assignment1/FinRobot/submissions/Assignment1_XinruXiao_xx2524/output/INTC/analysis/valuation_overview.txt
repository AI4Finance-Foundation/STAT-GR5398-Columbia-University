Intel Corporation’s current valuation reflects a period of significant operational and financial transition. As of the latest actual fiscal year, 2025A, Intel reported revenue of $52.85 billion, continuing a multi-year decline from $79.02 billion in 2021A. The historical revenue CAGR from 2021A to 2025A is -9.6%, highlighting the company’s recent challenges. EBITDA for 2025A rebounded to $14.35 billion after a sharp drop to $1.20 billion in 2024A, but remains well below the $33.87 billion achieved in 2021A. The EBITDA margin in 2025A was 27.2%, recovering from a low of 2.3% in 2024A.

Intel’s EV/EBITDA multiple has been volatile. In 2025, the EV/EBITDA is 14.50x, a significant improvement from the elevated 106.94x in 2024, but still above the 6.55x–7.13x range seen in 2021A–2022A. Compared to peers, AMD trades at 47.85x and NVIDIA at 33.78x EV/EBITDA in 2025, indicating that while Intel’s multiple is lower than these competitors, it remains above its own historical average.

Peer EBITDA data shows that NVIDIA’s profitability has surged, reaching $86.14 billion in 2025, far outpacing Intel and AMD. Intel’s 2025A EBITDA of $14.35 billion is more than double AMD’s $7.28 billion, but well below NVIDIA’s level.

Looking ahead, Intel’s forecasts suggest modest revenue growth resuming in 2026E–2028E, with EBITDA margins gradually improving to 30.6% by 2028E. However, EPS remains negative throughout the forecast period, indicating ongoing profitability challenges.

In summary, Intel’s current valuation reflects a company in recovery, with profitability and growth still lagging leading peers. While its EV/EBITDA is lower than AMD and NVIDIA for 2025, it is elevated relative to Intel’s own historical levels, suggesting the market is cautiously optimistic but still pricing in risk. A fair value assessment should weigh Intel’s improving margins and stabilization against persistent earnings weakness and strong peer performance.
