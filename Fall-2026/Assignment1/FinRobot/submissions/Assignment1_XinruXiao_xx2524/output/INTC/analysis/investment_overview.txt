Intel Corporation’s recent financial performance reflects a period of significant transition and challenge. From 2021A to 2025A, revenue declined from $79.0 billion to $52.9 billion, representing a historical CAGR of -9.6%. This contraction was accompanied by a sharp drop in EBITDA, which fell from $33.9 billion in 2021A to $1.2 billion in 2024A, before recovering to $14.4 billion in 2025A. Contribution margin also compressed from 55.4% in 2021A to 34.8% in 2025A, while EBITDA margin declined from 42.9% to 27.2% over the same period. Earnings per share (EPS) turned negative in 2024A and 2025A, with reported figures of -$4.38 and -$0.0589, respectively.

Key growth drivers for Intel include its diversified product portfolio across client computing, data center and AI, and foundry services. The company’s strategic collaboration with Infosys to develop a unified AI fabric highlights its commitment to innovation and ecosystem development. Despite these efforts, Intel has faced intense competition and market headwinds, as evidenced by declining revenues and margins.

Looking ahead, Intel’s Financial Metrics forecast a return to top-line growth, with revenue expected to rise to $61.2 billion by 2028E. Contribution and EBITDA margins are projected to gradually improve, reaching 37.8% and 30.6%, respectively, in 2028E. However, EPS is forecast to remain negative through 2028E. The company’s focus on operational efficiency is reflected in declining SG&A margins, projected to fall to 7.2% by 2028E.

In summary, while Intel is positioned for a moderate recovery in revenue and profitability metrics over the next several years, the company continues to face profitability challenges and competitive pressures. Investors should monitor Intel’s execution on its strategic initiatives and its ability to restore sustainable earnings growth.
