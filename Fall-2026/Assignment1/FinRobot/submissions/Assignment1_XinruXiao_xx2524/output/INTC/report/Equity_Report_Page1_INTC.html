Company Overview

Intel Corporation is a leading global technology company specializing in the design, development, manufacturing, marketing, and servicing of computing and related products. Headquartered in Santa Clara, California, and incorporated in 1968, Intel operates internationally, with significant activities in the United States, Ireland, and Israel. The company is organized into three main segments: Client Computing Group (CCG), Data Center and AI (DCAI), and Intel Foundry.

Intel’s CCG segment provides products such as client and commercial CPUs, discrete client GPUs, edge computing, and connectivity solutions. The DCAI segment focuses on data center and AI products, including server CPUs, discrete GPUs, and networking products. The Intel Foundry segment encompasses semiconductor manufacturing, including wafer fabrication, substrates, and related services. Additionally, Intel develops driving assistance and self-driving solutions and manufactures multi-beam mask writing tools. Its customer base includes original equipment manufacturers, original design manufacturers, cloud service providers, and other manufacturers and service providers. Intel distributes its products through direct sales, distributors, resellers, retailers, and OEM partners. The company has also entered into strategic collaborations, such as its partnership with Infosys Limited to develop a multi-layer AI fabric.

In terms of market position, Intel is a major player in the semiconductor industry, competing with companies like AMD and NVIDIA. The company’s historical financial performance from 2021A to 2025A shows a period of revenue contraction, with revenue declining from $79.02 billion in 2021A to $52.85 billion in 2025A, reflecting a historical CAGR of -9.6%. Contribution margin and EBITDA margin also decreased over this period, with contribution margin falling from 55.4% in 2021A to 34.8% in 2025A, and EBITDA margin dropping from 42.9% to 27.2%. Intel’s EBITDA declined from $33.87 billion in 2021A to $1.20 billion in 2024A, before recovering to $14.35 billion in 2025A. Earnings per share (EPS) also turned negative in 2024A and 2025A.

Looking ahead, Intel’s forecasts indicate a return to revenue growth, with revenues expected to reach $61.18 billion by 2028E. Margins are projected to gradually improve, with contribution margin rising to 37.8% and EBITDA margin to 30.6% in 2028E. However, EPS is forecast to remain slightly negative through 2028E. Intel’s financial trajectory reflects both the challenges of recent years and its ongoing efforts to regain momentum through innovation and strategic partnerships in a highly competitive industry.

Investment Update

Intel Corporation’s recent financial performance reflects a period of significant transition and challenge. From 2021A to 2025A, revenue declined from $79.0 billion to $52.9 billion, representing a historical CAGR of -9.6%. This contraction was accompanied by a sharp drop in EBITDA, which fell from $33.9 billion in 2021A to $1.2 billion in 2024A, before recovering to $14.4 billion in 2025A. Contribution margin also compressed from 55.4% in 2021A to 34.8% in 2025A, while EBITDA margin declined from 42.9% to 27.2% over the same period. Earnings per share (EPS) turned negative in 2024A and 2025A, with reported figures of -$4.38 and -$0.0589, respectively.

Key growth drivers for Intel include its diversified product portfolio across client computing, data center and AI, and foundry services. The company’s strategic collaboration with Infosys to develop a unified AI fabric highlights its commitment to innovation and ecosystem development. Despite these efforts, Intel has faced intense competition and market headwinds, as evidenced by declining revenues and margins.

Looking ahead, Intel’s Financial Metrics forecast a return to top-line growth, with revenue expected to rise to $61.2 billion by 2028E. Contribution and EBITDA margins are projected to gradually improve, reaching 37.8% and 30.6%, respectively, in 2028E. However, EPS is forecast to remain negative through 2028E. The company’s focus on operational efficiency is reflected in declining SG&A margins, projected to fall to 7.2% by 2028E.

In summary, while Intel is positioned for a moderate recovery in revenue and profitability metrics over the next several years, the company continues to face profitability challenges and competitive pressures. Investors should monitor Intel’s execution on its strategic initiatives and its ability to restore sustainable earnings growth.

Valuation

Intel Corporation’s current valuation reflects a period of significant operational and financial transition. As of the latest actual fiscal year, 2025A, Intel reported revenue of $52.85 billion, continuing a multi-year decline from $79.02 billion in 2021A. The historical revenue CAGR from 2021A to 2025A is -9.6%, highlighting the company’s recent challenges. EBITDA for 2025A rebounded to $14.35 billion after a sharp drop to $1.20 billion in 2024A, but remains well below the $33.87 billion achieved in 2021A. The EBITDA margin in 2025A was 27.2%, recovering from a low of 2.3% in 2024A.

Intel’s EV/EBITDA multiple has been volatile. In 2025, the EV/EBITDA is 14.50x, a significant improvement from the elevated 106.94x in 2024, but still above the 6.55x–7.13x range seen in 2021A–2022A. Compared to peers, AMD trades at 47.85x and NVIDIA at 33.78x EV/EBITDA in 2025, indicating that while Intel’s multiple is lower than these competitors, it remains above its own historical average.

Peer EBITDA data shows that NVIDIA’s profitability has surged, reaching $86.14 billion in 2025, far outpacing Intel and AMD. Intel’s 2025A EBITDA of $14.35 billion is more than double AMD’s $7.28 billion, but well below NVIDIA’s level.

Looking ahead, Intel’s forecasts suggest modest revenue growth resuming in 2026E–2028E, with EBITDA margins gradually improving to 30.6% by 2028E. However, EPS remains negative throughout the forecast period, indicating ongoing profitability challenges.

In summary, Intel’s current valuation reflects a company in recovery, with profitability and growth still lagging leading peers. While its EV/EBITDA is lower than AMD and NVIDIA for 2025, it is elevated relative to Intel’s own historical levels, suggesting the market is cautiously optimistic but still pricing in risk. A fair value assessment should weigh Intel’s improving margins and stabilization against persistent earnings weakness and strong peer performance.

  • ▪ Uncertain Recovery: Despite forecasted revenue growth from 2026E to 2028E, Intel’s EPS remains negative in all forecast years, indicating ongoing challenges in returning to sustainable profitability.
