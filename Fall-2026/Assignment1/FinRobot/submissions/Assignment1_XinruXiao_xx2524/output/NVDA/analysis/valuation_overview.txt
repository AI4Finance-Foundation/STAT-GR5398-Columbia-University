NVIDIA Corporation’s valuation metrics reflect its rapid transformation and dominant position in the semiconductor industry, particularly in AI and data center solutions. As of 2026A, NVIDIA reported an EBITDA of $144.55 billion and an EBITDA margin of 66.9%, significantly higher than its closest peers. For comparison, Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Intel (INTC) reported 2025 EBITDA of $7.28 billion and $14.35 billion, respectively, while NVIDIA’s 2025 EBITDA was $86.14 billion. This highlights NVIDIA’s exceptional scale and profitability.

NVIDIA’s EV/EBITDA multiple has moderated from a peak of 81.05x in 2023A to 43.45x in 2024A, 33.78x in 2025A, and 31.43x in 2026A. These multiples remain above those of AMD (38.27x in 2024A, 47.85x in 2025A) and Intel (14.50x in 2025A), reflecting the market’s premium for NVIDIA’s superior growth and margins. Notably, NVIDIA’s historical revenue CAGR from 2022A to 2026A was 68.3%, underscoring its explosive expansion, while EBITDA margins have consistently exceeded 58% since 2024A.

Looking ahead, NVIDIA’s own forecasts anticipate continued revenue and EBITDA growth, with 2027E EBITDA expected at $159.85 billion and a margin of 70.5%. While revenue growth rates are forecast to moderate (5.0% in 2027E, 6.0% in 2028E, 4.0% in 2029E), profitability remains robust, with EBITDA margins projected to rise further.

In summary, NVIDIA trades at a premium to peers on EV/EBITDA, justified by its industry-leading growth, scale, and profitability. The company’s fair value appears supported by its strong financial performance and outlook, though the premium valuation reflects high market expectations for sustained leadership in AI and accelerated computing.
