Company Overview

Microsoft Corporation is a leading global technology company headquartered in Redmond, Washington, and operates in the Software - Infrastructure segment of the technology sector. Founded in 1975, Microsoft has developed a broad and diversified business model centered on the creation, marketing, and ongoing support of software, digital services, computing devices, and integrated solutions. The company’s operations are structured into three main divisions: Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing.

The Productivity and Business Processes segment delivers essential tools for both enterprise and individual users. This includes the comprehensive Office suite—such as Exchange, SharePoint, Microsoft Teams, Office 365 Security and Compliance, Microsoft Viva, and Skype for Business—alongside consumer offerings like Skype, Outlook.com, OneDrive, and LinkedIn. Dynamics 365, a suite of integrated cloud and on-premises business applications, also falls under this division.

The Intelligent Cloud segment is focused on advanced infrastructure and platform services. It encompasses licensing for products such as SQL Server, Windows Servers, Visual Studio, and System Center, as well as Client Access Licenses. This division also includes GitHub, a leading platform for developer collaboration; Nuance, which provides AI solutions for healthcare and businesses; and Azure, Microsoft’s expansive cloud computing platform. Additional services include enterprise support, consulting, and professional services related to Microsoft’s server and desktop technologies.

The More Personal Computing segment covers a wide range of consumer and commercial computing experiences. It generates revenue from Windows operating system licensing (including OEM and commercial agreements), patent licensing, and Windows IoT. This division also produces hardware such as Surface devices, PC accessories, and Xbox gaming consoles, and manages gaming content, subscription services, and royalties from third-party titles. Search services like Bing and Microsoft’s advertising platforms are also part of this segment.

Microsoft distributes its products through various channels, including OEMs, wholesale distributors, resellers, digital marketplaces, its own online storefronts, and physical retail outlets.

In terms of recent financial performance, Microsoft reported revenue of $331.8 billion in 2026A, up from $198.3 billion in 2022A, representing a historical revenue CAGR of 13.7% from 2022A to 2026A. EBITDA increased from $100.2 billion in 2022A to $207.5 billion in 2026A, with the EBITDA margin rising from 50.6% to 62.5% over the same period. The company’s earnings per share (EPS) grew from 9.7 in 2022A to 18.0 in 2026A. These results underscore Microsoft’s strong market position, broad product portfolio, and continued growth across its core business segments.

Investment Update

Microsoft Corporation delivered robust financial performance through the latest reported period, 2026A. Revenue grew from $198.3 billion in 2022A to $331.8 billion in 2026A, representing a historical CAGR of 13.7% over this period. The company’s EBITDA also expanded significantly, reaching $207.5 billion in 2026A, up from $100.2 billion in 2022A. EBITDA margin improved from 50.6% in 2022A to 62.5% in 2026A, reflecting strong operational leverage and disciplined cost management. Contribution margin remained consistently high, ranging from 68.4% to 69.8% between 2022A and 2024A, and was 67.9% in 2026A.

Key growth drivers included continued momentum across Microsoft’s three core segments: Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing. The Intelligent Cloud division, anchored by Azure, benefited from rising demand for cloud infrastructure and AI-driven solutions. Productivity and Business Processes, with offerings like Office 365 and Dynamics 365, maintained relevance for enterprise and consumer customers. The More Personal Computing segment contributed through Windows licensing, Surface hardware, and gaming, including Xbox and related services.

Looking ahead, Microsoft’s Financial Metrics forecast steady revenue growth, with revenue expected to reach $384.1 billion by 2029E. EBITDA is projected to rise to $238.1 billion in 2029E, with margins remaining strong. EPS is forecast to increase from $19.09 in 2027E to $21.47 in 2029E. Revenue growth rates are expected to moderate, with annual increases of 5.0% in 2027E, 6.0% in 2028E, and 4.0% in 2029E.

Overall, Microsoft’s historical performance demonstrates strong execution and margin expansion, while forecasts indicate continued, albeit moderating, growth supported by its diversified technology portfolio and leadership in cloud and productivity solutions.

Recent News Summary

Microsoft Corporation has recently been in the spotlight due to several notable developments, particularly in artificial intelligence and strategic business shifts. Reports indicate that Microsoft plans to deploy its self-developed AI chips on a large scale next year, a move that has contributed to a nearly 1% rise in its share price during early trading. This initiative underscores Microsoft’s commitment to expanding its AI capabilities and reducing reliance on third-party chip suppliers, which could enhance its competitive position in the rapidly evolving AI sector.

The company’s strong performance during the third-quarter tech recovery has also been highlighted, with Microsoft standing out among its peers. Analysts and investors are increasingly focused on Microsoft’s AI-driven growth story, with some suggesting that the company’s aggressive investment in AI justifies a strong buy rating. This sentiment is reinforced by observations that Microsoft is prioritizing AI investments over other segments, such as its Xbox gaming business, which one analyst described as “almost irrelevant” compared to the company’s AI ambitions.

Despite broader market dips, Microsoft’s stock has shown resilience, gaining ground even as other stocks declined. However, the company faces scrutiny over recent price target revisions by analysts and changes to Xbox pricing, which contributed to a 4.5% slide in its stock price on one trading day. High-profile investors, such as Michael Burry, have also made notable moves, with Burry exiting his Microsoft position, reflecting shifting sentiment among some market participants.

Overall, Microsoft’s strategic focus on AI, coupled with its robust cloud and productivity businesses, positions it well for future growth. However, investors should monitor ongoing developments in AI deployment, analyst sentiment, and the company’s ability to navigate challenges in its gaming and hardware segments.

Valuation

Microsoft Corporation’s current valuation reflects its strong historical performance and robust financial profile. As of the latest actual fiscal year, 2026A, Microsoft reported revenue of $331.8 billion and EBITDA of $207.5 billion, with an EBITDA margin of 62.5%. Over the historical period from 2022A to 2026A, Microsoft achieved a revenue CAGR of 13.7%, demonstrating significant growth momentum.

In terms of valuation multiples, Microsoft’s EV/EBITDA ratio for 2026A stands at 13.87. This is notably lower than its 2024A multiple of 26.11 and 2025A multiple of 23.60, indicating a substantial compression in valuation despite continued earnings growth. Compared to peers, Apple’s EV/EBITDA for 2024A is 26.56, while Cisco’s is 13.78. By 2026A, Microsoft’s EV/EBITDA is closely aligned with Cisco’s (23.66), but well below Apple’s most recent available multiple, suggesting Microsoft is trading at a relative discount to Apple and in line with Cisco on a forward basis.

Looking ahead, Microsoft’s own forecasts (Financial Metrics table) project continued revenue and EBITDA growth through 2029E, with revenue expected to reach $384.1 billion and EBITDA $238.1 billion in 2029E. EPS is also forecast to rise from 18.0 in 2026A to 21.47 in 2029E. However, the forecasted revenue growth rates moderate to single digits (5.0% in 2027E, 6.0% in 2028E, and 4.0% in 2029E), and EBITDA margins are expected to remain strong, stabilizing around 59-62%.

In summary, Microsoft’s current valuation multiple is below its recent historical average and is competitive with key peers. Given its strong actual financial results, high margins, and continued growth outlook, Microsoft appears fairly valued relative to its sector, with the potential for upside if it sustains or exceeds forecasted performance.
