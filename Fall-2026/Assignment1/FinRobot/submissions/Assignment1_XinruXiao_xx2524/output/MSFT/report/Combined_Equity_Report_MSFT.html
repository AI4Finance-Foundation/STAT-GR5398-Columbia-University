Company Overview

Microsoft Corporation is a leading global technology company headquartered in Redmond, Washington, and operates in the Software - Infrastructure segment of the technology sector. Founded in 1975, Microsoft has developed a broad and diversified business model centered on the creation, marketing, and ongoing support of software, digital services, computing devices, and integrated solutions. The company’s operations are structured into three main divisions: Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing.

The Productivity and Business Processes segment delivers essential tools for both enterprise and individual users. This includes the comprehensive Office suite—such as Exchange, SharePoint, Microsoft Teams, Office 365 Security and Compliance, Microsoft Viva, and Skype for Business—alongside consumer offerings like Skype, Outlook.com, OneDrive, and LinkedIn. Dynamics 365, a suite of integrated cloud and on-premises business applications, also falls under this division.

The Intelligent Cloud segment is focused on advanced infrastructure and platform services. It encompasses licensing for products such as SQL Server, Windows Servers, Visual Studio, and System Center, as well as Client Access Licenses. This division also includes GitHub, a leading platform for developer collaboration; Nuance, which provides AI solutions for healthcare and businesses; and Azure, Microsoft’s expansive cloud computing platform. Additional services include enterprise support, consulting, and professional services related to Microsoft’s server and desktop technologies.

The More Personal Computing segment covers a wide range of consumer and commercial computing experiences. It generates revenue from Windows operating system licensing (including OEM and commercial agreements), patent licensing, and Windows IoT. This division also produces hardware such as Surface devices, PC accessories, and Xbox gaming consoles, and manages gaming content, subscription services, and royalties from third-party titles. Search services like Bing and Microsoft’s advertising platforms are also part of this segment.

Microsoft distributes its products through various channels, including OEMs, wholesale distributors, resellers, digital marketplaces, its own online storefronts, and physical retail outlets.

In terms of recent financial performance, Microsoft reported revenue of $331.8 billion in 2026A, up from $198.3 billion in 2022A, representing a historical revenue CAGR of 13.7% from 2022A to 2026A. EBITDA increased from $100.2 billion in 2022A to $207.5 billion in 2026A, with the EBITDA margin rising from 50.6% to 62.5% over the same period. The company’s earnings per share (EPS) grew from 9.7 in 2022A to 18.0 in 2026A. These results underscore Microsoft’s strong market position, broad product portfolio, and continued growth across its core business segments.

Overall, Microsoft’s historical performance demonstrates strong execution and margin expansion, while forecasts indicate continued, albeit moderating, growth supported by its diversified technology portfolio and leadership in cloud and productivity solutions.

However, the forecasted revenue growth rates moderate to single digits (5.0% in 2027E, 6.0% in 2028E, and 4.0% in 2029E), and EBITDA margins are expected to remain strong, stabilizing around 59-62%.

In summary, Microsoft’s current valuation multiple is below its recent historical average and is competitive with key peers. Given its strong actual financial results, high margins, and continued growth outlook, Microsoft appears fairly valued relative to its sector, with the potential for upside if it sustains or exceeds forecasted performance.
