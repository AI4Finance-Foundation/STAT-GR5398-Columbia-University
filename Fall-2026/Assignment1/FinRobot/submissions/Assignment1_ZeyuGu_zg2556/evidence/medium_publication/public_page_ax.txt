			117 text The verifier combines an evidence-bounded review with deterministic checks. It resolves citations to actual prompt lines, checks numeric bindings and source coverage, preserves original/revised mappings, and makes the renderer use the treated text. Unsupported claims retain a visible warning. A claim recognized as Contradicted must be corrected from evidence or withheld.
			118 text Real failures drove several revisions of this same improvement: exact quote copying failed, numeric bindings used the wrong values, source words were omitted, and a review cited only a company-name line to support a business description. The final V6 rejects structural-only citations. Earlier attempts, source versions and costs remain available. Development used the evaluated cohort, so this is not a held-out benchmark.
			119 text Under the original V6 evaluation, independent after auditing found 48 contradictions among 1,590 assertions. Support rises from 19.69% to 22.52%, but more repeated claims change the denominator. Verifiable numeric error falls from 25/171 to 29/217: a lower rate alongside a higher count. It would be misleading to present that as a clean accuracy improvement.
			120 text The useful gain is visible issue coverage. There were no baseline warnings. After treatment, warnings cover 1,158 of 1,232 unsupported or contradicted assertions: 93.99% recall, with 78.78% precision. The system retains 96.13% of independently supported lexical fact keys and 100% of supporting evidence spans. Those are imperfect retention proxies, not proof of semantic recall. It did not improve its score by deleting all information; it often preserves too much repeated unverified text.
			121 text The remaining failures matter. Seventy-four problematic assertions are unflagged, including eight contradictions. One says NVIDIA’s comparable multiple is above AMD even though the supplied figures are 33.7774x and 47.8456x. 			134 heading What happened when I strengthened the reviewer, Value: 2, ID: 61f4
				135 text What happened when I strengthened the reviewer
			136 text I retained the V6 experiment and ran a separate follow-up with stricter instructions on fiscal periods, company comparisons and growth horizons, plus exact-string deduplication. The full same-input, eight-section study on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel reduced unflagged problematic assertions from 74 to 31. Warning recall rose from 93.99% to 97.70%, with precision rising from 78.78% to 82.42%. That is a measurable improvement in triage. Yet eight contradictions still passed without warnings. The newer output had 325 supported claims among 1,673 assertions (19.43%), 41 contradictions and 23 errors among 180 verifiable numeric claims. Supported lexical-key retention fell to 76.06%; paraphrases affect that proxy, and evidence-span retention was 93.02%. More warnings did not establish a trustworthy editor.
			137 text The judge itself supplied another failure case. A model evaluating warned prose skipped 18 NVIDIA units as nonfactual. After I hid only the warning prefixes, it stopped skipping those units but still combined separate facts and missed explicit prompt evidence. Both failed proposals are preserved. A Codex task agent explicitly adjudicated every changed unit against the original evidence; no human validation was performed. The same development cohort influenced the instructions, so these findings are not a held-out test.
			138 text A bounded reasoning-model pilot also produced a negative result. One frozen NVIDIA section on GPT-5.4 used all 26,000 allotted output tokens as reasoning, returned no visible review and cost $0.400930. I did not expand that high-effort setting or count it as a successful verifier run; a separate low-effort treatment followed. The raw response is saved. Together these experiments argue for measuring information retention, missed contradictions and evaluator failures alongside warning rates. The complete package retains V6 as the historical original experimental release and reports the V7.1 candidate separately; neither is an unattended investment product.
