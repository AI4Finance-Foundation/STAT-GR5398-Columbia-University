NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) stands out among its peers AMD and Intel with significantly stronger financial growth and profitability metrics. From 2022A through 2026A, NVIDIA’s revenue exhibits an extraordinary compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 68.3%, far surpassing typical industry growth rates. This rapid expansion is supported by a robust increase in EBITDA, which grows from $11.35 billion in 2022A to $144.55 billion in 2026A, reflecting an impressive EBITDA margin improvement from 42.2% to 66.9%. In contrast, AMD and Intel show comparatively modest EBITDA levels over the same period. For example, AMD’s EBITDA in 2025 is $7.275 billion and Intel’s is $14.35 billion, both substantially lower than NVIDIA’s $86.14 billion.

NVIDIA also demonstrates superior operational efficiency, with its SG&A margin decreasing from 8.0% in 2022A to an estimated 2.1% in 2026A, indicating better cost control relative to peers. The contribution margin remains robust, peaking around 75% in 2025A and stabilizing above 70% thereafter, which is a testament to NVIDIA’s strong pricing power and product mix.

Valuation multiples show that NVIDIA trades at a premium compared to AMD and Intel, with EV/EBITDA ratios above 30x in forecast years, while AMD and Intel’s multiples fluctuate more widely and generally remain lower. This premium valuation reflects market confidence in NVIDIA’s dominant position in high-growth markets such as AI and data centers.

Overall, NVIDIA’s financial trajectory and operational metrics position it well ahead of AMD and Intel in terms of growth, profitability, and market valuation. However, the higher valuation also implies elevated expectations, and NVIDIA will need to sustain its growth to justify this premium.
