Intel Corporation (INTC) is a leading multinational technology company primarily focused on the design and manufacture of semiconductor products. Its business model centers around developing advanced microprocessors and related technologies that power a wide range of computing devices—from personal computers and servers to embedded systems and Internet of Things (IoT) applications. Intel’s product portfolio includes central processing units (CPUs), graphics processing units (GPUs), chipsets, and other semiconductor components, targeting various market segments such as consumer electronics, data centers, and enterprise solutions.

In terms of market position, Intel has historically been a dominant player in the semiconductor industry, competing with companies like AMD and Nvidia. Despite this strong legacy, recent financial performance indicates challenges. From 2021 to 2025, Intel’s revenue has experienced a significant decline, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of -9.6%, dropping from $79.0 billion in 2021 to approximately $52.9 billion in 2025. The revenue contraction reflects market pressures and competitive dynamics impacting the company’s sales volumes and pricing power.

Profitability metrics also reveal a period of stress. Contribution profit decreased sharply from $43.8 billion in 2021 to $18.4 billion in 2025, with contribution margin shrinking from 55.4% to 34.8%. EBITDA followed a similar pattern, declining from $33.9 billion in 2021 to $14.4 billion in 2025, and EBITDA margin falling from 42.9% to 27.2%. This erosion in margins and profits underscores operational and competitive challenges impacting efficiency and cost management.

The company’s earnings per share (EPS) trend is particularly concerning, with EPS falling from $4.89 in 2021 to negative values by 2024 (-$4.38) and remaining near zero or negative through 2025 (-$0.0589). This suggests profitability pressures and potential write-downs or restructuring charges affecting net income. Intel’s selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) expenses have remained relatively stable as a percentage of revenue, around 8-11%, indicating steady overhead costs despite declining sales.

Looking ahead, forecasts from 2026 to 2028 suggest modest revenue recovery, growing from approximately $55.5 billion in 2026 to $61.2 billion in 2028, with gradual margin improvements. Contribution margin is expected to rise from 35.8% to 37.8%, and EBITDA margin is projected to improve from 27.6% to 30.6%, indicating a potential operational turnaround.

In comparison to peers, Intel’s EBITDA remains substantially higher than AMD’s but trails Nvidia in recent years. The company’s EV/EBITDA multiples have fluctuated widely, reaching an exceptionally high 106.94 in 2024, reflecting market uncertainty or valuation adjustments.

In summary, Intel Corporation operates as a key player in semiconductor manufacturing with a broad product lineup. However, it has faced recent financial challenges marked by declining revenues, compressed margins, and negative earnings. Forecasts indicate a cautious recovery phase with improving profitability metrics, but the company remains in a competitive and rapidly evolving market environment.
