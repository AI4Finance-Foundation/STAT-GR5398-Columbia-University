Intel Corporation (INTC) has experienced a significant decline in revenue from 2021A to 2025A, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of -9.6%, dropping from $79.0 billion in 2021 to $52.9 billion in 2025. In contrast, its peers AMD and NVIDIA show different trajectories. Although precise revenue figures for AMD and NVIDIA are unavailable in the data, EBITDA comparisons provide insight into their operational performance and scale.

Intel's EBITDA has also declined sharply, from $33.9 billion in 2021A to $14.4 billion in 2025A, with a particularly low EBITDA margin of 2.3% in 2024A, reflecting operational challenges. AMD’s EBITDA, by comparison, is significantly lower in absolute terms, around $4.1 billion to $7.3 billion during 2021-2025, which shows a smaller but more consistent scale relative to Intel. NVIDIA’s EBITDA is not available for 2021 but grows substantially, reaching $86.1 billion in 2025, indicating a strong growth trajectory and superior profitability compared to Intel.

Valuation multiples further highlight differences. Intel’s EV/EBITDA ratio spiked to 106.9x in 2024, reflecting market concerns or earnings volatility, before falling back to 14.5x in 2025. AMD's EV/EBITDA ratios fluctuate between 17.9x and 57.1x in the same period, suggesting higher growth expectations relative to Intel but with more valuation volatility. NVIDIA consistently trades at high EV/EBITDA multiples, ranging from 33.8x to 81.0x, aligned with its rapid growth and premium market position.

Intel’s contribution margin declined from 55.4% in 2021 to 34.8% in 2025, indicating margin pressure, whereas peers’ margin data is unavailable but implied to be better given their EBITDA trends. Intel’s earnings per share (EPS) dropped sharply, turning negative in 2024 and remaining slightly negative thereafter, contrasting with AMD and NVIDIA which have maintained positive earnings.

In summary, Intel faces declining revenue, margin compression, and profitability challenges relative to AMD and NVIDIA. While Intel remains a large player by absolute EBITDA, its peers demonstrate stronger growth and market valuation metrics, reflecting a competitive disadvantage in recent years. Forecasts suggest modest revenue recovery and margin improvement for Intel, but the company trails its peers in growth momentum and market valuation.
