Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) has demonstrated notable revenue growth over the 2021 to 2025 period, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 20.5%. Revenue rose from $16.4 billion in 2021 to $34.6 billion in 2025. Despite a slight revenue dip in 2023 to $22.68 billion from $23.6 billion in 2022, AMD rebounded strongly with projected revenues of $25.8 billion in 2024 and $34.6 billion in 2025.

Contribution profit increased substantially, reaching $17.15 billion in 2025, with contribution margin improving to 49.5%, indicating enhanced operational efficiency and profitability. EBITDA has shown volatility, declining in 2023 to $4.15 billion from $5.53 billion in 2022, before recovering to $7.28 billion in 2025. Correspondingly, EBITDA margin improved to 21.0% by 2025, up from 18.3% in 2023 but still below the 25.3% margin seen in 2021. SG&A expenses increased in absolute terms and as a percentage of revenue, reaching 12.0% in 2025, reflecting increased investments in sales and marketing.

Earnings per share (EPS) showed significant fluctuation, falling from $2.61 in 2021 to $0.53 in 2023 before recovering to $2.67 in 2025. Forecasts for 2026 through 2028 suggest continued revenue growth but at a more moderate pace, reaching approximately $40.1 billion by 2028, with contribution margin and EBITDA margin improving further to 52.5% and 42.0%, respectively. EBITDA is forecasted to more than double between 2025 and 2028, reaching over $16.8 billion.

Peer comparisons show AMD’s EBITDA trailing Intel and Nvidia in recent years, with Nvidia exhibiting significantly higher EBITDA levels and lower EV/EBITDA multiples. AMD’s EV/EBITDA multiples have varied widely, reaching as high as 57.1x in 2023, which indicates market volatility or varying investor sentiment.

Overall, AMD’s recent financial performance illustrates strong revenue growth driven by operational improvements and expanding margins. The outlook suggests continued growth with improving profitability metrics, supported by ongoing investments. However, relative to peers, AMD faces competitive pressures reflected in its EBITDA scale and valuation metrics. Investors should monitor AMD’s margin trajectory and competitive positioning in the semiconductor industry.
