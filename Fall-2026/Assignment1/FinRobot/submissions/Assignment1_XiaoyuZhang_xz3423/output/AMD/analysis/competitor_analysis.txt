Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) operates in a competitive semiconductor market alongside peers such as Intel Corporation (INTC) and NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA). From 2021 to 2025, AMD demonstrated strong revenue growth with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 20.5%, increasing revenue from $16.43 billion in 2021 to $34.64 billion in 2025. This growth rate notably outpaces typical industry expansion, highlighting AMD’s robust market penetration and product demand.

When comparing EBITDA, AMD's figures have generally trailed behind its larger peers. In 2025, AMD’s EBITDA is forecasted at approximately $7.28 billion, while Intel projects around $14.35 billion and NVIDIA significantly outpaces both at $86.14 billion. AMD’s EBITDA margin shows improvement, rising from 25.3% in 2021 to a forecasted 21.0% in 2025, but still remains below NVIDIA’s margin, which is unavailable here but implied to be higher given the EBITDA scale relative to revenue. Intel’s margins appear more volatile, with a sharp decline in 2024 EBITDA but a rebound afterward.

AMD’s EV/EBITDA multiples indicate a premium valuation compared to Intel, with AMD’s 2025 EV/EBITDA at about 47.8x versus Intel’s 14.5x. NVIDIA’s multiple stands at 33.8x in 2025, suggesting that the market values AMD’s growth prospects more aggressively relative to Intel, though NVIDIA commands higher absolute profitability and scale.

In terms of contribution margin, AMD improves from 48.2% in 2021 to a forecasted 49.5% in 2025, reflecting operational efficiency gains. AMD’s SG&A expenses as a percentage of revenue rise slightly over the period, from 8.8% to 12.0%, which could impact profitability but also signals investment in growth initiatives.

In summary, AMD is growing faster than its peers and improving operational efficiency but remains smaller in absolute profitability compared to Intel and NVIDIA. The company's higher EV/EBITDA suggests market confidence in future growth, but it faces pressure to scale profitability and margins to compete with industry leaders.
