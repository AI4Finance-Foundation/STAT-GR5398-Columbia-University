Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is a global semiconductor company specializing in the design and development of high-performance computing and graphics solutions. Its business model centers on creating microprocessors, graphics processing units (GPUs), and related technologies that serve a diverse range of markets including personal computers, data centers, gaming, and embedded systems. AMD generates revenue primarily through the sale of these products to original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), cloud service providers, and consumers.

AMD's product portfolio includes CPUs for desktops, laptops, and servers, as well as GPUs for gaming and professional visualization. The company also develops semi-custom chips used in gaming consoles. This diversified product offering positions AMD competitively in both the CPU and GPU markets, where it contends with major peers such as Intel and NVIDIA.

In terms of market position, AMD has exhibited strong revenue growth historically. From 2021 to 2025, the company’s revenue increased at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 20.5%, rising from $16.4 billion in 2021 to $34.6 billion in 2025. Despite a slight revenue decline in 2023, AMD rebounded with a 13.7% increase in 2024 and a robust 34.3% growth projected for 2025. Contribution margins improved over the period, reaching 49.5% in 2025 from 48.2% in 2021, reflecting operational efficiencies and product mix improvements.

Profitability metrics show some volatility. EBITDA grew from $4.17 billion in 2021 to $7.28 billion in 2025, though the EBITDA margin fluctuated, peaking at 25.3% in 2021, dipping to 18.3% in 2023, and recovering to 21.0% in 2025. Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A) expenses increased in absolute terms and as a percentage of revenue, reaching 12.0% in 2025, indicating heightened investments in marketing and administrative support. Earnings per share (EPS) showed variability with a low of $0.53 in 2023 and a rebound to $2.67 in 2025.

Compared to peers, AMD’s EBITDA is smaller than Intel’s and NVIDIA’s, with Intel reporting substantially higher EBITDA figures over recent years. AMD’s EV/EBITDA multiples have been relatively high, ranging from 17.9x to 57.1x between 2022 and 2023, indicating market expectations for growth but also reflecting valuation volatility. Forecast data for 2026 through 2028 suggest continued revenue growth, reaching approximately $40.1 billion by 2028, with improving contribution margins and EBITDA margins projected to expand significantly, reaching 42.0% by 2028.

Overall, AMD’s business model leverages innovation in semiconductor design to address growing market demand across computing and graphics sectors. The company has demonstrated strong revenue growth and margin improvement historically, with forecasts indicating further growth and profitability enhancement in the coming years. However, competitive pressures and investment costs contribute to fluctuations in profitability metrics.
