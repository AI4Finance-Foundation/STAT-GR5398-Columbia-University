## Abstract

This project implements a multi-company equity research workflow with FinRobot and examines whether financial narratives generated by a large language model are numerically grounded, reproducible, and stable. Five semiconductor companies are analyzed using financial tables, forecasts, peer comparisons, machine-generated investment narratives, and HTML research reports. For reliability assessment, 22 financial records for AVGO and QCOM are reconciled to locally saved SEC Company Facts; two illustrative five-year discounted cash-flow (DCF) scenarios are constructed; 206 candidate statements across three companies are preliminarily audited for evidentiary grounding; three independent NVDA narrative runs are compared; and a financial grounding verifier is revised and tested against a baseline. On a curated 14-passage benchmark, passage-level verifier F1 rises from 0.667 to 1.000. The result demonstrates improvement on the evaluated cases, not production-level generalization. The full candidate-sentence grounding audit found 113/206 Supported statements (54.85%), with particularly low grounding in risk narratives. The main takeaway is that deterministic reconciliation and explicit provenance are important complements to LLM-generated financial prose.

## 1. Research Objectives

Financial equity research combines structured accounting data with qualitative interpretation. An LLM can produce readable analysis quickly, but errors may arise when it mixes fiscal periods, conflates actuals with forecasts, references unsupported valuation figures, or compares incompatible metrics. This assignment addresses three questions:

1. Can FinRobot generate research deliverables for five related semiconductor companies using heterogeneous financial-data sources?
2. How reliable and stable are its financial claims when compared with recorded evidence and across repeated generation runs?
3. Does a targeted, rule-based financial grounding verifier reduce known numerical errors without introducing excessive false positives?

The study evaluates financial-text quality, not investment performance. Nothing herein should be interpreted as a live trading recommendation.

## 2. Implementation and Data Provenance

The five-company universe consists of NVDA, AMD, INTC, AVGO, and QCOM. Historical records and illustrative forecasts were processed into normalized financial tables and peer comparisons, and seven narrative sections were generated for each company. Five professional HTML equity reports were produced and passed 25 internal technical checks (five checks per company) for the expected sections and data consistency after a peer-comparison correction.

The original workflow uses Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) where accessible. Because the available FMP Free endpoints did not provide the needed AVGO and QCOM statements, these two companies use locally saved SEC EDGAR Company Facts and XBRL concepts. This distinction is retained in the research provenance. SEC Company Facts is a primary regulatory data source, but selecting the appropriate taxonomy concept, fiscal period, and financial definition remains an analyst responsibility. Financial projections are scenario outputs, not company guidance.

A material problem identified during the workflow was an inconsistent NVDA peer comparison: one earlier 2027E EBITDA figure was approximately $382 billion even though the main NVDA projection was approximately $159.85 billion. The peer table was rebuilt from the same per-company analysis files used elsewhere in the report. The original failure became a useful positive test case for the verifier improvement.

## 5. Part 2B: Illustrative Discounted Cash-Flow Analysis

Using the audited FY2025 FCF proxies as starting values, a five-year cash-flow model projected 2026–2030 growth at 5%, 6%, 4%, 4%, and 4%, respectively. A base discount rate of 11% and terminal growth of 3% produced the following discounted cash-flow proxies:

| Company | FY2025 FCF proxy | Base discounted cash-flow proxy | Sensitivity range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| AVGO | $26.914B | $371.348B | $277.80B–$575.56B |
| QCOM | $12.820B | $176.885B | $132.33B–$274.16B |

The sensitivity grid varies the discount rate from 9% to 13% and terminal growth from 2% to 4%. Values fall as the discount rate increases and rise as terminal growth increases, confirming the expected direction of model sensitivity. Both outputs change substantially over the chosen grid, showing why reporting a single point estimate without scenarios can be misleading.

**Financial interpretation constraint:** Because operating cash flow minus capex is an FCF proxy, the discount rate is an illustrative rather than calibrated WACC, and net debt/cash/share-count adjustments were not completed, these values are *cash-flow present-value scenarios*. They are neither verified enterprise values nor equity values and **must not** be called per-share targets. The assumptions are pedagogical and not claimed to be externally estimated capital-market parameters.

## 6. Part 2C: Evidence Grounding Audit

### 6.1 Scope and methodology

The expanded audit reviewed **206 candidate narrative statements**
from NVIDIA (NVDA), AMD, and Broadcom (AVGO), spanning seven
substantive FinRobot-generated sections per company.

Each candidate statement received a preliminary whole-sentence label:

- **Supported:** its material factual and numerical content agrees
  with the evidence available to the generating agent.
- **Unsupported:** relevant evidence is absent or insufficient,
  including unevidenced causal or market interpretations.
- **Contradicted:** a material claim conflicts with the recorded
  evidence, such as a wrong peer-company amount or an incorrect
  Actual/Estimate period designation.

Grounding Rate is defined as the number of Supported candidate
statements divided by the total number of reviewed candidates.
The audit combines automated evidence assistance and manual-style
review. Labels represent a preliminary sentence-level adjudication,
not an independently validated atomic-claim gold standard.

### 6.2 Overall and company-level results

Of the 206 candidate statements, **113 were Supported,
66 Unsupported, and 27 Contradicted**, yielding an overall
candidate-level Grounding Rate of **54.85%**.

### 6.5 Observed grounding failures

Three recurring failure mechanisms were identified:

1. **Actual/Estimate confusion.** Historical values marked
   `2025A` or `2026A` were sometimes described as forecasted
   or estimated.
2. **Peer-figure attribution errors.** A generated NVDA
   valuation statement gave 2027E peer EBITDA estimates of
   $8.6B for AMD and $47.4B for INTC, whereas the saved
   comparison table records approximately $15.614B and
   $17.118B, respectively.
3. **Unsupported causal interpretation.** Correct financial
   figures were used to assert unverified claims about
   pricing power, management efficiency, investor sentiment,
   future demand, or business risk.

These patterns illustrate why numerical consistency alone is
not equivalent to fully grounded financial analysis.

### 6.6 Limitations and reproducibility

The 206 observations are extracted **candidate sentences**,
not necessarily all atomic propositions in the complete reports.
Compound statements can mix supported figures with unsupported
interpretation. Several qualitative annotations warrant further
independent review. Grounding was checked relative to the saved
FinRobot input evidence, not independently against all public
company filings.

Seven substantive agent sections per company were evaluated.
News-summary placeholders without substantive evidence-bearing
statements are recorded as **N/A**, not as 100% grounded.

The original 24-claim exploratory sample remains available as
a separate historical experiment but is superseded by the
206-candidate audit for section-level reporting.

## 7. Part 2D: Three-Run NVDA Stability Test

The same frozen NVDA financial tables, model configuration, and prompt were reused across three independent runs to generate investment-overview and risk sections. The purpose was to examine narrative variation while holding recorded financial inputs fixed.

Three prominent risk themes recurred in all runs: revenue-growth volatility, elevated valuation multiples, and uncertainty around SG&A assumptions. Other risk emphasis varied (e.g., competitive pressure, margin swings, costs). Crucially, historical FY2026 actual values were described with estimated/projected language in all three runs. This supports a recurring *error pattern within this three-run experiment*, not a universal claim about the model.

The model did not produce three comparable, independently computed Buy/Hold/Sell recommendations or per-share price targets. Only Run 3 mentioned the external analyst consensus target of $339.17 with a Buy rating, information explicitly supplied in the experiment input. Thus a quantitative price-target standard deviation or independent recommendation agreement rate is **not available** and is not reported as zero. This limitation is itself a stability finding: valuation information was inconsistently surfaced and did not constitute an independent valuation.

## 8. Part 3: Financial Grounding Verifier Improvement

A focused rule-based verifier checks financial amounts, units, periods, and relationships against known company metrics. Version 0.1 exhibited false alarms on valid dollar figures and missed a catastrophic 2027E EBITDA mismatch. Version 0.2 improved monetary-unit normalization, detection of certain large financial mismatches, and handling of duplicated actual-versus-estimate alerts.

The same curated set of 14 passages was evaluated with both versions: 12 manually constructed examples targeting known error modes and two previously generated agent passages. The binary evaluation unit was a whole passage: a finding of one or more issues counted as an error detection. Results were:

| Dataset | Version | TP | FP | FN | TN | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Constructed (n=12) | v0.1 | 4 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 0.444 | 0.800 | 0.571 |
| Constructed (n=12) | v0.2 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| All available (n=14) | v0.1 | 6 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 0.545 | 0.857 | 0.667 |
| All available (n=14) | v0.2 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

On the full benchmark, F1 improved by **33.3 percentage points**, precision by **45.5 points**, and recall by **14.3 points**. Five false positives and one false negative were eliminated on these cases. A correct $144.6B 2026A EBITDA passage illustrates an earlier false alarm; a false $382B 2027E EBITDA passage illustrates a previously missed error.

**Evaluation limitation:** The test set is small, manually curated, and closely aligned with error patterns used to design the improvement. The two actual Agent passages were detected by both versions. Thus the perfect v0.2 score is a *targeted regression-test result*, not evidence of perfect performance on unseen LLM reports. Future work requires a larger independently labeled sample with more companies, financial concepts, and adversarial claim styles.

## Updated Part 3 — Verifier v0.3 and Real-Report Evaluation

The final improvement is an opt-in, post-generation, rule-based
Financial Grounding Verifier integrated into the report generation
pipeline. It generates JSON findings while retaining the original
LLM output. It does not automatically rewrite the report text.

Version 0.3 adds local financial-metric attribution to avoid
comparing explicitly labeled Revenue or Cost of Operations amounts
against EBITDA. It also introduces a targeted historical-versus-
forecast wording check.

On the same curated 14-passage benchmark:

| Version | TP | FP | FN | TN | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v0.1 | 6 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 0.5455 | 0.8571 | 0.6667 |
| v0.2 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| v0.3 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |

On seven original NVDA report sections, v0.2 raised three
EBITDA-mismatch alerts that manual review identified as
metric-attribution false positives: one Revenue amount and two
Cost of Operations amounts. Version 0.3 eliminated all three
without losing detection of the constructed $382B 2027E
EBITDA error.

A separate offline run completed successfully across **21 original
sections** from NVDA, AMD, and AVGO. Version 0.3 returned
zero findings, which means **no errors were flagged by the current
rules**, not that every statement was supported or correct.

This is a measured improvement in screening specificity, not
a demonstrated improvement in whole-report Grounding Rate.
Automated flagging does not change original text, and a complete
before/after claim-grounding comparison remains unavailable.

## 9. Synthesis and Limitations

This project demonstrates that output generation, numerical consistency, evidentiary grounding, and repeatability are distinct dimensions of an equity research agent. Generating five readable reports validates workflow execution. Matching 22 SEC-derived values validates selected extraction pathways but does not independently validate accounting semantics. DCF scenarios illuminate valuation uncertainty but should not be mistaken for stock-price recommendations. Grounding checks and stability runs reveal how apparently coherent narratives may still confuse fiscal-year labels or selectively mention external valuation figures. The verifier experiment shows concrete benefits from deterministic unit and relationship checks but is too small to establish broad accuracy.

Data access constraints also matter: FMP Free coverage varied by ticker, SEC XBRL concepts required mapping, fiscal years differed across issuers, and the report did not include a rigorous stock-price valuation based on independently estimated WACC, net debt, diluted shares, or a broad out-of-sample verifier benchmark.

## 10. Conclusion

FinRobot can support a multi-company research workflow efficiently, but its natural-language financial analysis should be treated as a draft requiring evidence verification. The strongest contribution is the integration of source-aware auditing, explicit scenario assumptions, repeated-generation diagnostics, and a measured verifier improvement. A future production system should verify atomic claims against primary filings, retain traceable provenance, enforce Actual/Estimate period semantics, separate analyst consensus from internal valuations, and benchmark verification against independently reviewed examples.
