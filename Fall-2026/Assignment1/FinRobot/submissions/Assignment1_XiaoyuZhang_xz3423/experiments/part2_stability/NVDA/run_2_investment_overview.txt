NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) has demonstrated remarkable financial growth over the period from 2022 through 2026, with revenue expanding from $26.9 billion in 2022A to an estimated $215.9 billion in 2026A, reflecting a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 68.3%. This rapid increase is accompanied by significant improvements in profitability metrics. Contribution profit rose from $17.5 billion in 2022A to $153.5 billion in 2026A, with contribution margin improving from 64.9% in 2022 to 71.1% in 2026. EBITDA showed a substantial increase as well, from $11.35 billion in 2022A to $144.6 billion in 2026A, and EBITDA margin expanding from 42.2% to 66.9% over the same period.

A notable trend is the reduction in SG&A expenses as a percentage of revenue, declining from 8.0% in 2022A to 2.1% in 2026A, indicating better operational efficiency. Earnings per share (EPS) also reflect strong growth, increasing from $0.39 in 2022A to $4.93 in 2026A.

When compared to key peers like AMD, Intel, Broadcom, and Qualcomm, NVIDIA's EBITDA growth far outpaces the competition, with EBITDA in 2026E projected at $144.6 billion versus peers in the range of roughly $14.7 billion to $35.8 billion. Its EV/EBITDA multiple has decreased from 81.0x in 2023 to 31.4x in 2026, suggesting some multiple contraction but still indicating a premium valuation relative to peers.

Looking forward to 2027E-2029E, revenue growth moderates to a single-digit pace (4-6% annually), with EBITDA margins continuing to improve gradually, reaching 73.5% by 2029E. EPS growth also slows but remains positive.

Key growth drivers likely include continued expansion in high-performance computing, AI, and data center demand, although specific drivers are not detailed in the data. Risks to the outlook include potential market saturation, competitive pressures, and execution risks in sustaining high margins and growth rates.

In summary, NVIDIA’s financial trajectory shows robust revenue and profitability expansion through 2026, with strong operational leverage and efficiency gains. The valuation premium reflects market expectations of continued leadership in key technology segments, but investors should be mindful of potential risks and the moderating growth expected in the later forecast years.
