# LoRA improved financial answer format without establishing forecasting skill

This article covers the retrospective validation experiment and the completed frozen final test.

I trained LoRA adapters for Qwen 2.5-7 B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R 1-Distill-Llama-8 B and Llama-3.1-8 B BASE on financial news, price history and basic financial fields from a frozen FinGPT Dow 30 dataset. Each run used 990 earlier-week training examples, one RTX 4090, int 8 base weights and 248 optimizer steps. Checkpoint selection and generation both used the same 240 validation examples, so these are development results rather than evidence of future-time generalization.

The models learned the reference-answer format. Llama BASE often generated empty templates. In the reviewed HON case, it also continued the input into unsupported future-period news; half of all BASE outputs reached the 1920-token cap. Its adapter produced coherent three-section answers,240 natural EOS endings and much higher overlap with reference text. This is a real improvement in presentation, but presentation is only part of financial analysis.

The trained Qwen and DeepSeek models each predicted the correct direction on 145 of 240 examples. Llama reached 144. Always predicting up also reached 145 because the validation period contained 145 up labels and 95 down labels. The adapters' course-parser MSE values were 7.4292,7.5083 and 7.1375; a fixed+0.5 reference-prediction baseline scored 6.3458 over the same 240 references. These encoded-reference errors are not independently verified market-return errors.

Balanced accuracy gives the classes equal weight and shows modest descriptive differences from the 50% majority baseline. That does not establish a reliable advantage: there are only eight validation weeks, rows within a week are related, and the checkpoints were selected on those rows. Model families also differ. Llama is a BASE model without a chat template, while Qwen is instruction tuned and DeepSeek is a reasoning distillation. Equal LoRA settings do not remove those differences.

The evaluation exposed a second problem: a parser can mistake format for an answer. DeepSeek's original base outputs were incompatible with the strict course layout, giving course-valid 0 despite 240 nonempty outputs. Llama's repeated Up/Down placeholders could pass that permissive parser while failing to supply a meaningful directional answer. I retained raw scores, reported valid denominators and kept separately named normalization/sensitivity variants rather than replacing inconvenient results.

Human feedback focused attention on grounding. I rated 18 selected claims, then AI-authored guidance added evidence numbering and warned against unsupported company attribution and unbenchmarked financial-ratio judgments. A fresh 48-output comparison held each model's serving settings constant between original and revised prompts. Some errors disappeared, while others persisted or appeared. Neither adapter adopted the requested citation markers. The six development cases do not demonstrate a stable grounding improvement, and AI assessments were kept separate from human ratings.

Source review explains why fluent answers still need scrutiny. The Llama adapter could correctly cite Honeywell's expansion or Travelers' EPS beat, then call a debt ratio high without a sector benchmark. It could assign an unnamed dividend-stock list to Goldman Sachs or an unnamed long-haul list to Microsoft. These are evidence gaps; a plausible narrative and a correct direction do not make the reasoning grounded.

The dataset itself limits the conclusions. Financial headers in 61 training and 3 validation examples postdate the prediction origin, and complete independent availability timestamps are missing for all 1,230 rows. This does not prove every row leaked, but disclosure does not repair the training data. Original DeepSeek targets also leave its reasoning boundary open. The retrospective preserves these facts and avoids claiming that parser changes repaired supervision.

The useful outcome is a reproducible failure analysis: complete raw outputs, selected adapters, source hashes, training evidence, explicit denominators and independent Claude reviews are retained. LoRA can teach financial-answer style while leaving weak attribution, uncalibrated predictions and data-timing problems. A later experiment would need verified information availability and grounded targets before its forecasting performance could be interpreted more strongly.

The test labels contain 137 up and 163 down. Always-up 137/300 remains the frozen train-majority comparator, selected from up 542/990 before access. Counting the observed down labels yields 163/300 for constant-down, above all six variants; this is a post-hoc label-distribution fact, not a newly selected primary baseline. The adapters predict up on 245/219/207 rows; their balanced accuracies are 0.5008/0.5134/0.5166. Qwen declines from 161/300 before LoRA to 142/300 after it. Exceeding the minority-class always-up score does not establish forecasting skill.

The course parser rejects three reference answers, so all three adapters have 300 individually parseable outputs but 297 valid answer/reference pairs. Their MSE values 8.7475/8.5724/8.5152 remain worse than the identical-subset+0.5 constant score 7.6465. Llama BASE's 234 Up/Down placeholders and 220 course-valid pairs (all parsed as up,208 zero margins) show why parser acceptance must not be called a meaningful prediction.

Test serving used Qwen 16, DeepSeek 16 and Llama 8, with smaller final batches recorded. Qwen's primary validation used batch 1, so validation/test timing and behavior are not controlled for identical serving parameters. DeepSeek's original unclosed adapter reasoning supervision remains a confound; shorter output is not equal-quality reasoning acceleration.

The subsequent frozen final test produced 1,800 answers on 300 retained identities after human authorization. All 300 results below are followed by separate 20 previously inspected and 280 other rows in the accompanying report; the other 280 are not claimed pristine. qwen base reached 161/300 under the frozen final-line rule, with 296 valid directions and 208 course-valid answers. qwen adapter reached 142/300 under the frozen final-line rule, with 300 valid directions and 297 course-valid answers. deepseek base reached 130/300 under the frozen final-line rule, with 265 valid directions and 0 course-valid answers. deepseek adapter reached 148/300 under the frozen final-line rule, with 300 valid directions and 297 course-valid answers. llama base reached 2/300 under the frozen final-line rule, with 4 valid directions and 220 course-valid answers. llama adapter reached 150/300 under the frozen final-line rule, with 300 valid directions and 297 course-valid answers. Always-up reached 137/300. Dataset labels, encoded references and availability limitations still constrain interpretation. This test was not used to choose checkpoints, prompts or parameters. The full report retains the course-valid denominators, same-subset baselines, completion diagnostics and all raw outputs.
