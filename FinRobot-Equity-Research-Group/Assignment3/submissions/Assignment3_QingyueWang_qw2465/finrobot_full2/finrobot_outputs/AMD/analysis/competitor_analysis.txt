Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) competes primarily with Intel (INTC), NVIDIA (NVDA), and Qualcomm (QCOM) in the semiconductor industry. Recent financial metrics illustrate AMD’s strong revenue growth, with a projected CAGR of 20.5% through 2027, outpacing historical averages for the sector. AMD’s contribution margin has steadily improved, from 44.9% in 2022 to an expected 52.5% in 2027, signaling enhanced operational efficiency.

However, AMD’s EBITDA margins, while improving in the forecast period (from 18.3% in 2023 to a projected 42.0% in 2027), still lag behind NVIDIA, which is expected to reach an industry-leading EBITDA of $382 billion by 2027, far exceeding AMD’s projected $8.6 billion. Intel’s earnings have been volatile, with a sharp dip in 2024 and moderate recovery thereafter. Qualcomm’s EBITDA growth is more stable, but at lower absolute levels compared to NVIDIA and Intel.

From a valuation standpoint, AMD’s EV/EBITDA multiples remain elevated relative to peers (e.g., 47.8x in 2025 vs. Intel’s 14.5x and Qualcomm’s 12.7x), reflecting strong investor growth expectations but also implying a premium that could be sensitive to execution risks. AMD’s 2023 PE ratio spiked to 278x, indicating either depressed earnings or overvaluation compared to Intel and Qualcomm.

In summary, while AMD is successfully expanding revenue and improving margins, it faces intense competition from NVIDIA, whose growth in AI and data center markets is setting a high benchmark, and from Intel and Qualcomm, which maintain strong positions in CPUs and communications. AMD’s premium valuation underscores the market’s confidence in its strategy, but also highlights the need for continued innovation and margin expansion to justify its relative multiple.
