Company Overview

Costco Wholesale Corporation (COST) is one of the world’s leading membership-based warehouse club retailers. The company operates a global chain of large-format stores that sell a wide array of merchandise, including groceries, household goods, appliances, apparel, electronics, and fuel. Costco’s business model is built on a low-cost, high-volume strategy, leveraging its buying power to negotiate favorable terms with suppliers and pass savings on to its members. Membership fees represent a significant and stable revenue stream, contributing to the company’s ability to maintain low prices and foster strong customer loyalty.

Costco’s primary products and services revolve around its warehouse clubs, where customers pay an annual membership fee to access deeply discounted merchandise. The company also offers ancillary services such as optical centers, pharmacies, travel services, and fuel stations. Its private label, Kirkland Signature, is a prominent feature, offering high-quality products across categories at competitive prices, further differentiating Costco from traditional retailers.

In terms of market position, Costco is a dominant player in the warehouse club segment, competing with companies like Walmart’s Sam’s Club, BJ’s Wholesale, and traditional retailers such as Walmart and Target. Costco’s scale, efficient operations, and disciplined cost management have enabled it to consistently outperform most peers on key metrics. Financially, Costco has demonstrated robust growth, with revenue rising from $195.9 billion in 2021 to $254.5 billion in 2024, representing a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 8.9%. EBITDA also expanded from $8.6 billion in 2021 to $12.1 billion in 2024, with EBITDA margin improving from 4.4% to 4.8% over the same period. Forecasts indicate continued growth, with revenue expected to surpass $318 billion by 2027 and EBITDA margin projected to exceed 8%.

Relative to peers, Costco’s EBITDA is second only to Walmart, and its forward EV/EBITDA multiples (over 30x in 2025) indicate a premium valuation reflecting investor confidence in its resilient model and growth prospects. Earnings per share (EPS) continues to grow, with 2024 EPS of $16.60 expected to reach $21.75 by 2027.

In summary, Costco’s distinctive membership model, focus on value and efficiency, and strong financial performance have solidified its position as a top-tier retailer, with a clear runway for continued expansion both in the U.S. and internationally.

Recent Financial Performance

For fiscal year 2024, Costco delivered revenue of $254.5 billion, up 5.0% year-over-year, following a compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) of 8.9% since 2021. Contribution profit rose to $32.1 billion, with EBITDA reaching $12.1 billion (4.8% EBITDA margin, up from 4.4% in 2023). EPS grew to $16.60, reflecting consistent bottom-line expansion. The company maintains industry-leading operational efficiency, with SG&A margin stable near 9% and contribution margin gradually increasing from 12.1% in 2022 to 12.6% in 2024.

Growth Drivers

Costco’s growth is underpinned by steady same-store sales, membership fee income, and ongoing club expansion, both domestically and internationally. The company’s ability to control costs, leverage scale, and drive high renewal rates continues to support profitability. Looking ahead, consensus forecasts project revenue to reach $318.6 billion by 2027 (CAGR ~6%), with EBITDA margin expected to expand to 8.2% by 2027, reflecting continued operating leverage and efficiency gains.

Outlook and Valuation

Costco trades at a premium, with a 2024E EV/EBITDA of 32.5x versus peers Walmart (13.6x) and Target (9.6x), justified by its superior growth and defensiveness. EBITDA is forecast to grow over 20% annually through 2027, outpacing peers, and consensus expects further margin expansion with contribution margin rising to 15.8% by 2027. While the PE ratio remains elevated (53.8x 2024E), Costco’s predictable cash flows, membership-centric model, and global growth potential support its premium valuation and long-term investment appeal.

In summary, Costco remains a best-in-class retailer, offering resilient financial performance, strong growth drivers, and attractive long-term prospects, albeit at a premium valuation.

Valuation Analysis

Costco Wholesale Corporation (COST) currently trades at premium valuation multiples relative to both its historical range and key peers. As of 2024, Costco’s PE ratio stands at 53.8x and its EV/EBITDA is 32.5x, both well above historical averages and the peer group. For comparison, Walmart (WMT) and Target (TGT) trade at EV/EBITDA multiples of 13.6x and 9.6x respectively, while BJ’s Wholesale (BJ) is at 11.2x. Even looking forward to 2025, Costco’s EV/EBITDA is projected at 30.8x, still over 1.5x higher than Walmart’s 20.0x and more than triple Target’s 9.1x.

Operationally, Costco’s growth and margin expansion partially justify its valuation premium. Revenue CAGR is estimated at 8.9% through 2027, outpacing most peers in the mature retail sector. Contribution margin is expected to rise from 12.3% in 2023 to 15.8% by 2027, and EBITDA margin is forecast to increase from 4.4% to 8.2% over the same horizon. Earnings per share are forecast to grow at a mid-teens rate, providing solid visibility for ongoing profit expansion.

Despite these strengths, the current multiples imply a “best-in-class” narrative is fully priced in, leaving little margin for error. Investors are paying a significant premium for Costco’s consistent growth, strong membership model, and margin improvement. However, relative to peers and historical norms, the stock appears overvalued on both absolute and relative bases. A fair value assessment, applying a more normalized 20–25x EV/EBITDA multiple, would imply material downside from current levels. Unless growth accelerates meaningfully beyond current forecasts or Costco expands margins even faster, the shares look expensive and may be vulnerable to multiple compression if sector sentiment shifts.
