Meta Platforms, Inc. (META) stands out among its peers—Alphabet (GOOGL), Pinterest (PINS), and Snap (SNAP)—for its robust financial performance and operational efficiency. Meta exhibits a strong revenue CAGR of 14.3% from 2021 to 2027, outpacing the general digital advertising market and reflecting the continued strength of its core platforms (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp). Its contribution margin remains consistently high (80.8%–85%), indicating efficient management of operational costs relative to revenues.

Compared to Alphabet, Meta delivers lower absolute EBITDA ($59.1B vs. $97.9B in 2023), but is rapidly narrowing the gap with higher projected EBITDA growth through 2026. While GOOGL retains scale advantages, Meta’s EBITDA margins are expected to surpass Alphabet’s, forecasted to reach 74.5% by 2027, compared to Alphabet’s more modest increases. This margin expansion is driven by Meta’s tightening SG&A expenses and operational leverage.

In contrast, both Pinterest and Snap lag far behind. Pinterest’s EBITDA remains marginal and volatile, while Snap continues to post negative EBITDA through 2026, struggling to monetize its user base effectively. Meta’s scale, profitability, and margin profile are several magnitudes above these smaller peers.

On valuation, Meta’s EV/EBITDA ratios remain competitive and generally lower than Alphabet’s (16.4x for 2025E vs. 21.2x for GOOGL), suggesting Meta is attractively valued relative to its earnings power. Meanwhile, Pinterest and Snap show erratic or negative multiples, reflecting investor skepticism about their long-term profitability.

Overall, Meta’s combination of strong revenue growth, industry-leading margins, efficient cost structure, and attractive valuation positions it as a clear leader among social media and digital advertising peers. Its financial performance is second only to Alphabet, with a trajectory that may further close the profitability gap in the coming years.
