Tesla, Inc. (TSLA) continues to stand out in the electric vehicle (EV) sector with robust revenue growth and significant market presence, but recent financial metrics reveal growing challenges relative to its peers.

Tesla's revenue CAGR from 2021 to 2027 is projected at 15.2%, underscoring strong topline growth in a maturing market. However, after rapid expansion in 2021–2022, revenue growth has decelerated sharply (0.9% in 2024 and -2.9% in 2025), indicating increasing market saturation and competitive pressures. In comparison, General Motors (GM) and Ford (F), Tesla’s primary US-based automotive peers, have more stable, if lower, EV segment growth rates but benefit from diversified portfolios and established market positions.

Tesla's EBITDA margin peaked in 2022 at 21.7% but dropped to 15.3% in 2023 and is projected to further decline to 12.4% in 2025, before recovering slowly. This margin compression reflects rising operational costs and pricing pressures. By contrast, GM consistently leads the peer group in EBITDA, maintaining figures above $16B through 2026 and EBITDA margins in the 6–10% range, while Ford follows with margins slightly lower than Tesla’s, but with less volatility.

Tesla’s valuation remains elevated, with a 2023 PE ratio of 52.6 and an EV/EBITDA multiple of 52.8, far exceeding GM (EV/EBITDA 7.2) and Ford (14.7). This premium reflects strong investor confidence in Tesla’s technology leadership and growth prospects, but it also exposes the stock to downside risk if growth or margins disappoint.

Operationally, Tesla remains lean, with a lower SG&A margin (4.7–8.4%) compared to legacy automakers, reflecting its direct-to-consumer sales model and digital-first approach. However, declining contribution and EBITDA margins highlight the need for renewed product innovation or cost control to defend profitability as competition intensifies.

In summary, while Tesla leads the EV market in scale and remains highly valued, its recent slowing growth, shrinking margins, and premium valuation versus GM and Ford underscore the importance of execution and differentiation as the industry matures.
