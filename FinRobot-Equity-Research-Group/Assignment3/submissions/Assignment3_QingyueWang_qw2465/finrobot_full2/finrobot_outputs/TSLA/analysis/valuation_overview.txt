Tesla, Inc. (TSLA) is currently trading at valuation multiples that are significantly above its automotive peers. As of 2023, TSLA’s PE ratio stands at 52.6x, rising to over 180x in 2024 and projected to remain above 300x through 2027, despite EPS declines. In comparison, legacy automaker Ford (F) and General Motors (GM) trade at much lower EV/EBITDA multiples. For 2023, Tesla’s EV/EBITDA is 52.8x, far above GM’s 7.2x and Ford’s 14.7x. This premium persists in forward years; in 2025, Tesla’s EV/EBITDA is estimated at 122.6x, while GM and Ford are at 10.5x and 23.0x, respectively.

Tesla’s operating performance is showing some pressure. Revenue growth has decelerated sharply from 51.4% in 2022 to less than 1% in 2024, and is projected at only mid-single digits through 2027. EBITDA margin has also compressed from a peak of 21.7% in 2022 to an estimated 13.3% in 2025, reflecting both slowing top-line growth and rising costs. While Tesla's contribution margins are forecast to recover mildly, overall profitability is under pressure, and EPS is expected to decline in 2024 and 2025 before recovering modestly.

Compared to peers, Tesla’s valuation is difficult to justify on current earnings growth and margin trends. The company still commands a large premium based on its perceived long-term growth potential, innovation, and leadership in EVs and autonomy. However, with growth slowing and profitability pressured, the risk of multiple compression is high if Tesla does not reaccelerate growth or improve margins. Based on peer multiples and the current growth outlook, Tesla appears overvalued relative to the sector, with fair value likely well below current trading levels unless there is a material change in the trajectory of earnings or margins.
