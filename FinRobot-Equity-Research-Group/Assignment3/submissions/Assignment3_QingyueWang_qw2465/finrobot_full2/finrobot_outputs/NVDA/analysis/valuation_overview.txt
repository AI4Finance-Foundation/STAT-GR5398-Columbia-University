NVIDIA's current valuation reflects its dominant position in AI and accelerated computing. The company’s revenue growth has been extraordinary, with a CAGR of 68.3% projected from 2022 to 2027. EBITDA margins are expected to expand from 22.2% in 2023 to over 70% by 2027, underscoring the scalability of NVIDIA’s business model as revenue surges and operating leverage improves.

The company’s PE ratio has moderated from the triple-digit highs of 2023 (109x) to a projected 32x by 2027, reflecting both rapid earnings growth and some normalization in investor expectations. Similarly, the EV/EBITDA multiple is forecast to decline from 81x in 2023 to 31x in 2026, indicating that earnings are catching up with the company’s elevated market capitalization.

Compared to peers, NVIDIA trades at a premium. For 2025, NVIDIA’s EV/EBITDA is 33.8x vs. AMD at 47.8x, AVGO at 50.6x, and INTC at 14.5x. However, NVIDIA’s EBITDA generation far surpasses peers, with 2025 EBITDA estimated at $86.1B—more than double Broadcom (AVGO) and an order of magnitude above AMD and Intel. While the premium is substantial, it is justified by NVIDIA’s superior growth, margin profile, and market leadership in high-demand segments like AI.

On fair value, while NVIDIA’s multiples remain high by historical tech standards, they are becoming more reasonable as earnings ramp. If management delivers on growth, the stock could justify a forward PE in the 30s and an EV/EBITDA in the low 30s—near current 2026 projections. However, any stumble in execution or a slowdown in AI demand could compress these multiples. In sum, NVIDIA is richly valued, but current metrics are defensible given its exceptional growth and profitability profile.
