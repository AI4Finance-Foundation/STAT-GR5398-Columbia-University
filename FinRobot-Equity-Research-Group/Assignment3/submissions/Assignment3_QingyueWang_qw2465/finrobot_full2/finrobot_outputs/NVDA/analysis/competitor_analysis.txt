NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) stands out among its peers—AMD, Broadcom (AVGO), and Intel (INTC)—as the clear leader in both growth trajectory and profitability, especially in recent years driven by AI and data center demand.

Financially, NVIDIA’s revenue growth is exceptional, with a forecasted CAGR of 68.3% from 2024 to 2027, far outpacing the broader semiconductor industry. The company’s revenue more than doubled from $60.9B in 2024 to a projected $130.5B in 2025, and is expected to reach nearly $250B by 2027. This explosive growth dwarfs what is projected for AMD, AVGO, and INTC, whose revenues and EBITDA expand at a much slower pace.

In terms of profitability, NVIDIA’s EBITDA margin reached 58.4% in 2024 and is projected to climb above 70% by 2027, significantly higher than peers. For comparison, even AVGO, the most profitable among NVIDIA’s competitors, is projected to reach only about $43.3B EBITDA by 2027, against NVIDIA’s $382B. AMD and INTC lag further behind, with projected 2027 EBITDA of $8.6B and $47.4B, respectively.

Valuation remains elevated for NVIDIA, with EV/EBITDA multiples peaking at 81x in 2023 and forecast to normalize to 31x by 2026—still a premium over peers, reflecting market confidence in NVIDIA’s sustained growth and leadership in high-margin AI accelerators. Competitors trade at much lower multiples, highlighting their more modest growth prospects.

In summary, NVIDIA is the fastest-growing and most profitable major chipmaker, commanding a premium valuation. Its leadership in AI and data center segments sets it apart from AMD, AVGO, and INTC, who remain competitive but are unable to match NVIDIA’s scale, growth, or margins in the current cycle.
