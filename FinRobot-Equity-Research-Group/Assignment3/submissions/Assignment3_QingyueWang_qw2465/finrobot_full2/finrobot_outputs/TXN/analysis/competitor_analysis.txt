Texas Instruments (TI) stands as a premier analog and embedded semiconductor manufacturer, competing closely with Analog Devices (ADI), Microchip Technology (MCHP), and Qualcomm (QCOM). Analyzing recent financials, TI’s performance has softened since its 2022 peak, with revenues declining from $20.0B in 2022 to $15.6B projected in 2024 before a recovery to $17.7B in 2025. Revenue CAGR for 2021–2027 is slightly negative at -0.9%, reflecting sector-wide cyclical weakness.

In terms of profitability, TI remains robust but is under pressure. Contribution and EBITDA margins have trended down (EBITDA margin from 56.0% in 2022 to 46.7% in 2025E), though they remain industry-leading. By comparison, ADI’s projected 2025 EBITDA is $5.03B, MCHP’s $1.04B, and QCOM’s $14.9B. QCOM dwarfs its peers on EBITDA, though it derives substantial revenue from digital and wireless, not TI’s analog core.

Valuation-wise, TI trades at a premium: its forward EV/EBITDA is 20.6x for 2025, above QCOM’s 12.7x and more aligned with ADI’s 24.2x. This suggests investors reward TI for its consistent margins and broad customer base, despite slower growth. However, recent margin compression and negative revenue growth compare unfavorably to peers like QCOM, which shows stronger EBITDA growth.

TI’s competitive strengths remain its scale, cost discipline, and analog market leadership. Risks include margin pressure from rising costs and slower end-market recovery. Compared to ADI and MCHP, TI is more resilient but less dynamic than QCOM. Over the next few years, TI’s recovery hinges on demand normalization and its ability to defend margins amid industry volatility.
