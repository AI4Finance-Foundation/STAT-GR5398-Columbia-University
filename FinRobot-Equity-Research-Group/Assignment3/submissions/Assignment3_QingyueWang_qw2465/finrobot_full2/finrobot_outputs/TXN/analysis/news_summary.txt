Texas Instruments Incorporated (TXN) has emerged as a standout performer in 2026, with its stock soaring an impressive 62% year-to-date. This rally has been fueled by robust momentum in the semiconductor sector, particularly as investor optimism around artificial intelligence (AI) and embedded systems continues to lift chipmakers. Despite the recent run-up, some analysts note that Texas Instruments is no longer the undisputed sector benchmark, with competitors like ON Semiconductor showing strong results in recent months.

Financially, TXN faced a challenging 2023 and early 2024, with revenue declining from $20.0 billion in 2022 to $15.6 billion in 2024, reflecting a two-year contraction driven by cyclical headwinds and muted demand in some end-markets. However, consensus estimates point to a recovery, with revenues expected to rebound to $17.7 billion in 2025 and compound annual growth resuming through 2027. Margins, while compressed from 2021-2023 highs, are forecast to gradually recover as well, with contribution and EBITDA margins expected to inch up, reflecting improved operating leverage.

Relative to peers such as Analog Devices (ADI), Microchip (MCHP), and Qualcomm (QCOM), TXN remains competitive in terms of EBITDA and maintains a premium EV/EBITDA multiple, a testament to its strong brand, diversified product base, and perceived resilience. However, valuation is becoming a concern, with the forward P/E ratio elevated (around 30x for 2025E) and the EV/EBITDA premium widening.

Retail sentiment is moderately bullish, especially on X.com, where buzz is high but trending downward, suggesting some caution after the recent rally. Overall, while TXN’s fundamentals are stabilizing and long-term prospects remain intact, the sharp run-up in share price and premium valuation may limit near-term upside, making it more attractive for long-term investors than for those seeking immediate gains.
