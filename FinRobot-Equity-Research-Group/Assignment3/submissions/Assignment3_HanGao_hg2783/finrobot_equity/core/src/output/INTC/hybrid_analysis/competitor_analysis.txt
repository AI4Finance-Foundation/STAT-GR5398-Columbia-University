Intel Corporation has faced significant financial headwinds over the past several years, as indicated by its declining revenues, margins, and profitability. From 2021 to 2023, revenues dropped sharply from $79.0 billion to $54.2 billion, representing a compounded annual decline of 9.6%. This is in stark contrast to peers such as AMD, NVIDIA, and TSMC, which have generally reported robust growth over the same period driven by demand for AI, data centers, and advanced process technology.

Intel’s EBITDA margins have also deteriorated, falling from 42.9% in 2021 to just 2.3% in 2024, with some recovery projected in the coming years but still well below historical levels and industry leaders. NVIDIA, for example, recently posted EBITDA margins above 40%, while TSMC consistently delivers margins in the 40-50% range. Intel’s declining contribution margin—from 55.4% in 2021 to 32.7% in 2024—further underscores competitive pressures and operational challenges, likely stemming from delays in advanced manufacturing nodes and increased competition in both PC and data center markets.

SG&A as a percentage of revenue is trending downward, indicating some efforts towards cost control; however, this has not offset the significant drop in profitability. Earnings per share have turned negative, and with no meaningful PE ratio, Intel currently lags competitors who are delivering both growth and profitability.

Looking forward, modest revenue growth is forecasted (4-6% CAGR from 2025E-2027E), but the company’s financial health remains challenged relative to peers. Intel’s turnaround will depend on its ability to regain process leadership, improve margins, and capitalize on emerging growth markets, where competitors are currently outpacing it.
