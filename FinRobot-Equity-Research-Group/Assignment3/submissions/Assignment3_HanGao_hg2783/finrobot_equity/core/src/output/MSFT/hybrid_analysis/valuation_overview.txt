Microsoft’s recent financial performance shows robust growth, with revenue rising from $168.1B in 2021 to $281.7B in 2025, reflecting a 13.9% CAGR. Margins remain strong: contribution margin is consistently near 69% and EBITDA margin expands from 50.6% to 56.9% over this period, pointing to effective operating leverage and cost control. SG&A as a percentage of revenue declines, supporting improving profitability.

Future revenue growth assumptions are more moderate, with 2025–2027E annual growth rates forecast at 5.0–6.0%. These lower growth rates are supported by drivers identified in the SEC filings: continued product innovation, new launches, and the ability to monetize an expanding portfolio. Management’s emphasis on strategic expansion, demand resilience, and disciplined cost structure underpins these forward-looking assumptions, and the filings suggest that margin improvement can further support EPS growth.

Relative to large-cap technology peers, Microsoft’s scale, margins, and product breadth are competitive strengths. Its current margin structure and anticipated continued expansion position it well versus peers who may face similar macro or competitive pressures but lack Microsoft’s diversification and margin discipline.

Risks cited in SEC filings—macroeconomic headwinds, inflation, supply chain, and competition—are material and could constrain upside, but Microsoft’s historical execution and margin discipline provide a degree of downside protection. The fair value context for Microsoft reflects high quality and resilient earnings, with future growth slowing but underpinned by credible revenue drivers. Overall, the revenue growth assumptions appear reasonable and supported by both recent trends and identified catalysts, while profitability metrics suggest Microsoft remains a premium-valued, sector-leading franchise.
