Snowflake Inc. currently trades at elevated valuation multiples, reflecting its rapid historical growth and strong market positioning, but traditional metrics such as PE ratio are not meaningful given continued negative EPS through 2027E. The company’s revenue grew at a 40.9% CAGR from 2022A to 2026A, with revenue rising from $1.2B to $4.7B. However, the outlook calls for a marked deceleration: consensus revenue growth slows to 5.0% in 2025E, 6.0% in 2026E, and 4.0% in 2027E, suggesting the business is entering a maturing phase.

Peer comparison would likely highlight Snowflake’s premium valuation versus other SaaS and data cloud peers, justified in part by its superior historical growth, but increasingly challenged as growth normalizes and profitability only modestly improves. Contribution margin expands steadily (from 62.4% in 2022A to 70.2% in 2027E), reflecting operating leverage and cost discipline, while EBITDA margin turns positive in 2025E (12.9%) and improves to 15.9% by 2027E. Yet, SG&A remains elevated at over 54% of revenue even by 2027E, indicating persistent cost intensity.

SEC filings reinforce that management is focused on innovation, expansion, and margin discipline as key levers, but also flag macroeconomic, competitive, and execution risks that may cap upside. The revenue growth assumptions for 2025–2027, while much lower than historical rates, appear supported by ongoing portfolio and product expansion, as well as resilient demand cited in filings. However, given slowing growth, persistent losses, and intensifying risks, Snowflake’s current valuation likely embeds significant execution expectations. Upside to fair value could materialize if new products or markets drive re-acceleration, but risks of further multiple compression remain as growth slows and profitability improvements are gradual.
