Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL) delivered a strong first quarter for 2026, beating Wall Street estimates on both revenue and earnings. The company reported quarterly EPS of $5.11, nearly doubling the consensus estimate of $2.64 and representing significant year-over-year improvement from $2.81 per share. Total revenue came in at $109.9 billion, driven by robust performance across multiple business segments.

The headline story was Google Cloud, which surpassed $20 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time, marking a 63% year-over-year increase. Management noted that growth was actually capacity-constrained, suggesting even stronger demand exists than reported results reflect. This signals meaningful upside potential as infrastructure investments come online. The company also reported a surging backlog, reinforcing confidence in sustained cloud momentum.

Subscription services emerged as another key growth driver, with Google adding 25 million paid subscriptions in Q1, bringing the total to 350 million. YouTube and Google One AI features were the primary contributors, underscoring the monetization potential of Alphabet's AI integrations beyond core search.

On the advertising front, Alphabet is actively exploring ad placements within AI Mode and has not ruled out monetizing the Gemini app directly. A new partnership with Stripe enables commerce transactions within Gemini and AI Mode, creating an additional revenue layer from agentic AI interactions.

Financially, the long-term outlook remains compelling. EBITDA margins are projected to expand from 44.7% in 2025 to 51.7% by 2027, reflecting operating leverage and AI-driven efficiency. EPS is forecast to grow steadily toward $13.01 by 2027.

Retail sentiment is broadly positive, with X.com and Polymarket showing bullish readings above 65%, though Reddit sentiment is more cautious at 38% bullish, indicating some divergence among retail investor communities. Overall, Alphabet presents a strong investment case anchored by cloud growth, AI monetization, and expanding margins.
