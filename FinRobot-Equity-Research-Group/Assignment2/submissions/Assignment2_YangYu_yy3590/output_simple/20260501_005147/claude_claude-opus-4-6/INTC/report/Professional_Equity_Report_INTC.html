Company Overview

Intel Corporation is the world's largest integrated semiconductor manufacturer and one of the most iconic names in the technology industry. Founded in 1968 and headquartered in Santa Clara, California, Intel designs and manufactures microprocessors, chipsets, and related technologies that power personal computers, data centers, networking equipment, and an expanding range of connected devices. The company operates across several key segments, including Client Computing Group, Data Center and AI, Network and Edge, Mobileye (autonomous driving technology), and Intel Foundry Services, which represents the company's ambitious push to become a contract chip manufacturer for third parties.

Intel's business model historically relied on its dominant position in x86 processor architecture for PCs and servers, combined with its vertically integrated manufacturing capability. However, the company has faced significant competitive pressure in recent years from AMD in traditional computing markets and from NVIDIA in the rapidly growing artificial intelligence and accelerated computing segments. The financial data starkly illustrates this competitive erosion. Revenue declined from approximately 79 billion dollars in 2021 to roughly 52.9 billion dollars in 2025, representing a negative compound annual growth rate of 9.6 percent. This sustained top-line contraction reflects lost market share and a broader industry shift toward AI-centric workloads where Intel has lagged.

Profitability has deteriorated substantially. EBITDA margins collapsed from 42.9 percent in 2021 to just 2.3 percent in 2024, though a partial recovery to 27.2 percent occurred in 2025, likely driven by cost restructuring efforts. Earnings per share turned negative in 2024 at negative 4.38 dollars and remained slightly negative in 2025, with forward estimates projecting continued losses through 2028. The peer comparison is particularly telling. NVIDIA's EBITDA surged from roughly 6 billion dollars in 2023 to over 86 billion dollars in 2025, dwarfing Intel's recovery, while AMD has also steadily grown its profitability.

Intel's valuation reflects investor skepticism. Its EV/EBITDA ratio has been volatile, spiking to nearly 107 times in 2024 during the earnings trough before normalizing to approximately 14.5 times in 2025, well below AMD and NVIDIA multiples. Forward estimates project modest 5 percent annual revenue growth through 2028 with gradually improving margins, but the company faces an uncertain path. Intel's turnaround hinges on the success of its foundry services strategy, competitive product execution in AI and computing, and disciplined capital allocation amid massive fabrication investments.

Despite the EBITDA recovery, Intel continues to report negative earnings per share, posting negative 0.06 dollars in 2025. The competitive landscape remains challenging. Looking ahead, consensus estimates project modest 5 percent annual revenue growth through 2028 with continued margin expansion toward 30.6 percent EBITDA margins. The key question is whether Intel's foundry investments and product roadmap can drive a return to positive net income. Until EPS turns positive, the stock remains a turnaround story with meaningful execution risk. Investors should monitor progress in the foundry business and market share trends in data center and client computing segments.

Analysis

Intel Corporation faces a challenging competitive landscape against peers AMD and NVIDIA, with its financial trajectory revealing significant structural pressures over the past several years. From 2021 to 2025, Intel's revenue declined at a compound annual rate of negative 9.6 percent, falling from roughly 79 billion dollars to approximately 53 billion dollars. This contraction stands in stark contrast to NVIDIA, which has experienced explosive growth, with EBITDA surging from around 11.4 billion in 2022 to an extraordinary 86.1 billion in 2025. AMD has also shown steady improvement, growing its EBITDA from 4.2 billion in 2021 to 7.3 billion in 2025.

Intel's profitability has been severely compressed. Its EBITDA margin collapsed from 42.9 percent in 2021 to just 2.3 percent in 2024 before partially recovering to 27.2 percent in 2025. Meanwhile, the company has posted negative earnings per share in 2024 and 2025, rendering its PE ratio meaningless and signaling deep operational difficulties. The projected estimates through 2028 show only modest revenue growth of 5 percent annually and continued negative EPS, suggesting the turnaround will be protracted.

From a valuation perspective, Intel traded at an EV to EBITDA multiple of 14.5 in 2025, significantly below AMD at 47.8 and NVIDIA at 33.8. While this appears cheap on the surface, it reflects the market's skepticism about Intel's ability to regain competitiveness in both data center and client computing segments. NVIDIA dominates the AI accelerator market with unmatched scale, while AMD continues to gain share in CPUs and GPUs.

Intel's declining contribution margin, from 55.4 percent to 34.8 percent over the period, underscores rising cost pressures tied to its ambitious foundry investments. The company must demonstrate meaningful execution on process technology and product competitiveness to close the widening gap with its peers. Without a credible path to profitability, Intel risks further market share erosion and sustained valuation discounts relative to AMD and NVIDIA.

Risk Factors

# Intel Corporation (INTC) — Key Investment Risks

  • ‣ Persistent Revenue Decline with No Clear Inflection Point: Revenue has contracted at a -9.6% CAGR from $79B (2021) to $52.9B (2025), and forward estimates assume only a modest 5% annual recovery — well below the growth rates of peers AMD and especially NVIDIA, whose EBITDA is projected to reach $382B by 2027 versus Intel's $47B, suggesting Intel is losing structural market share in data center and AI semiconductors.
  • ‣ Sustained Negative Earnings and No Path to Profitability on an EPS Basis: EPS has deteriorated from $4.89 (2021) to effectively zero/-negative territory (-$0.06 in 2025), and projections through 2028 show continued negative EPS (-$0.07), implying that massive capital expenditures (foundry buildout, process node investments) and elevated cost of operations (~65% of revenue) are consuming any EBITDA improvement before it reaches the bottom line — creating indefinite shareholder value destruction.
  • ‣ Severe Competitive Displacement in High-Growth AI/Data Center Markets: NVIDIA's EBITDA exploded from ~$6B (2023) to $86B (2025) and is projected at $382B (2027), while AMD also shows steady growth to $8.6B; Intel's EBITDA collapsed to $1.2B in 2024 before recovering, but remains a fraction of peers, indicating Intel is largely excluded from the AI accelerator boom that is driving industry value creation.
  • ‣ Margin Compression and Capital Intensity Risk from Foundry Strategy: Contribution margin has eroded from 55.4% (2021) to 34.8% (2025), and while forward estimates project gradual recovery to 37.8%, this remains far below historical levels; Intel's IDM 2.0/foundry strategy requires tens of billions in capex (new fabs in Ohio, Arizona, Germany) that depress free cash flow and may never achieve competitive returns if Intel fails to win meaningful external foundry customers against TSMC.
  • ‣ Valuation Anomaly Signals Deep Market Skepticism: The PE ratio has swung from ~10x (2021) to deeply negative/meaningless levels (-658x in 2025), and the EV/EBITDA multiple spiked to 107x in 2024 before normalizing to ~14.5x in 2025 — still above its 2021-2022 range of 6.5-7x on much lower absolute EBITDA; this suggests the market is pricing in turnaround optionality that carries significant execution risk, and any delay in process technology milestones (e.g., Intel 18A yield issues) or foundry customer wins could trigger a sharp de-rating.

Key Takeaways

Revenue Growth

Intel has experienced a prolonged revenue decline, with a -9.6% CAGR from 2021A ($79.0B) to 2025A ($52.9B), driven by consecutive years of double-digit and single-digit declines as the company lost market share to AMD and NVIDIA. The projected 5.0% annual growth from 2026E–2028E represents a modest stabilization, but revenue remains far below its 2021 peak, and the assumed recovery appears conservative relative to the competitive headwinds Intel faces.

Gross Profit Margin

Intel's contribution (gross profit) margin deteriorated sharply from 55.4% in 2021A to a trough of 32.7% in 2024A, reflecting rising cost pressures from its ambitious foundry buildout and a less favorable product mix. A gradual recovery to 34.8% in 2025A and a projected 37.8% by 2028E suggests incremental improvement, but margins remain well below historical levels, indicating Intel has yet to regain meaningful pricing power or manufacturing cost efficiency.

SG&A Expense Margin

SG&A margin has improved notably from a peak of 11.1% in 2022A to 8.7% in 2025A, with projections declining further to 7.2% by 2028E, reflecting Intel's aggressive cost-cutting and restructuring initiatives. This disciplined overhead reduction is one of the few bright spots in Intel's financial profile, as absolute SG&A spending has dropped from $7.0B to $4.6B, demonstrating management's commitment to operational efficiency even amid revenue pressure.

EBITDA Margin

EBITDA margin collapsed from 42.9% in 2021A to a near-breakeven 2.3% in 2024A before rebounding to 27.2% in 2025A, signaling a partial recovery but still far from peak profitability. Projected margins of 27.6%–30.6% through 2028E remain significantly below 2021 levels, and Intel's EV/EBITDA of 14.5x in 2025A—while improved from the distressed 106.9x in 2024A—still reflects investor skepticism compared to NVIDIA's superior scale, with NVIDIA generating ~6x Intel's EBITDA by 2025 and commanding a higher but arguably more justified valuation multiple.
