Company Overview

NVIDIA Corporation is the world's leading designer of graphics processing units (GPUs) and a dominant force in accelerated computing, headquartered in Santa Clara, California. Originally founded in 1993 to serve the PC gaming graphics market, NVIDIA has transformed itself into the preeminent supplier of the computing infrastructure powering artificial intelligence, data centers, autonomous vehicles, and professional visualization workloads.

NVIDIA's business model centers on designing high-performance semiconductor chips, which are manufactured by foundry partners such as TSMC, and sold alongside a rich software ecosystem including its CUDA platform, networking solutions, and AI frameworks. Its primary revenue segments include Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, and Automotive. In recent years, the Data Center segment, driven by insatiable demand for AI training and inference hardware, has become the overwhelming growth engine for the company.

NVIDIA's market position is unrivaled in the AI accelerator space. Its flagship products, including the H100, A100, and the newer Blackwell architecture GPUs, are considered the gold standard for AI workloads. This dominance is reflected in the financial data. Revenue surged from approximately 27 billion dollars in fiscal 2022 and 2023 to nearly 61 billion in fiscal 2024, then more than doubled to 130.5 billion in fiscal 2025, reaching roughly 216 billion in fiscal 2026, representing a staggering compound annual growth rate of 68.3 percent over the period. EBITDA followed a similarly explosive trajectory, rising from roughly 6 billion in the trough year of 2023 to 144.6 billion in fiscal 2026, with EBITDA margins expanding from 22.2 percent to 66.9 percent, showcasing extraordinary operating leverage.

Compared to peers AMD and Intel, NVIDIA's profitability is in a league of its own. Its fiscal 2025 EBITDA of 86.1 billion dwarfed AMD's 7.3 billion and Intel's 14.4 billion. Despite trading at a premium EV/EBITDA multiple relative to Intel, NVIDIA's valuation has actually compressed from over 81 times in 2023 to roughly 31 times by 2026, reflecting earnings growth outpacing stock price appreciation.

Earnings per share grew from 0.18 dollars in fiscal 2023 to 4.93 dollars in fiscal 2026, with consensus estimates projecting continued growth to 5.88 dollars by fiscal 2029. Forward estimates project more modest revenue growth of around 5 percent annually from fiscal 2027 onward, suggesting analysts anticipate a normalization after the initial AI infrastructure buildout, though margin expansion is expected to continue with EBITDA margins reaching an estimated 73.5 percent by fiscal 2029. NVIDIA remains the definitive beneficiary of the global AI revolution.

However, driven by the artificial intelligence boom, Nvidia's financial profile transformed. This dominance is projected to compound by 2025, with Nvidia forecasting $86.1 billion in EBITDA, dwarfing AMD's $7.2 billion and Intel's $14.3 billion.

Nvidia's superior performance is underpinned by a staggering 68.3 percent revenue compound annual growth rate and rapidly expanding profitability. Ultimately, Nvidia operates in a league of its own, combining hyper-growth, expanding margins, and an increasingly compelling valuation relative to its closest competitors.

Risk Factors

# NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) — Key Investment Risks

  • ‣ Severe Revenue Growth Deceleration: Revenue growth is projected to collapse from 65.5% (2026A) to just 5.0% annually (2027E–2029E), yet the stock trades at a forward PE of ~35x. If the AI infrastructure spending cycle plateaus or contracts faster than modeled, the current valuation multiple — still elevated relative to peers like Intel (EV/EBITDA ~14.5x) — could compress sharply, creating significant downside risk.
  • ‣ Unsustainably Optimistic Margin Expansion Assumptions: The projections assume EBITDA margins expand from 66.9% (2026A) to 73.5% (2029E) while SG&A margins shrink to an implausibly low 0.6% of revenue. This implies near-elimination of selling and administrative functions at a $250B revenue company, suggesting the model may overstate future profitability; any normalization of operating expenses (regulatory compliance, sales infrastructure for new markets) would materially reduce projected earnings.
  • ‣ Customer Concentration & Hyperscaler Vertical Integration: NVIDIA's revenue is heavily dependent on a handful of hyperscale cloud customers (Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon) who are actively developing custom AI accelerators (e.g., Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia). If even one or two major customers shift meaningful workloads to in-house silicon, the 5% growth assumption for 2027E–2029E could prove optimistic, and cost-of-operations leverage would deteriorate.
  • ‣ Competitive Threat from AMD and Resurgent Intel: AMD's EBITDA is growing rapidly (from $5.3B in 2024 to a projected $8.6B in 2027), and Intel is projecting a dramatic recovery to $47.4B EBITDA by 2027 — suggesting both competitors are investing aggressively in data center GPUs and AI accelerators. NVIDIA's current ~75% data center GPU market share faces credible erosion, which could pressure both pricing power and the contribution margins underpinning the entire financial model.
  • ‣ Regulatory and Geopolitical Export Restrictions: U.S. government export controls already restrict NVIDIA's ability to sell advanced AI chips (H100/H200/Blackwell) to China, which historically represented a significant revenue stream. Escalation of trade restrictions — or expansion to other markets (Middle East, Southeast Asia) — poses a direct top-line risk that is not captured in the steady 5% growth projection, while simultaneously incentivizing foreign competitors and state-sponsored chip development programs.

Key Takeaways

Revenue Growth

NVIDIA experienced extraordinary revenue growth of 125.9% in 2024A and 114.2% in 2025A, driven by surging demand for AI/data center GPUs, but growth is projected to decelerate sharply to just 5.0% annually from 2027E–2029E. The 7-year CAGR of 68.3% is exceptional, though the dramatic slowdown in projected growth suggests the company may be approaching a maturation phase or that forward estimates are conservatively modeled.

Gross Profit Margin

Contribution (gross profit) margin recovered strongly from a trough of 56.9% in 2023A to 75.0% in 2025A, reflecting NVIDIA's exceptional pricing power in the AI accelerator market and favorable product mix shift toward high-margin data center products. Projected margins of 72–74% through 2029E indicate sustained strength, though the slight dip in 2026A (71.1%) may reflect competitive pressures or product transition costs.

SG&A Expense Margin

SG&A margin has declined dramatically from 9.0% in 2023A to 2.1% in 2026A and is projected to fall to just 0.6% by 2029E, showcasing tremendous operating leverage as revenue scales far faster than selling and administrative costs. However, the projected absolute decline in SG&A dollars from $4.6B (2026A) to $1.5B (2029E) appears aggressive and potentially unsustainable, as a company of NVIDIA's scale would likely need to maintain or grow these investments to support its expanding customer base and competitive positioning.

EBITDA Margin

EBITDA margin has expanded remarkably from a low of 22.2% in 2023A to 66.9% in 2026A, with projections reaching 73.5% by 2029E — margins that are virtually unheard of at this revenue scale and far exceed peers like AMD (mid-20s%) and Intel (low-to-mid teens%). NVIDIA's EV/EBITDA multiple of ~31x in 2026A, while lower than its historical peaks, still commands a significant premium over Intel (14.5x) and AMD (47.8x in 2025), reflecting the market's confidence in NVIDIA's dominant competitive moat in AI infrastructure.
