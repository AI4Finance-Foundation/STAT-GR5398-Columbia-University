- **Slowing Revenue Growth**: Apple’s revenue growth has decelerated significantly, with only 2.0% growth in 2024A and a negative growth rate in 2023A (-2.8%), as shown in the financial profile. This trend, highlighted in recent SEC filings, signals maturing core markets and increasing reliance on incremental innovation rather than breakthrough products.

- **Dependence on Contribution Margin Expansion**: Projected increases in contribution and EBITDA margins (reaching nearly 50% and 44.8% by 2027E, respectively) indicate that future profitability is increasingly reliant on cost control rather than top-line growth. This exposes Apple to risk if cost pressures (such as supply chain or labor) intensify or if operational efficiencies are not realized as forecasted.

- **EPS Stagnation**: Earnings per share (EPS) growth has stalled, with virtually no change between 2022A and 2024A (6.15 to 6.11), raising concerns about the effectiveness of capital allocation and share repurchases. Stagnant EPS could undermine investor confidence, especially if revenue growth remains weak.

- **Escalating SG&A Expenses**: SG&A costs have risen steadily (from $21.97B in 2021A to $27.6B in 2025A), outpacing revenue growth in some periods. While SG&A margin is projected to decline, persistent increases in absolute SG&A spending may pressure margins if revenue does not accelerate as anticipated.

- **Market Saturation and Product Cycle Risk**: The modest revenue growth and high contribution margins suggest a saturated market for Apple’s primary products. Any misstep in product launches, slower adoption of new devices, or weaker-than-expected demand could have an outsized impact on financial performance, as highlighted in recent company disclosures.
