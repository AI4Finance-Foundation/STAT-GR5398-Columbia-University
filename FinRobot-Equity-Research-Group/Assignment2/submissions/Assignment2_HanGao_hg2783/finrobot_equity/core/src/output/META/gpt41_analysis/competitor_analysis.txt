Meta Platforms, Inc. (META) remains a strong performer among its large-cap tech peers—Apple (AAPL), Amazon (AMZN), Alphabet (GOOGL), and Microsoft (MSFT)—with robust financial metrics and improving profitability.

Revenue growth for META rebounded strongly after a slight decline in 2022 (-1.1%), reaching 15.7% in 2023 and projected to accelerate further in 2024-2025 (21.9% and 22.2% respectively). This outpaces the typical growth rates of its peers, reflecting META’s strong user engagement and advertising business.

META’s EBITDA margin has shown significant improvement, rising from 32.3% in 2022 to a projected 74.5% by 2027. This trajectory is notably higher than peers, with most competitors maintaining margins in the 30%-60% range. The company’s contribution margin is also expanding, suggesting efficient cost management, particularly with SG&A as a percentage of revenue decreasing from 23.2% (2022) to a projected 10.5% (2027).

Comparing EBITDA, META still lags behind AAPL, MSFT, and GOOGL in absolute terms through 2026, but its growth rate is strong and accelerating. For instance, META’s EBITDA is projected to nearly triple from $59.1B in 2023 to $128.7B in 2026, closing the gap with peers.

On valuation, META’s EV/EBITDA ratio is generally lower than AAPL and MSFT, but comparable to or slightly above GOOGL and AMZN by 2025. This suggests the market sees META as less richly valued, potentially reflecting greater perceived risks, but also offering more upside if performance continues to improve.

In summary, META is outpacing peers in growth and margin expansion, though it remains behind in absolute EBITDA. Its improving profitability and reasonable valuation make it a competitive force among mega-cap tech companies.
