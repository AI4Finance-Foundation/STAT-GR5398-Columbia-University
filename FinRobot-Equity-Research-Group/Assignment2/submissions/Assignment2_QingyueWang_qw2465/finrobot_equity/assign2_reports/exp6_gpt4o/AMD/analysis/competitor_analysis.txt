Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) has shown impressive revenue growth over recent years, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 20.5% from 2021 to 2027. This growth outpaces many of its competitors in the semiconductor market, though it is important to consider the broader context of the industry, which has seen significant volatility and growth opportunities.

When compared to its peers, Intel Corporation (INTC) and NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA), AMD's EBITDA performance highlights both strengths and challenges. AMD's EBITDA has fluctuated, peaking in 2025 at $7.275 billion, which is lower than NVIDIA's expected EBITDA of $86.137 billion in the same year. This suggests that while AMD is growing, NVIDIA has a more robust trajectory, likely due to its stronghold in high-performance computing and AI sectors.

In terms of valuation, AMD's EV/EBITDA ratio indicates a premium valuation compared to Intel but is generally lower than NVIDIA's. For instance, in 2023, AMD's EV/EBITDA was 57.12, much higher than Intel's 22.48 but lower than NVIDIA's 81.05. This implies investors may perceive AMD as a growth company, though not as aggressive as NVIDIA.

AMD's financial metrics reflect a company that is successfully expanding its market share, with a steadily increasing contribution margin, reaching 52.5% by 2027. However, its profit margins are below those of NVIDIA, which may impact its long-term profitability and competitive positioning.

Overall, AMD is positioned as a strong contender in the semiconductor industry, leveraging its growth strategy effectively. However, the company faces stiff competition from NVIDIA, which appears to maintain a stronger growth trajectory and valuation, and Intel, which continues to be a significant player despite its current challenges.
