Intel Corporation (INTC) faces significant challenges compared to its peers, AMD and NVIDIA, as evident from the financial metrics and competitive positioning over recent years.

In terms of revenue, Intel has experienced a substantial decline, with a CAGR of -9.6% from 2021 to 2025. This trend starkly contrasts with the growth trajectories seen in the semiconductor industry, particularly with peers like NVIDIA, which has leveraged its leadership in GPUs and AI solutions to achieve significant financial growth. AMD, on the other hand, has shown resilience and growth through its focus on high-performance computing and data centers.

Intel's EBITDA margin has also seen a dramatic drop from 42.9% in 2021 to a mere 2.3% in 2024, recovering to 27.2% in 2025. This fluctuation indicates operational challenges and perhaps inefficiencies in adapting to market dynamics. In comparison, NVIDIA's EBITDA margins have been robust, underpinned by strong demand in the AI and gaming sectors.

The EV/EBITDA ratio further highlights Intel's competitive disadvantage, particularly in 2024, where its ratio skyrockets to 106.938, suggesting market skepticism about its future cash flow generation compared to AMD and NVIDIA. While AMD's and NVIDIA's ratios also fluctuate, they generally reflect healthier market confidence.

Intel’s EPS has plummeted, indicating pressure on profitability and shareholder value. The negative EPS from 2024 onwards is a critical concern, contrasting sharply with the positive performance of its peers.

In conclusion, Intel is struggling with declining revenues, reduced profitability, and market perception issues. Its peers, particularly NVIDIA, have capitalized on emerging tech trends, leaving Intel with significant strategic and operational challenges to address in the short to medium term.
