NVIDIA Corporation (NASDAQ: NVDA) is a Santa Clara-based semiconductor and technology company that has evolved from its origins as a graphics processing unit (GPU) designer into one of the most strategically critical technology companies in the world. Founded in 1993, NVIDIA has built a dominant position at the intersection of accelerated computing, artificial intelligence, and data center infrastructure.

NVIDIA's business model centers on designing high-performance chips and systems, with manufacturing outsourced to foundry partners such as TSMC. The company operates primarily through two segments: Data Center, which has become the overwhelming revenue driver, and Gaming, which historically anchored the business. Additional segments include Professional Visualization, Automotive, and OEM. The Data Center segment, powered by flagship products such as the H100 and Blackwell-architecture GPUs, has become indispensable to hyperscalers, cloud providers, and enterprise customers building AI training and inference infrastructure. NVIDIA's CUDA software platform creates a deeply entrenched ecosystem that reinforces hardware adoption and raises switching costs considerably.

The company's market position is effectively without peer in AI accelerator hardware. While AMD competes with its Instinct GPU line and Intel pursues the market through its Gaudi accelerators, neither has meaningfully challenged NVIDIA's dominance. This is reflected in the EBITDA comparison data, where NVIDIA's 2025 EBITDA of approximately 86 billion dollars dwarfs AMD's 7.3 billion and Intel's 14.4 billion for the same period.

NVIDIA's recent financial performance has been extraordinary by virtually any measure. Revenue surged from approximately 27 billion dollars in fiscal 2023 to 216 billion dollars in fiscal 2026, representing a four-year compound annual growth rate of 68.3 percent. EBITDA margins have expanded sharply from 22.2 percent in 2023 to nearly 67 percent in fiscal 2026, reflecting powerful operating leverage as data center demand accelerated. EPS grew from 0.18 dollars in 2023 to 4.93 dollars in fiscal 2026.

Forward estimates project continued, though moderating, growth with revenues approaching 250 billion dollars by fiscal 2027 and EBITDA margins potentially exceeding 73 percent. The company's EV/EBITDA multiple has compressed from over 81 times in 2023 to approximately 31 times in 2026, suggesting the market is beginning to price NVIDIA more in line with its earnings power. Despite geopolitical export restrictions and intensifying competition, NVIDIA remains the defining infrastructure provider of the artificial intelligence era.
