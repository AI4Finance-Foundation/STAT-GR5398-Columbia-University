NVIDIA Competitor Analysis

NVIDIA has established itself as the dominant force in the semiconductor industry, particularly in AI and data center computing, significantly outpacing peers AMD and Intel across virtually every key financial metric.

Revenue growth tells the most compelling story. NVIDIA achieved a staggering 125.9% revenue increase in fiscal 2024 followed by 114.2% in 2025, reaching $130.5 billion, while AMD and Intel have struggled to generate comparable momentum. Intel, once the industry leader, saw its EBITDA collapse to just $1.2 billion in 2024 before partially recovering, reflecting deep structural challenges in its manufacturing and competitive positioning. AMD has shown more resilience, growing EBITDA from $4.1 billion in 2023 to $7.3 billion in 2025, but remains a fraction of NVIDIA's scale.

NVIDIA's EBITDA margin expansion is particularly striking, climbing from 22.2% in 2023 to 66.9% in 2026, demonstrating exceptional operating leverage as AI infrastructure spending accelerates. Neither AMD nor Intel approaches this profitability profile.

On valuation, NVIDIA's EV/EBITDA multiple has compressed meaningfully from 81x in 2023 to approximately 33.8x in 2025, suggesting the market is increasingly pricing in sustained earnings power rather than pure speculation. AMD trades near 47.8x, reflecting growth optimism but at a premium relative to its actual earnings scale.

Looking ahead, NVIDIA's estimated EBITDA of $144.6 billion in 2026 dwarfs projected figures for both peers, reinforcing its structural advantage in AI accelerators and positioning it as the clear industry leader with minimal near-term competitive threat to its core business.
