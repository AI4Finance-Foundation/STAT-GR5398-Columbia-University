NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) has established itself as the clear performance leader among its semiconductor peers, particularly Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Intel (INTC), as reflected in both its financial trajectory and profitability metrics.

NVIDIA’s revenue growth has been explosive, with a CAGR of 68.3% from 2022 to 2027E, far outpacing historical growth rates seen at AMD or Intel. The company’s top line is forecast to expand from $27B in 2022 to nearly $250B by 2027E, underscoring the surging demand for its GPUs and AI accelerators. By contrast, AMD and Intel’s revenue and EBITDA growth have been comparatively modest, with neither rival approaching NVIDIA’s scale or growth pace.

Profitability is another area where NVIDIA distinctly outshines its competitors. NVIDIA’s EBITDA margin is projected to rise from 22.2% in 2023 to over 73% in 2027E, a level well above AMD and Intel. EBITDA for NVIDIA is set to soar from $6B in 2023 to $382B in 2027E, while AMD and Intel are expected to reach just $8.6B and $47.4B, respectively, in the same year. This margin expansion is driven by operating leverage and NVIDIA’s premium product mix, as evidenced by declining SG&A as a percentage of revenue.

Valuation-wise, NVIDIA trades at a premium, with an EV/EBITDA multiple peaking at 81x in 2023, though expected to normalize toward 31x by 2026. This is still above Intel but in line with or below AMD’s volatile multiples, reflecting market confidence in NVIDIA’s sustained growth and earnings power.

In summary, NVIDIA’s superior growth, industry-leading margins, and robust earnings expansion firmly position it ahead of AMD and Intel, justifying its valuation premium and leadership in the AI-driven semiconductor space.
