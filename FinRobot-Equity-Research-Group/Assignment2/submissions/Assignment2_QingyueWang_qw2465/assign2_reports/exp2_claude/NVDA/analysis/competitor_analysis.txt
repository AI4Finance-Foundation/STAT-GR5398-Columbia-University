NVIDIA Competitor Analysis

NVIDIA has emerged as the dominant force in the semiconductor industry, outpacing peers AMD and Intel by a substantial margin across virtually every key financial metric. The company's revenue growth trajectory is exceptional, with a five-year CAGR of 68.3%, driven primarily by surging demand for AI accelerators and data center infrastructure.

In terms of EBITDA, NVIDIA's performance dwarfs its competitors. While AMD generated approximately $7.3 billion in EBITDA in 2025 and Intel rebounded to $14.4 billion after a sharp decline in 2024, NVIDIA posted $86.1 billion in the same period, representing roughly a 12x and 6x advantage over AMD and Intel, respectively. NVIDIA's EBITDA margin of 66.9% in fiscal 2026 far exceeds what either peer has historically achieved, reflecting its superior pricing power and operating leverage.

From a valuation standpoint, NVIDIA's EV/EBITDA multiple has compressed meaningfully from 81x in 2023 to approximately 33.8x in 2025, suggesting the market is increasingly pricing the company on earnings power rather than pure growth expectations. AMD trades at a comparable multiple near 47.8x despite significantly weaker profitability, implying a relative valuation premium that may be difficult to justify. Intel's multiple volatility reflects its ongoing restructuring challenges.

Looking ahead, NVIDIA's forward estimates project continued margin expansion and EPS growth toward $5.88 by 2027, while its PE ratio gradually declines toward 32x. The competitive gap with AMD and Intel appears likely to widen further, as NVIDIA continues to benefit from its entrenched position in AI computing infrastructure.
