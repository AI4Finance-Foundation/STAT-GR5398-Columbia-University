Company Overview

NVIDIA Corporation (NASDAQ: NVDA) is a Santa Clara-based semiconductor and technology company that has evolved from its origins as a graphics processing unit (GPU) designer into one of the most strategically critical technology companies in the world. Founded in 1993, NVIDIA has built a dominant position at the intersection of accelerated computing, artificial intelligence, and data center infrastructure.

NVIDIA's business model centers on designing high-performance chips and systems, with manufacturing outsourced to foundry partners such as TSMC. The company operates primarily through two segments: Data Center, which has become the overwhelming revenue driver, and Gaming, which historically anchored the business. Additional segments include Professional Visualization, Automotive, and OEM. The Data Center segment, powered by flagship products such as the H100 and Blackwell-architecture GPUs, has become indispensable to hyperscalers, cloud providers, and enterprise customers building AI training and inference infrastructure. NVIDIA's CUDA software platform creates a deeply entrenched ecosystem that reinforces hardware adoption and raises switching costs considerably.

The company's market position is effectively without peer in AI accelerator hardware. While AMD competes with its Instinct GPU line and Intel pursues the market through its Gaudi accelerators, neither has meaningfully challenged NVIDIA's dominance. This is reflected in the EBITDA comparison data, where NVIDIA's 2025 EBITDA of approximately 86 billion dollars dwarfs AMD's 7.3 billion and Intel's 14.4 billion for the same period.

NVIDIA's recent financial performance has been extraordinary by virtually any measure. Revenue surged from approximately 27 billion dollars in fiscal 2023 to 216 billion dollars in fiscal 2026, representing a four-year compound annual growth rate of 68.3 percent. EBITDA margins have expanded sharply from 22.2 percent in 2023 to nearly 67 percent in fiscal 2026, reflecting powerful operating leverage as data center demand accelerated. EPS grew from 0.18 dollars in 2023 to 4.93 dollars in fiscal 2026.

Forward estimates project continued, though moderating, growth with revenues approaching 250 billion dollars by fiscal 2027 and EBITDA margins potentially exceeding 73 percent. The company's EV/EBITDA multiple has compressed from over 81 times in 2023 to approximately 31 times in 2026, suggesting the market is beginning to price NVIDIA more in line with its earnings power. Despite geopolitical export restrictions and intensifying competition, NVIDIA remains the defining infrastructure provider of the artificial intelligence era.

Investment Update

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) Investment Update

NVIDIA has delivered one of the most remarkable financial transformations in semiconductor history, with revenue surging from $26.9 billion in fiscal 2022 to $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026, representing a five-year CAGR of 68.3%. This explosive growth has been driven almost entirely by insatiable demand for AI accelerators, with data center customers racing to build out large-scale GPU infrastructure. The company's ability to translate top-line momentum into profitability has been equally impressive, with EBITDA margins expanding from 22.2% in fiscal 2023 to 66.9% in fiscal 2026, reflecting extraordinary operating leverage as fixed costs are spread across a rapidly growing revenue base.

The primary growth driver remains AI infrastructure buildout across hyperscalers, enterprise customers, and sovereign AI programs globally. NVIDIA's CUDA software ecosystem continues to serve as a powerful competitive moat, creating high switching costs that reinforce hardware dominance. EPS has grown from $0.18 in fiscal 2023 to $4.93 in fiscal 2026, with estimates projecting further expansion to $5.88 by fiscal 2027.

Relative to peers, NVIDIA's EBITDA trajectory is in a different category entirely. AMD and Intel combined cannot approach NVIDIA's absolute earnings scale, and NVIDIA's EV/EBITDA multiple has compressed meaningfully from 81x in fiscal 2023 to approximately 31x in fiscal 2026, suggesting the market is beginning to price in a more mature growth profile.

The key risks to monitor include revenue growth deceleration, which estimates project slowing to 4-6% annually through fiscal 2027, potential export restrictions, and emerging competition from custom silicon developed by hyperscaler customers. At current valuations, execution must remain flawless.

Valuation

NVIDIA Valuation Analysis

NVIDIA's current valuation reflects its extraordinary growth trajectory and dominant positioning in AI accelerator hardware. Trading at a forward P/E of approximately 35.9x for fiscal 2025 estimates, declining to 32.4x by 2027, the multiple appears elevated in absolute terms but is increasingly supported by exceptional earnings power. The stock's historical P/E of roughly 109x in 2023 has compressed significantly as earnings have surged, with EPS growing from $0.18 in 2023 to $4.93 in 2025 actual results and projected to reach $5.88 by 2027.

On an EV/EBITDA basis, NVIDIA's multiple has similarly contracted from a peak of 81x in 2023 to approximately 31.4x for 2026 estimates. This compression is driven by explosive EBITDA growth, with margins expanding from 22.2% in 2023 to a projected 73.5% by 2027, a level unmatched among semiconductor peers. Compared to AMD, which trades near 47.8x EV/EBITDA on 2025 figures with substantially lower absolute EBITDA, and Intel, which has struggled to generate consistent profitability, NVIDIA commands a justified premium reflecting superior execution and structural AI demand tailwinds.

Revenue growth is moderating from triple-digit rates toward a projected 4-6% range by 2026-2027, which introduces some risk to premium multiple sustainability. However, the combination of expanding EBITDA margins approaching 73%, declining SG&A intensity now below 2%, and a five-year revenue CAGR of 68.3% supports a fair value assessment in the range of $165 to $185 per share on a blended forward earnings basis. Downside risks include AI capital expenditure normalization and intensifying competition from AMD and custom silicon initiatives.

Risks

# NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) - Key Investment Risks

  • ▪ Revenue Growth Deceleration Risk: Revenue growth is projected to collapse from 65.5% (2026A) to just 4-6% (2025E-2027E), signaling potential market saturation in AI/data center demand; any shortfall from even these modest estimates could trigger significant multiple compression given NVDA's still-elevated EV/EBITDA of ~31-34x versus AMD's historical range of 18-57x
  • ▪ Margin Sustainability Concern: Despite projected EBITDA margin expansion to 73.5% by 2027E, Cost of Operations has grown disproportionately from $9.4B (2022A) to $62.5B (2026A) — a 562% increase — suggesting rising supply chain and manufacturing costs that could compress margins if pricing power weakens amid increased competition
  • ▪ Valuation Premium Vulnerability: NVDA trades at EV/EBITDA of ~31-34x (2026E-2027E) versus Intel's 14.5x and AMD's historical ~38-57x range; with EPS growth slowing from 66% (2025A) to ~6% (2027E), the current P/E of ~32-36x leaves limited margin of safety if AI capital expenditure cycles turn
  • ▪ Customer Concentration & Demand Cyclicality Risk: The explosive revenue surge from $27B (2023A) to $216B (2026A) is heavily dependent on hyperscaler AI infrastructure spending; any pullback in cloud capex budgets or delays in AI monetization by Microsoft, Google, or Meta could cause severe demand shocks
  • ▪ Competitive & Geopolitical Displacement Risk: AMD's projected EBITDA growth to $8.6B (2027E) and Intel's recovery to $47.4B signal intensifying competition; simultaneously, U.S. export restrictions on advanced chips to China — historically a significant revenue contributor — represent a structural headwind that could impair the 2027E revenue trajectory of ~$250B
