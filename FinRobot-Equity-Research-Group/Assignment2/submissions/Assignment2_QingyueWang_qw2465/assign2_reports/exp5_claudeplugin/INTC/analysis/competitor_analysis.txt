Intel Competitor Analysis

Intel Corporation operates in one of the most competitive segments of the semiconductor industry, facing significant pressure from both AMD and NVIDIA across its core markets. The data paints a challenging picture for Intel relative to its peers.

From a revenue standpoint, Intel has experienced a sustained decline, posting a four-year CAGR of negative 9.6% from 2021 to 2025, falling from approximately 79 billion dollars to just under 53 billion dollars. AMD and NVIDIA, by contrast, have grown meaningfully over the same period, with NVIDIA in particular experiencing explosive revenue and EBITDA expansion driven by surging demand for AI accelerators.

Intel's EBITDA deterioration is the most striking comparative weakness. Its EBITDA collapsed to just 1.2 billion dollars in 2024, a fraction of AMD's 5.3 billion and a stark contrast to NVIDIA's 35.6 billion in the same year. While Intel has partially recovered to 14.4 billion in 2025, NVIDIA is projected to reach 86 billion in 2025 and an extraordinary 382 billion by 2027, reflecting an entirely different growth trajectory.

Valuation multiples further highlight the disparity. Intel's EV/EBITDA spiked to nearly 107 times in 2024 due to its near-zero EBITDA, while NVIDIA and AMD traded at more normalized multiples reflective of stronger earnings quality. Intel's negative EPS through the forecast period underscores ongoing profitability challenges.

In summary, Intel faces structural headwinds in both traditional CPU markets and the AI-driven compute segment, where NVIDIA dominates and AMD is gaining ground. Without a meaningful product or manufacturing inflection, Intel risks further competitive erosion.
